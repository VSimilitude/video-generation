# `b-wall` — along the sea wall

**Node:** `b-wall` · **Path:** `[wall]` · **Scenes:** `b-wall.1`, `b-wall.2`, `b-wall.3`

**When / where:** twenty to nine to ten o'clock · the jetty gate, brick seventeen
→ the Boat Shed's front door, brick one · tide step ten down to step seven.

Crumb takes the long bright road west. He passes an empty rock and his own front
door, and at half past nine, at brick seven, he walks backwards for five minutes
beside Nan Prattle, who gives him three things without being asked.

**Contracted:** ends at the Boat Shed door, brick one, ten o'clock, step seven,
Crumb alone, door still shut. Ledger `L-B1`, `L-B2`, `L-B3`. Nan is at brick
seven at half past nine because the timetable says so (bible W-4), and nothing
Crumb does moves her (P-4).

### `b-wall.1` · An empty rock and his own front door

**When/where:** twenty to nine to nine o'clock · tide at step ten (step nine as
the hour is sung) · the sea wall, brick seventeen west to brick thirteen.

*Staging.* The top of the wall: seventeen bricks running away west, low and
warm, the tide pools shining below from brick sixteen down. Crumb sets off
sideways along the top, which is the fastest a hermit crab goes. Behind him the
jetty gate, the grating, and a lobster asleep in it. Nothing on the wall but
Crumb. Far, far away at the west end, a speck of a snail, moving so slowly you
would have to watch a long time to be sure.

> Narrator: Twenty to nine. The water was at step ten.
>
> Narrator: Crumb went west along the top of the wall.
>
> Narrator: Behind him, the grating. Inside the grating, the lobster, asleep.
>
> Narrator: Sixteen bricks to go. Eighty minutes. Dark and short, bright and
> long, both the same.
>
> Crumb: Eighty minutes is *ages*.
>
> Narrator: It was not ages. But he did not know that yet.
>
> [beat]

*Ten to nine. Brick fifteen.* Grumble's Rock, flat and barnacled, down in the
pools. Crumb slows. He has a hello ready. He has been carrying it since brick
seventeen.

> Narrator: At ten to nine he came to brick fifteen.
>
> Narrator: Grumble's Rock was down below, flat and barnacled and shining.
>
> Narrator: There was nobody on it.
>
> [beat]
>
> Crumb: Morning, rock.
>
> [beat]
>
> Narrator: The rock said nothing. Rocks are like that.
>
> Crumb: *(getting on)* He's probably busy.

*Five to nine. Brick fourteen.* The Shell Shelf, up under the cliff: a row of
empty shells, and the big spiral one at the end of the row. Crumb's own front
door, and he goes straight past it, sideways, at speed.

> Narrator: At five to nine he came to brick fourteen. That is where Crumb lives.
>
> Narrator: A little chalk shelf, and a row of empty shells on it.
>
> Narrator: And at the end of the row, the big spiral one.
>
> [beat]
>
> Crumb: Not yet.
>
> [beat]
>
> Crumb: *(to the spiral shell, politely)* Sorry. Can't stop. Bell.
>
> Narrator: And he went past his own front door without going in, which is a
> hard thing to do when you are six.

*Nine o'clock. Brick thirteen.* Up on the cliff, the kittiwakes sing the hour.
Four notes. Badly.

> Narrator: Then the kittiwakes sang nine o'clock.
>
> Narrator: There are four notes in the hour. The kittiwakes get about two of
> them.
>
> [beat]
>
> Crumb: Nine o'clock. Brick thirteen.
>
> Narrator: The water was at step nine now, and coming up the wall a little at a
> time, the way it does.
>
> Narrator: And a long way off, at the other end of the wall, something was
> coming the other way.
>
> Narrator: Crumb could not see what it was yet.
>
> [beat]
>
> Narrator: He could hear it.

### `b-wall.2` · Nan Prattle, brick seven, half past nine

**When/where:** nine o'clock to twenty-five to ten · tide step nine, then step
eight from half past nine · the sea wall, brick thirteen west to brick seven.

*Staging.* Half an hour of wall. Crumb going west, brisk and sideways. Nan
Prattle coming east, a periwinkle the size of a thumb, creeping one brick every
quarter of an hour, with her kelp washing-line wound twice round her shell — and
talking the whole way, to nobody, because there is nobody there yet. They close
on each other brick by brick. At half past nine they meet at brick seven. Crumb
turns himself round and walks backwards, because that is the only way to have a
conversation with Nan Prattle.

> Narrator: Nan Prattle walks the wall every morning of her life. West end to
> east end. One brick every quarter of an hour.
>
> Narrator: She is going to meet her grandsons' boat. She has never once been
> late and she has never once hurried.
>
> Narrator: And she talks the whole way.
>
> [beat]
>
> Narrator: They met at brick seven at half past nine, with the water at step
> eight.
>
> Nan: Crumb! Crumb, Crumb, Crumb. Out early.
>
> Crumb: Morning, Nan. I'm going to the—
>
> Nan: Don't stop, my love. I can't stop. Walk with me.
>
> Narrator: So Crumb turned round and walked backwards.
>
> [beat]
>
> Nan: That's it. Everybody does it.

*The stream.* Nan talks. Crumb walks backwards. He is going the wrong way now,
slowly, and he knows it, and he cannot get a word in edgeways or any other way.

> Nan: My boys are outside the bar. Waiting for the bell, like always.
>
> Nan: The little one has a cold. He says he hasn't got a cold.
>
> Nan: He has got a cold.
>
> [beat]
>
> Crumb: Nan, the—
>
> Nan: And Grumble's still asleep, the old thing. Still in that grating.
>
> Crumb: *(quickly)* His rock was empty!
>
> Nan: Course it was, my love. He wakes when the sun comes over the headland.
> Twenty to ten. Every day of his life.
>
> Nan: He'll be up on his rock in a quarter of an hour. Like always.
>
> [beat]
>
> Nan: *(fondly)* Nine complaints. You could set the tide by him.
>
> [beat]

*The one thing Nan does not know.* Crumb gets his news out at last. It takes
him three goes.

> Crumb: Nan.
>
> Nan: — and the haddock yesterday, well, I say haddock —
>
> Crumb: *Nan.*
>
> Nan: Yes, my love?
>
> Crumb: The bell rope broke. This morning. In my claws.
>
> [beat]
>
> Narrator: Nan Prattle kept creeping.
>
> Narrator: Nan Prattle stopped talking.
>
> [beat]
>
> [beat]
>
> Nan: Broke.
>
> Crumb: It came apart. Both ends. It was old.
>
> Nan: *(starting up again, but different)* Then you want the Boat Shed, brick
> one, and the spare line on the peg. Lift the door, don't pull it. Now the
> haddock —
>
> Crumb: Sorry — *lifted?*
>
> Nan: Lifted, my love. Everyone pulls. Everyone falls over.
>
> [beat]
>
> Crumb: *(saying it to himself)* Lift. Not pull. Lift, not pull.

*The first thing given.* `L-B1` — he knows about the door before he gets there.

*The second thing given.* `L-B2` — Nan starts unwinding the kelp washing-line
off her shell without being asked, which takes her a minute or two and does not
slow her down by so much as an inch.

> Nan: And take my line.
>
> Crumb: Your washing line?
>
> Nan: Take it.
>
> Narrator: It was a claw's-length of dry kelp cord. That is all it was.
>
> Crumb: Nan — it's not long enough. Not for the bell. It's nowhere near.
>
> Nan: It isn't.
>
> [beat]
>
> Nan: Take it anyway. Everybody takes something.
>
> [beat]
>
> Nan: And bring it back.
>
> Crumb: I'll bring it back. I promise.
>
> Narrator: And he put it in his shell, where he keeps things, next to nothing
> else at all.

*The third thing given.* `L-B3` — the debt, and the send-off.

> Nan: I'll be at brick seventeen at noon. I'm always at brick seventeen at
> noon.
>
> Nan: You can tell me how it ends.
>
> Crumb: I don't know how it ends.
>
> [beat]
>
> Nan: Then you'd better go and find out, my love.
>
> [beat]
>
> Narrator: Twenty-five to ten.
>
> Narrator: And Crumb got on.

### `b-wall.3` · The last six bricks

**When/where:** twenty-five to ten to ten o'clock · tide step eight, then step
seven at ten · the sea wall, brick seven west to the Boat Shed door, brick one.

*Staging.* Crumb turns round the right way and goes west, quick and sideways.
Behind him, getting smaller and quieter brick by brick, Nan creeps east, still
talking. The tide pools end at brick ten; after that it is just wall, and the
water at the foot of it, higher than it was. Ahead: the slip, and the Boat Shed
on it, tarred black, with its one door.

> Narrator: Walking backwards for five minutes had cost Crumb a bit of brick
> seven.
>
> Narrator: He got it back before brick five.
>
> [beat]
>
> Narrator: He had trotted the whole way. Now he trotted a little quicker, and
> that was all.
>
> Narrator: Eighty minutes is eighty minutes. Whichever road. Whatever you do on
> the way.
>
> [beat]
>
> Narrator: Behind him, Nan was still talking. He could hear her at brick six.
>
> Narrator: He could hear her at brick five.
>
> Narrator: At brick four he could only hear a bit of her, and it sounded happy.
>
> [beat]

*Brick three.* The cord comes out. Not for anything. Just because it is there
and his claws want a job.

> Narrator: At brick three Crumb took Nan's cord out and tied a Bosun's Loop in
> it, the way Barnaby showed him.
>
> Narrator: Round, under, through, and pull.
>
> [beat]
>
> Narrator: It came out right first go.
>
> Crumb: *(quietly, very pleased)* Ha.
>
> Narrator: Then he untied it and put it away again, because it was Nan's, and
> he had promised.

*Brick one. Ten o'clock.* The Boat Shed on the slip. The high thin cloud has
gone; it is clear and sunny now, the way it always is after ten in Salt Pocket
Cove. Below the shed, the water is up the slip a little way. On the cliff, the
kittiwakes have a go at the hour.

> Narrator: The kittiwakes sang ten o'clock. Four notes. Two of them right.
>
> Narrator: Brick one. The Boat Shed, tarred black, sat on the slip with its
> door shut.
>
> Narrator: Down at the jetty, a very long way east, the water was at step
> seven.
>
> Crumb: Ten o'clock. Two hours till the bell.
>
> [beat]
>
> Narrator: Crumb looked at the door. The door looked back, the way doors do.
>
> Crumb: Lift. Not pull.
>
> Narrator: And Crumb put his claws under the bottom of the Boat Shed door.
>
> [beat]
