## `a-pipe.1` — The lobster in the doorway

**When/where:** twenty to nine, tide at step ten. The grating at the top of the
jetty steps, brick seventeen. The kittiwakes sang eight, badly, forty minutes
ago.

**Staging.** The grating: a square of old iron bars set into the rock beside the
top step, with black behind it. Below and to the right, the jetty steps go down
to the water, step ten wet, step eleven under. Barnaby is on his post, off to
the side, saying nothing — Crumb has already said goodbye. Crumb stands at the
bars, sideways on, looking in at nothing. Just inside the bars, in the dark,
there is a long barnacled shape that anybody would take for a rock.

Narrator: Twenty to nine. The water was at step ten and coming up.

Narrator: Two roads to the Boat Shed. Crumb had picked the dark one.

Narrator: The bars of the grating were wide enough for a hermit crab. Nearly.

*(He squeezes. His shell goes through. His back end, which sticks out, goes
through last and unwillingly — P1.)*

Narrator: Inside, it was black. Crumb put a claw out and felt something.

Narrator: It was warm. It was hard. It had barnacles on it.

Narrator: It was not a rock.

Grumble: That was my elbow.

`[beat]`

Crumb: Sorry! Sorry. Sorry, Grumble.

Grumble: Know when I get up?

Crumb: No.

Grumble: Twenty to ten.

`[beat]`

Grumble: It is twenty to nine.

*(Grumble does not shout. Grumble has never shouted at anybody. He simply lies
there being awake at the wrong time, in the dark, with a hermit crab standing
on his elbow — T-3, T-4.)*

Crumb: I have to get to the Boat Shed.

Grumble: Through here.

Crumb: Through here.

Grumble: Right.

`[beat]`

Crumb: Where does the Pipe come out?

Grumble: Don't know.

Crumb: Have you never looked?

Grumble: No.

`[beat]`

Grumble: I sleep at this end.

Narrator: And that was true. Grumble had slept just inside that grating for
years and years, and had never once gone in further, and had never once wanted
to.

*(Grumble hauls himself out past Crumb, through the bars, into the grey
morning. He stops. He looks at the sky the way you look at a bill.)*

Grumble: Well. I am up now.

*(He starts west along the pools, towards his rock at brick fifteen. He does
not look back. He does say one more thing.)*

Grumble: Mind the green.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Then Crumb was on his own, and the dark began.

*(Crumb goes in. Two turns of the Pipe and the grating is a small grey square
behind him. Six minutes later, from somewhere out on the rock at brick fifteen,
a flat voice comes back through the bars.)*

Grumble: Complaint one. Too much cloud.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Grumble does nine complaints about the weather, twelve minutes apart,
in the same order, every single day.

Narrator: He had just started them an hour early.

`[beat]`

## `a-pipe.2` — Which way is up the water

**When/where:** ten to nine until half past nine, inside the Pipe, deep in the
headland. Nothing to see. Outside, the water was at step ten, and then at nine
after the kittiwakes sang. The only clock in here is the singing, and it comes
through the rock.

**Staging.** Black. Genuinely black — not blue-dark, not moonlit: the frame is
sound and touch, a claw tip lit by nothing, the shape of the Pipe guessed at
from echoes. The floor is round and slippy and slopes up towards the west. The
walls are green, though nobody can see that yet. Down the middle of the floor
runs a thread of water no deeper than a claw tip. It is uncomfortable in here.
It is not frightening (T-4).

Narrator: The Pipe was cold. The Pipe was slippy. The Pipe smelled of old rain.

Narrator: Crumb walked with both claws out and touched nothing at all.

Crumb: Hello.

Narrator: *(cameo-voicing the echo, in Crumb's own voice, a moment late)* Hello.

`[beat]`

Crumb: Sorry.

`[beat]`

Narrator: The green got on his feet. Then his knees. Then, when he sat down for
a think, all the way up his back.

Narrator: Then he stood up and did not know which way he was facing.

*(This is the wobble of the segment, and it lasts four lines. He does not cry
and nobody comes — T-5.)*

Crumb: Which way am I facing?

Narrator: He could not see the walls. He could not see his claw. He could have
been pointing at the Boat Shed, or straight back at Grumble.

Narrator: So Crumb sat still, in the dark, and did the only thing he had left.

Narrator: He thought about it.

Crumb: It is a drain.

Narrator: It was a drain.

Crumb: Water goes down to the sea.

Narrator: Water does.

Crumb: So I go up the water.

`[beat]`

Narrator: He put one claw down until he found the little thread of water on the
floor, and he felt which way it was running.

Narrator: Then he turned until it ran *away* behind him, and he walked with one
claw in it, all the way, like holding a hand.

Narrator: And high above him, through a whole headland of rock, four notes came
down, very faint, and very bad.

Narrator: Nine o'clock. The kittiwakes.

Crumb: Twenty minutes gone.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Sixty to go. Eighty minutes, either road. Dark and short, bright and
long, both the same.

## `a-pipe.3` — What is behind the Boat Shed

**When/where:** half past nine until ten o'clock. Tide at step eight, and at
step seven when the hour is sung. Inside the Pipe, the far end — then out into
the daylight at the back of the Boat Shed, brick one, and round to the front
door on the slip.

**Staging.** The black slowly stops being total: no light yet, but the echo goes
short and flat, the way sound does when a room is about to end. Then a smell,
then a grey seam along the floor, then a stripe. Out of the stripe: the back of
the Boat Shed, tarred boards, the sky right overhead — and the high thin cloud
sliding off it as he comes out (weather, on every path, at ten). The ground back
here is **higher than the slip**: the shed is built into the slope, its front on
the slip and its back against the headland rock, so the Pipe's west mouth sits
well above its east mouth at the jetty — which is why the trickle runs east to
the sea and why Crumb has been walking uphill all morning. Crumb is green from
his back to his claw tips.

Narrator: After a long time the air changed.

Crumb: That is tar.

`[beat]`

Crumb: The shed is made of tar and wood.

Narrator: And then, low down, a line of grey. And then a stripe. And then far
too much sky all at once.

Narrator: Crumb came out backwards, under a gap at the bottom of a wall, into
the morning.

*(He stands up. He turns round. He looks at what he has just come out of.)*

Narrator: The west end of the Pipe came out at the back of the Boat Shed. Right
under the back wall. Somebody, a very long time ago, had built the shed on top
of it.

Narrator: And nobody in Salt Pocket Cove ever goes round the back of the Boat
Shed, because everything in the Boat Shed is at the front.

`[beat]`

Crumb: I know where the Pipe goes.

`[beat]`

Crumb: And nobody else does.

Narrator: Old Barnaby has watched that grating for ninety years, wondering. He
cannot move, and he cannot go and look, and nobody has ever been able to tell
him.

Narrator: Crumb thought about that for a moment. Then he put it in his pocket
for later, like a good stone.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Then he looked down at himself.

Narrator: Crumb was green. Green feet, green knees, green shell, green all the
way to the tips of his claws.

*(He rubs one claw on the slip stone. He rubs harder. He rubs with the other
claw as well.)*

Crumb: It will not come off.

`[beat]`

Narrator: It was not going to come off today.

Narrator: Above him, on the cliff, four notes. Ten o'clock, and the same two
wrong as always.

Narrator: The cloud slid off the sky and it went blue and bright and warm.

*(He walks round the corner of the shed to the front, onto the slip. The sea is
below the doorstep, climbing. Down at the far end of the wall, sixteen bricks
away, the jetty is a small dark line.)*

Narrator: Ten o'clock. The water at step seven.

Narrator: Eighty minutes, exactly, the way it always is.

Narrator: And there, in front of a small green hermit crab, was the door of the
Boat Shed.

`[beat]`

### Writer's notes (not part of the state doc)

- **P4 stays open on purpose.** Crumb now knows where the Pipe comes out, but he
  has told nobody. The promise is paid at `ending` (E3), when he tells Barnaby —
  `a-pipe.3` only sets it aside "for later, like a good stone".
- **Crumb does not learn about the door.** He arrives at the Boat Shed with no
  idea it lifts instead of pulls, so `mid`'s M2 baseline (pull, pull harder, fall
  over, then think) runs on this path, which is the trunk's choice-1 callback.
- **Grumble's first complaint is heard, not seen.** It is spoken from off stage
  at 8:46 through the grating, so he still appears on screen in scene one only,
  and the hour-early cascade is shown starting without any of it being paid here.
- **Nothing in the segment values one road over the other** (T-2). The saying is
  quoted flat, twice, and Crumb's pride at the end is about having done a hard
  thing on his own, not about having chosen the right road.
