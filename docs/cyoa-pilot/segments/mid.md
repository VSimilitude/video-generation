## `mid.1` · The door that will not pull

**When/where:** ten o'clock, going on for ten past · tide at step seven · the
slip at brick one, outside the Boat Shed's front door. The kittiwakes sang ten a
minute ago and got two of the four notes.

*Staging.* The west end of everything. The slip: a stone ramp going down into
the water, wet at the bottom, dry at the top. The Boat Shed sits on it, tarred
black, small, shut. One door. Below the doorstep the sea is coming up the slip a
stone at a time, in no hurry at all. The cloud has gone off the sky and it is
blue and warm. Sixteen bricks away east, the jetty is a thin dark line, and
you cannot see the bell from here, but Crumb keeps checking anyway.

Narrator: Ten o'clock. Brick one. The far west end of everything.

Narrator: The Boat Shed sat on the slip with its door shut.

Narrator: Below it, the water was coming up the stones. A stone at a time. In no
hurry.

## `mid.2` · Forty Crumbs

**When/where:** about ten past ten until twenty past · tide still at step seven ·
inside the Boat Shed, brick one, with a stripe of daylight coming in under a
propped door.

*Staging.* One room, dark at the back. Crab pots stacked up like a staircase
nobody built on purpose. Dust with light in it. High up on the wall, on a
wooden peg, hangs a coil of mooring line: honey-coloured, thick as Crumb's whole
leg, wound round and round on itself, big as a cartwheel. It is the only rope in
Salt Pocket Cove. Everything in the room is much too big and none of it is going
to help.

Narrator: The Boat Shed was one room, and the back of it was dark.

Narrator: Crab pots. Dust. A bucket with a hole in.

Narrator: And up on the wall, on a peg, the spare mooring line.

`[beat]`

Crumb: *(very small)* There you are.

Narrator: It was honey-coloured and thick as his leg and it was wound round and
round itself like a snail the size of a cartwheel.

Narrator: It was the only rope in Salt Pocket Cove and nobody had ever cut it.

`[beat]`

*(Crumb goes up the stacked crab pots one at a time, which takes a while and
involves a wobble. At the top he is level with the peg. He gets a claw on the
coil and heaves it sideways off the wood.)*

Narrator: Crumb went up the crab pots like stairs, and got a claw round it, and
heaved.

`[beat]`

Narrator: The rope came off the peg.

Narrator: Then the rope came off the peg *properly.*

*(It goes down all at once, with Crumb underneath it. A slow flat thump. Dust
goes up. For a moment there is no Crumb at all, only rope.)*

`[beat]`

`[beat]`

Narrator: There was a small noise from underneath.

Crumb: Sorry.

`[beat]`

Narrator: He had apologised to the rope. The rope did not say anything. Ropes
are like that.

`[beat]`

*(He digs himself out sideways, one claw at a time, and stands on top of the
coil and looks along it.)*

Narrator: Now. Crumb had held the old bell rope that morning. Both ends of it,
in his own claws.

Narrator: So he knew exactly how much rope a bell wants.

Narrator: A bell wants ten Crumbs.

`[beat]`

Narrator: So he walked it out along the floor to have a look, and he counted.

Crumb: One. Two. Three.

Crumb: Four. Five. Six.

Crumb: Seven. Eight. Nine.

Crumb: *(arriving somewhere, pleased)* Ten.

`[beat]`

Crumb: That's the bell.

`[beat]`

Narrator: The rope kept going.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Twenty.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Thirty.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Forty.

`[beat]`

`[beat]`

Crumb: *(from the far end, quietly)* That's four bells.

Narrator: It was four bells. A mooring line is cut to the length a mooring line
is cut to, and it has been that length since before Crumb was an egg.

`[beat]`

*(He goes back to the coil and gets his claws under one side of it and lifts.
The coil comes up about the width of a claw, and then Crumb comes up instead.)*

Narrator: Then he tried to pick it up.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Crumb weighs about as much as a small stone.

Narrator: The rope weighed a great deal more than that.

`[beat]`

Crumb: *(flat, out of breath, lying on it)* Right.

Crumb: Four times too long. And too heavy.

Crumb: And the jetty's *that* way.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Twenty past ten.

## `mid.3` · Out onto the slip

**When/where:** twenty past ten to twenty-five past · tide at step seven, and
step six in five minutes · out through the door onto the slip, brick one.

*Staging.* The doorway from inside: a bright rectangle with the sea in the
bottom of it, nearer than it was. The propped door above, and the old oyster
shell still holding it up, right there on the doorstep, in shot for the whole
scene. Crumb behind the coil, shoving. The coil goes over the step and out into
the sun, and lies on the warm stone looking enormous. Below it, the water
climbing the slip. Behind him, the shell. Two things in the frame, one on each
side of a small hermit crab.

Narrator: There was no getting round it. The rope had to come out of the shed.

Narrator: So Crumb got behind it and shoved.

Narrator: He shoved, and it went the width of a claw.

Narrator: He shoved again, and it went a bit less than that.

Crumb: Sorry.

Narrator: *(deadpan)* He was apologising to it about every fourth shove.

Crumb: Sorry. Sorry. *Sorry.*

`[beat]`

> **[M3 · insert, L-B2 (wall paths)]**
>
> *(Nan's kelp cord comes out of the shell-pocket, goes twice round the coil and
> is tied off in the loop Barnaby showed him — a handle, on a rope that had none.
> Crumb gets it over his shoulder and leans, and the coil comes when he leans
> instead of when he shoves.)*
>
> Narrator: Then he stopped shoving, and took Nan's cord out of his shell, and
> tied it round the coil in a loop.
>
> `[beat]`
>
> Narrator: Now there was something his size to pull on.
>
> Narrator: And a rope you can pull on comes a great deal better than a rope you
> can only push.
>
> `[beat]`
>
> Crumb: *(leaning, out of breath, pleased)* Thank you, Nan.
>
> `[beat]`
>
> *(When the coil is up against the step he unties the cord, shakes it out, and
> puts it back in his shell-pocket, because it is Nan's and he promised.)*
>
> Narrator: And when the coil got to the step he untied the cord and put it away
> again, because it was Nan's, and he had promised.

Narrator: It took him five minutes to get it across a floor you could cross in
four steps.

Narrator: Then it went over the doorstep, all at once, the way things do at the
end, and out onto the slip into the sun.

`[beat]`

*(Crumb comes out after it, gets his back against it, and stops. He is puffed,
there is shed dust all over him, and his back end is sticking further out of his
shell than it was this morning.)*

Narrator: Twenty-five past ten.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Now. Crumb was six, and he could not do sums.

Narrator: But he could do this one.

`[beat]`

Narrator: From the peg to the doorstep had taken a quarter of an hour.

Narrator: From the doorstep to the jetty is sixteen bricks.

`[beat]`

Crumb: *(working it out, and not liking it)* I'd get there…

`[beat]`

Crumb: …in time for next year's bell.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Eighty minutes is what the cove is, either road. Leave now, you are at
the jetty at a quarter to twelve.

Narrator: The bell is at noon. That is all the spare there is.

`[beat]`

*(The water. It has come up the slip while he was inside. It is a good way below
the doorstep still, but you can see where it is going, and it is not stopping.)*

Narrator: And the water had come up the slip while he was in there.

Narrator: Not to the doorstep. Not yet. It would be at the doorstep at ten to
eleven, and it would not be a minute either side of that, because it never is.

Narrator: Step seven. Step six in five minutes. High water at noon.

`[beat]`

*(Hold the wide shot: the shell on the doorstep on one side, holding the door
up, its one rough edge in the sun. The water creeping up the slip on the other.
The coil in the middle. Crumb in front of it, very small.)*

Narrator: So. Behind him, an old oyster shell propping a door open, with one
rough edge on it.

Narrator: In front of him, the water, coming up the slip.

`[beat]`

Narrator: The line is too long and too heavy, and the tide will not wait.

Narrator: Crumb could saw off just the piece the bell needs — but nobody in Salt
Pocket Cove has ever cut the mooring line.

Narrator: Or he could wait for the water, and float the whole thing home.

`[beat]`

Narrator: **Cut the piece he needs** — or — **float the whole line.**

`[beat]`

### Writer's notes (not part of the state docs)

- **Insert budget: three used, three allowed.** M1 (one narration line per side),
  M2 (the door), M3 (the cord as a prop, wall only). Everything else in the
  segment is skeleton and runs word-for-word on all four paths.
- **M3 sits *after* the shoving, not before it, and it puts the cord back.** The
  skeleton's shove-and-apologise run is untouched; the insert then gives Crumb a
  handle, shows the coil coming better for a pull than for a push, and unties the
  cord at the doorstep so it is in his shell-pocket when the segment ends. That
  matters twice: `c-cut.1` re-hangs the remainder of the line in the shed, and if
  the cord were still round the coil it would go back on the peg with it and P5
  could never be paid. Note the loop makes the job *easier*, not *faster* — the
  five minutes across the floor is skeleton and does not move (bible P-1, T-2).
- **M2 is the callback and it is deliberately quiet.** The pipe child sees Crumb
  pull, fall over and reason it out from the floor; the wall child hears Nan's
  own words in Crumb's head — *everyone pulls, everyone falls over* — which is a
  description of what the other branch literally shows. Neither is faster and
  neither is praised (T-2). The door opens the same way in both.
- **The oyster shell earns its keep before it is an instrument.** It falls out of
  the frame on the lift, Crumb props the door with it and thanks it, and it is
  still in shot at the end of scene three doing that job. Choice 2 points at a
  thing the child has already watched be useful, not a prop conjured for the
  fork.
- **`shell:` normalised across both docs.** Both parents said the same thing in
  different words (`a-pipe` added the grating squeeze). No ledger entry licenses
  a difference, so the merge states it once, identically.
- **`feeling:` is identical on both docs on purpose.** Nothing in either ledger
  touches it, and after twenty-five minutes of the same rope both crabs feel the
  same way. That is the merge doing its job.
- **Grumble's `everyone` row is the only non-protagonist difference.** On the
  pipe doc he has just finished all nine complaints at twenty-two minutes past
  ten and leaves the rock at half past; on the wall doc he has just done the
  fourth and the fifth is due at twenty-six minutes to eleven. Same list, same
  order, same rock, one hour apart — the hinge, not a personality change.
- **Nan's row is identical on both docs**, at brick ten, on time. On one path
  Crumb walked backwards beside her for five minutes; on the other he has never
  seen her today. She is at the same brick either way. That is the control case.
- **Crumb never decides anything here.** The segment establishes the problem and
  stops; the last thing on screen is a rough shell edge and a rising tide, and
  the child chooses.
