# `ending` — the bell

**Node:** `ending` · **Paths in:** `[pipe, cut]`, `[wall, cut]`, `[pipe, float]`,
`[wall, float]` · **Scenes:** `ending.1`, `ending.2`, `ending.3`

**When / where:** a quarter to twelve to a few minutes past noon · the top of the
jetty steps and the last jetty post, brick seventeen · tide step four down to
step three, which is high water.

**On stage:** Crumb, Old Barnaby, the *Sandhopper* out past the bar, and Nan
Prattle arriving at brick seventeen at noon — the skeleton-critical cast
(`tree.md` §5). Grumble is a ledger-licensed *extra* presence and lives entirely
inside E1; nothing in the skeleton needs him.

This is the **second merged trunk** and the last segment of the story. Everything
below is one shared skeleton that plays on all four paths. The only
path-dependent material is the six inserts budgeted in `tree.md` §6, marked in
place:

**The rope-preparation slot.** E5 and E6 are the two sides of choice two and they
are mutually exclusive. Both of them are about *how the rope gets ready for the
bell*, so the skeleton keeps a single variant slot in `ending.2` — the job stated
before it, the Bosun's Loop and the knot after it. Each side ends with the rope
**through the eye**, because getting it there is the thing the two choices make
different: on `float` paths the eye is the whole obstacle and E6 is about
beating it, and on `cut` paths the end has to be finished first and then goes
through in one line. Neither side re-narrates the knot. Choice two is exhaustive,
so both sides carry ledger ids and there is no unmarked default at that slot
(`tree.md` §5).

**Contracted:** the rope goes on with the Bosun's Loop (P2); the kittiwakes sing
twelve, badly; three pulls and a long one; Crumb comes clean out of his shell on
the long pull (P1); the *Sandhopper* crosses the bar (P3); Barnaby sends him home
to the big spiral shell; Nan reaches brick seventeen at noon on every path.

## `ending.1` · A quarter to twelve

**When/where:** a quarter to twelve, going on for ten to · tide at step four ·
the top of the jetty steps and the last post, brick seventeen.

*Staging.* The jetty at very nearly high water. Twelve steps go down, and there
are only three or four of them left above the sea; the rest is green water moving
slowly against stone. The last post stands up out of it. Low down on the post,
under the water now, Old Barnaby, with his feathery feet out, fanning. Above
him, up in the sun, well clear of everything, the Tide Bell — with nothing
hanging from it. Beside the top of the steps, the grating. Behind, the sea wall
going away west, seventeen bricks of it, and one brick west of him, close enough
to hear, something small and slow and talking. Out past everything, the
*Sandhopper*, still waiting.
