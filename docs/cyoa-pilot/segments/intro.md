## `intro.1` — The shell at the end of the row

**When/where:** Eight o'clock in the morning. Tide at step eleven. The Shell
Shelf, brick fourteen, up under the cliff.

*A chalk ledge with a row of empty shells drying along it, smallest first,
biggest last. Crumb wakes up in the second-to-last one with his back end
sticking out of the door.*

Narrator: Salt Pocket Cove. Eight o'clock in the morning.
Narrator: Flat calm. High thin cloud, not burned off yet.
Narrator: Up on the cliff, the kittiwakes sang the hour.
Narrator: Four notes. Badly.
[beat]
Narrator: That is how you know what time it is here.

Narrator: Down on the Shell Shelf, Crumb woke up stuck.

Crumb: Sorry. Sorry. Sorry, shell.

Narrator: Crumb's shell was one size too small.
Narrator: His back end would not go in.
Narrator: It had not gone in for a while.

Crumb: I have grown. Again.

Narrator: At the end of the row sat the big spiral shell.
Narrator: Empty. Polished. Waiting.
Narrator: Crumb climbed in to see.
Narrator: He fitted the way a pea fits a bucket.
[beat]

Crumb: Barnaby says not yet.
Crumb: Not till I fill it.

Narrator: So he climbed out again. And said sorry.

Crumb: Sorry, big shell.

Narrator: Then Crumb remembered what day it was.
[beat]

Crumb: The bell. It's today!

Narrator: Today Crumb was to ring the Tide Bell.
Narrator: For the very first time in his life.
Narrator: The bell is at the jetty. Brick seventeen.
Narrator: Out past the wall, the water sat on step eleven.
Narrator: It climbs one step every half hour. All morning. On its own.
Narrator: Crumb had until noon.
Narrator: Crumb went up onto the wall and ran.
Narrator: Fourteen. Fifteen. Sixteen. Seventeen.

## `intro.2` — Three pulls and a long one

**When/where:** A quarter past eight. Tide still at step eleven. The jetty,
brick seventeen, at Old Barnaby's post.

*The last jetty post. Old Barnaby is bolted to it at about the height of step
five. Above him hangs the Tide Bell; below the bell hangs the old grey bell
rope. Twelve steps go down to the water.*

Narrator: A quarter past eight. The water still on step eleven.
Narrator: Crumb arrived at the jetty with no breath left in him.
Narrator: Old Barnaby was where Old Barnaby is.
Narrator: He has been bolted to that post for ninety years.
Narrator: He can see the whole cove. He cannot go anywhere in it.

Barnaby: Morning.

Crumb: Morning! I ran!

Barnaby: I saw. You always run.
[beat]
Barnaby: I have never run.
[beat]
Barnaby: Not once.
[beat]

Narrator: Above Barnaby hung the Tide Bell.
Narrator: Below the Tide Bell hung the bell rope.
Narrator: Grey. Furry. Older than the jetty.

Narrator: Far out past the cove sat the bar — a long shallow ridge.
Narrator: And out beyond the bar sat a fishing boat, waiting.

Barnaby: That's the Sandhopper. Nan's grandsons.
Barnaby: She can't cross the bar.
Barnaby: Not till the water's high.

Narrator: High water is at noon. Step three.

Barnaby: Then the bell says come home.
[beat]
Barnaby: No bell, no boat.
[beat]

Crumb: Noon. Step three. Got it.
Crumb: What if I get it wrong?

Barnaby: You won't. Three pulls.
Barnaby: Then one long one.

Crumb: Three pulls and a long one.

Barnaby: That's the whole job.
[beat]
Barnaby: One more thing. A knot.

Crumb: A knot?

Barnaby: The Bosun's Loop.

Narrator: Barnaby could not show him.
Narrator: Barnaby has no hands, and Barnaby does not move.
Narrator: So Barnaby said it instead, the way it was said to him.

Barnaby: Round the post.
Barnaby: Under itself.
Barnaby: Through the loop.
Barnaby: Pull.

Narrator: Crumb tried it on the end of the old bell rope.
Narrator: The first one fell apart.
Narrator: The second one was a tangle with a crab in it.
[beat]
Narrator: The third one held.

Crumb: Round, under, through, and pull!

Barnaby: That's it. Keep it.

Crumb: Keep it? What for?

Barnaby: You'll want it one day.
[beat]

## `intro.3` — The rope that was older than everybody

**When/where:** Half past eight, going on twenty to nine. The water climbs to
step ten. The top of the jetty steps, brick seventeen — the bell post on one
side, the grating on the other, the sea wall running away west behind.

*Crumb ties his loop one more time for luck. Beside the top of the steps, set
into the rock, is the grating: the east mouth of the Pipe. The sea wall goes
west from here, all seventeen bricks of it.*

Narrator: Half past eight. The water climbed to step ten.
Narrator: Crumb tied his knot once more. For luck.
Narrator: He pulled.
[beat]
Narrator: The rope did not snap.
Narrator: The rope went to dust.
[beat]
Narrator: Crumb was standing there holding two ends and a small grey-brown cloud.

Crumb: Sorry. Sorry. Sorry, rope.
Crumb: Barnaby. I broke the bell.

Barnaby: You didn't. Time did.
Barnaby: That rope was older than me.
[beat]
Barnaby: Nearly.
[beat]

Crumb: Is there another rope?

Barnaby: One. Only one.
Barnaby: The spare mooring line.
Barnaby: Boat Shed. Brick one.
Barnaby: On a peg. Inside.

Narrator: The Boat Shed sits at the far west end of the cove.
Narrator: Brick one.
Narrator: Crumb was standing at brick seventeen.
[beat]

Crumb: Has anyone ever cut it?

Barnaby: Never. Nobody. Not ever.
[beat]

Narrator: There are two roads from here to the Boat Shed.
Narrator: One is the sea wall. Long, and bright, along the top of the bricks.
Narrator: One is the Pipe. Short, and dark, straight through the rock.

Barnaby: Dark and short, bright and long.
Barnaby: Both the same.

Crumb: The same?

Barnaby: Eighty minutes. Either road.
[beat]
Barnaby: It's a cove, not a race.
[beat]

Narrator: Beside the top of the steps was the grating.
Narrator: The east mouth of the Pipe. Black as a pocket.

Barnaby: I have three stories.
Barnaby: The third one is that hole.

Crumb: How does it end?

Barnaby: I don't know.
[beat]
Barnaby: Ninety years I've watched it.
Barnaby: Nobody knows where it comes out.
Barnaby: And I can't go and look.
[beat]

Narrator: Barnaby laughed at that. He always does.
[beat]

Narrator: Something inside the grating snored.

Crumb: What was that?

Narrator: Behind the bars, in the dark, there was a claw.
Narrator: Then another claw.
Narrator: Then all of Grumble, fast asleep.

Barnaby: Grumble. He lives in there.
Barnaby: He wakes at twenty to ten.
[beat]

Narrator: The snore came again. Slower. Grumpier.
[beat]

Barnaby: Nan's on the wall. Brick three.
Barnaby: She'll be here at noon.

Narrator: Nan Prattle walks one brick every quarter of an hour.
Narrator: She talks the whole way.
Narrator: She is never late and she is never early.

Narrator: It was twenty to nine.
Narrator: The water was on step ten.
Narrator: Crumb was very small, and in a hurry.

Narrator: He stood at the top of the jetty steps, holding both ends of a rope
that wasn't one any more.
Narrator: On one side of him, the grating, with Grumble asleep in it.
Narrator: On the other side, the sea wall, going west.
[beat]

Narrator: Two roads to the Boat Shed, and both take eighty minutes.
Narrator: Dark and short, bright and long, both the same.
Narrator: So — which one?
[beat]
Narrator: **Through the Pipe** · or · **Along the sea wall**
