## `d-float.1` · Twenty-five minutes of nothing

**When/where:** twenty-five past ten to ten to eleven · tide at step seven, and
step six from half past ten · the slip at brick one, outside the Boat Shed.

*Staging.* The slip: a stone ramp going down into the sea, dry at the top, dark
and weedy at the bottom. The coil on the warm stone. Behind it the Boat Shed,
its door still propped half a crab off the step on the old oyster shell, exactly
where it was. Down the slip, marks cut in the stone the way the jetty has steps,
and the water sitting against one of them: **step seven**, then **step six**
after half past ten. Nothing else moves. Sixteen bricks away east, the jetty
is a thin dark line, and the bell on it is too small to see.

Narrator: Twenty-five past ten. Step seven.

Narrator: Crumb had decided.

`[beat]`

Narrator: He was going to wait for the sea to come and get the rope.

`[beat]`

*(He gets behind the coil and shoves it down the slip, a claw's width at a time,
until it is sitting on the dark weedy stone at the bottom, where the water will
arrive first. It takes him five minutes and it is the last shoving he will do
for a while.)*

Narrator: First he pushed it down to the wet stone, where the water would get to
it soonest.

Narrator: That took five minutes and it was the easy part.

`[beat]`

Crumb: *(patting the coil)* Sorry about this.

`[beat]`

Narrator: He was apologising in advance now. That was new.

`[beat]`

*The tide table.* The mark on the slip. The water against it, not obviously
moving, the way water is not obviously moving right up until it is somewhere
else.

Narrator: Half past ten. Step six.

Narrator: The water had come up one mark on the stone.

Narrator: It did not look like it had come up at all.

`[beat]`

Crumb: *(to the water, politely)* You could go a bit quicker.

`[beat]`

Narrator: The water did not go a bit quicker. The water has never gone a bit
quicker in the whole history of Salt Pocket Cove.

`[beat]`

*(Crumb climbs on top of the coil and sits on it, on dry stone, to see what it
is like. It is like sitting on a rope. He gets off. He walks to the water. He
walks back. He looks at the jetty. He cannot see the bell from here and he knows
he cannot see the bell from here, and he checks anyway.)*

Narrator: Then he sat on the rope to see what it would be like.

Narrator: It was like sitting on a rope.

`[beat]`

Narrator: So he got off, and went and looked at the water, and came back, and
looked at the jetty.

Narrator: You cannot see the bell from brick one. Crumb checked four times.

`[beat]`

Crumb: *(flat)* Waiting is *horrible.*

`[beat]`

Narrator: And it was. Waiting was the hardest work Crumb had done all morning,
and he had not moved a claw.

`[beat]`

*Twenty to eleven.* The water is up the weedy stone now. It is a hand's width
below the doorstep. Behind him the shed door stays propped, and the old oyster
shell stays where it is, holding it up, doing the job it was given.

Narrator: Twenty to eleven.

Narrator: The rope was still on the stone. The shed door was still propped open
behind him, on an old oyster shell, exactly where he had put it.

Narrator: And the water was a hand's width below the doorstep.

`[beat]`

Crumb: Ten minutes.

`[beat]`

Crumb: *(smaller)* Ten minutes is *ages.*

`[beat]`

Narrator: It was not ages. He had been wrong about that once already today.

`[beat]`

*Ten to eleven.* The sea reaches the doorstep. It does it quietly. It gets under
the coil first, the way water does, and the bottom of the coil goes dark, and
then the whole thing lifts about the width of a claw and swings, very slightly,
sideways.

Narrator: At ten to eleven the water came to the doorstep of the Boat Shed.

Narrator: Not a minute either side of it. It never is.

`[beat]`

Narrator: It went under the rope first.

`[beat]`

Narrator: And the rope came up off the stone.

`[beat]`

Crumb: *(very quietly)* Oh, you *float.*

`[beat]`

Narrator: Rope floats. Nobody had told Crumb, because nobody had ever needed to
tell him.

Narrator: And then he got on.

`[beat]`

## `d-float.2` · The bottom goes away

**When/where:** ten to eleven to about twenty past eleven · tide at step six,
then step five from eleven o'clock · out from the slip at brick one, east along
the flooded foot of the sea wall to about brick ten.

*Staging.* Low down, at water level, which is where this scene lives. The coil
sits up like a raft with a hermit crab on top of it. To the left, the sea wall
goes away east, brick after brick, the bricks bigger and closer than Crumb has
ever seen them because he is *under* them now instead of on them. Below: clear
green water and, at first, sand with light wobbling on it, close enough to
touch. Then the sand gets further away. Then it gets a lot further away.

Narrator: Ten to eleven, and Crumb went out onto the water on a rope.

`[beat]`

Narrator: At first it was easy, and cold, and slow.

Narrator: He could get a back leg down and walk himself along the bottom, a
shove at a time, with the rope on top of the water and Crumb on top of the rope.

Narrator: The sea was cold. The bit of Crumb that sticks out of his shell was
the bit that found that out.

`[beat]`

Crumb: *(through his teeth)* Cold. Cold. Cold.

`[beat]`

*Brick two. Brick three.* The wall going by, slowly, from underneath. Above him,
seventeen bricks of it, and one crab's worth of morning left.

Narrator: Brick two. Brick three.

Narrator: The wall went past above him, and it looked enormous, because he was
looking up at it.

`[beat]`

*Eleven o'clock.* Up on the cliff, the kittiwakes have a go at the hour.

Narrator: Then the kittiwakes sang eleven o'clock.

Narrator: Four notes. They got two.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Eleven o'clock. Step five.

`[beat]`

Narrator: And Crumb put a leg down for the bottom.

`[beat]`

Narrator: The bottom was not there.

`[beat]`

`[beat]`

Narrator: So he put it down further.

Narrator: There was no further. There was just *down.*

`[beat]`

*(Everything stops. He has all eight legs and both claws on the rope. Under him
the green goes down and down and turns a colder colour, and a long way below,
much too far below, the sand carries on being sand without him.)*

Crumb: *(small)* Oh.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Crumb was out of his depth.

Narrator: He had never in his life been out of his depth. He had lived on a
shelf and a wall and a beach, and there had always, always been a bottom.

`[beat]`

Narrator: His heart went very fast.

`[beat]`

`[beat]`

Narrator: And then nothing happened.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Nothing went on happening.

`[beat]`

Narrator: He was still up. The rope was still up. The sun was still out.

Narrator: Crabs float. Nobody had ever told him that either.

`[beat]`

*(One claw comes off the rope. Then the other one. He sits up. He looks over the
side, on purpose, which is a completely different thing from looking over the
side by accident.)*

Crumb: *(letting go, carefully)* …oh.

`[beat]`

Narrator: From out here he could see the *whole* cove.

Narrator: Brick one behind him, all the way to brick seventeen in front. The
cliff. The white bit where the kittiwakes sit. The tide pools with the water
going over them.

Narrator: And out past everything, small and patient, the *Sandhopper*, waiting
for a bell.

`[beat]`

Narrator: And under him, a long way down, the sand, with the light moving on it.

`[beat]`

Crumb: *(delighted, to nobody at all)* I'm *over the top of it!*

`[beat]`

Narrator: And Crumb, who had been frightened of deep water for about as long as
it takes to sing the hour badly, stopped being frightened of it.

Narrator: And stayed stopped.

`[beat]`

`[beat]`

*Learning the steering.* He tries to swim. He tries hard. He goes nowhere at
all, in a circle, and gets out of breath doing it.

Narrator: Then he tried to make the rope go faster.

Narrator: He paddled with both claws. He kicked with all his legs.

`[beat]`

Narrator: He went round in a slow circle and arrived exactly where he started.

`[beat]`

Crumb: *(puffed, flat)* Right. No.

`[beat]`

Narrator: So he stopped, and let it be, and watched what the water was doing on
its own.

`[beat]`

Narrator: The tide was coming in. And in Salt Pocket Cove, coming in means going
*east* — along the wall, past the pools, towards the jetty. It had been doing it
since eight o'clock this morning.

Narrator: Crumb was just the first one to get on.

`[beat]`

*(He puts one claw in the water and holds it there, like a rudder. The coil
stops drifting away from the wall and points along it. That is all it takes.)*

Narrator: You cannot push the sea. Crumb found that out in about a minute.

Narrator: But you can point.

`[beat]`

Narrator: One claw in the water, held still, and the whole rope swung round and
lay along the wall, going the right way.

`[beat]`

Narrator: So that was the job. Crumb steered. The sea did the carrying.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Brick eight. Brick nine. Twenty past eleven.

Narrator: He had gone further in half an hour, sitting still, than he had gone
all morning trying.

`[beat]`

## `d-float.3` · Past his own front door

**When/where:** about twenty past eleven to a quarter to twelve · tide at step
five, and step four from half past eleven · the flooded foot of the sea wall
from about brick ten east to the jetty steps at brick seventeen.

*Staging.* Same low waterline shot, but the raft is not sitting up like a raft
any more. The coil rides lower every brick. Where it stood out of the water like
a cartwheel at brick one, by brick twelve only the top of it shows, dark and
fat, with the sea washing over the middle of it and Crumb standing in an inch of
water on top of his own boat. Below and to the left, the tide pools have gone
under. Ahead, the jetty stops being a thin dark line and starts being a jetty.

Narrator: Now. A wet rope is not the same as a dry rope.

`[beat]`

Narrator: It had gone out onto the water fat and light and honey-coloured, and
it had sat up like a boat.

Narrator: An hour later it was dark all the way through, and fatter than it
started, and it was riding lower and lower and lower.

`[beat]`

*(Crumb looks at his own feet. He is standing in water. He was not, ten minutes
ago.)*

Crumb: *(looking down)* …I'm getting *wetter.*

Narrator: He was. The rope was drinking, and the more it drank the heavier it
got, and the heavier it got the lower it sat.

`[beat]`

Narrator: It did not sink. Ropes do not sink; they just stop being boats.

`[beat]`

Crumb: *(to the rope, flat)* Don't you dare.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Half past eleven. Step four.

`[beat]`

*Brick fourteen.* The Shell Shelf, up under the cliff, going past on his left:
the little chalk ledge, the row of empty shells on it, and the big spiral one at
the end of the row, sitting there in the sun being exactly the right size for
somebody slightly bigger than Crumb.

Narrator: At brick fourteen he went past his own front door.

Narrator: A little chalk shelf, up under the cliff, with a row of empty shells
on it.

Narrator: And at the end of the row, the big spiral one.

`[beat]`

Crumb: *(going past)* Not today.

`[beat]`

Narrator: He had never seen his house from underneath before.

`[beat]`

*Brick fifteen.* Grumble's Rock, flat and barnacled — and under the sea now,
with the water going smooth over the top of it. Nobody on it.

Narrator: At brick fifteen, Grumble's Rock had gone under the water.

Narrator: There was nobody on it.

`[beat]`

Crumb: Hello, rock.

`[beat]`

Narrator: The rock was under the sea and did not say anything. Rocks are like
that.

`[beat]`

*Twenty to twelve. Brick fifteen.* Up on top of the wall, going east, a
periwinkle the size of a thumb, at one brick every quarter of an hour, talking.
Down at the foot of the wall, going east, a hermit crab on a soaked mooring
line, at about four times that. He is going to be past her in seconds and there
is nothing either of them can do about it.

Narrator: And at twenty to twelve, at brick fifteen, he caught up with Nan
Prattle.

Narrator: She was on the top of the wall. He was in the water at the bottom of
it.

Narrator: She was going east. He was going east. He was going east about four
times faster.

`[beat]`

`[beat]`

Narrator: And then she was behind him.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Nan Prattle was at brick fifteen at twenty to twelve, the way she is
every day of her life. She would be at brick sixteen in five minutes, and brick
seventeen at noon.

Narrator: Nothing that happened this morning moved her one single brick.

`[beat]`

*Brick sixteen. The jetty.* It comes up fast at the end, the way the last bit
always does. The twelve steps, going down into the water, and the water sitting
at step four with only four steps of wet stone showing above it.

Narrator: Brick sixteen.

Narrator: And then the jetty, which had been a thin dark line all morning, was a
jetty.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Twelve steps go down into the water at the jetty.

Narrator: Today the water was at step four. So there were four steps left above
it, and that was all.

`[beat]`

Crumb: *(quietly, steering in)* Four steps.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Crumb had come sixteen bricks. He had four steps to go.

`[beat]`

*(He points the coil in with one claw, gets the other on the wet stone of step
four, and stops the whole thing, which takes everything he has. Then he climbs
out. Then he starts hauling.)*

Narrator: He put a claw on step four and stopped a rope the size of a cartwheel,
which is not a thing he could have done at eight o'clock this morning.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Then he climbed out of the sea, and turned round, and started
pulling.

`[beat]`

Narrator: A wet rope is heavier than a dry rope. Much heavier. Crumb found that
out one step at a time.

Narrator: Step four. Step three. Step two. Step one.

`[beat]`

`[beat]`

*(The whole coil comes up over the top step and flops onto the stone, dark and
fat and enormous, in a spreading puddle of the sea. Crumb sits down on the top
step next to it. He is wet to the eyes. Water is running out of his shell.)*

Narrator: And the whole of it came up onto the top of the steps and lay there.

Narrator: Twice as fat as it went in, and dark all the way through, and
absolutely soaking.

`[beat]`

Narrator: And so was Crumb.

`[beat]`

Crumb: *(sitting down hard, wet to the eyes)* …made it.

`[beat]`

Narrator: A quarter to twelve. Step four. Brick seventeen.

Narrator: The bell is at noon.

`[beat]`

- **One variant, spent on Nan.** `F1` is the segment's whole choice-1 budget.
  Choice 1 is exhaustive, so per `tree.md` §5 there is no unmarked default and
  **both** sides carry ledger ids. It is placed where the world forces it: a

- **The out-of-depth beat is timed to the bible, not to taste.** Bible P-10 says
  the water is shallow at the wall's foot *until eleven*. So the bottom goes away
  at eleven o'clock, on the kittiwakes' four bad notes, which is also the
  segment's spoken clock (W-3). Fright is brief and physical — a leg down, no
  bottom, a fast heart — and it is beaten by *nothing happening*, not by rescue

  stayed stopped.
