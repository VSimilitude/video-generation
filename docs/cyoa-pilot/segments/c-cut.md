## `c-cut.1` · Twenty minutes and an old shell

**When/where:** twenty-five past ten to a quarter to eleven · tide at step seven,
and step six from half past ten · the Boat Shed doorstep, brick one, the far west
end of everything.

*Staging.* The slip in the sun. The whole coil lying on the warm stone where he
shoved it. Behind it the Boat Shed, door propped half a crab off the step on an
old oyster shell, one rough edge of the shell in the light. Below, the water
climbing the slip a stone at a time. Nothing else in frame. No Nan, no lobster,
no jetty — the jetty is a thin dark line sixteen bricks east and you cannot see
anything on it from here, which is the whole point of this scene.

Narrator: Twenty-five past ten. Brick one.

Narrator: Crumb looked at the rope. Then he looked at the shell.

`[beat]`

Crumb: Ten Crumbs. That's all the bell wants.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Nobody in Salt Pocket Cove had ever cut the mooring line.

Narrator: Not once. Not ever. Same rope, same length, since before Crumb was an
egg.

`[beat]`

Crumb: *(to the rope, politely)* I'm only taking the end.

`[beat]`

Narrator: The rope did not say anything. Ropes are like that.

`[beat]`

*(He walks out along the line from its neat finished end, counting under his
breath, and puts a claw down.)*

Narrator: So he walked out ten Crumbs from the end, the way he had counted in the
shed, and he put his claw down.

Crumb: There.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Then he thought about the Bosun's Loop.

Crumb: Loops eat rope.

`[beat]`

*(He moves his claw one whole crab further along.)*

Crumb: Eleven. Eleven's safe.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Now. The old oyster shell was busy. It was holding the Boat Shed door
up.

Crumb: *(to the door)* Sorry, door.

*(He gets both claws round the shell and hauls it out from under. The door comes
down behind him — a flat black bang, and the dust jumps off the doorstep.)*

Narrator: And he pulled the shell out.

`[beat]`

Narrator: The door came down.

`[beat]`

Narrator: And that was that. One small hermit crab on a doorstep at the far west
end of everything. A shut black door behind him. A rope in front of him. And an
old flat shell in his claws.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Nobody could see him.

Narrator: Nan Prattle was away at brick ten, going away, talking.

Narrator: Old Barnaby was away at brick seventeen, and Barnaby can see the whole
cove.

`[beat]`

Narrator: What Barnaby could see was a speck.

Narrator: A speck is not the same as a claw.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Nobody was at brick one. Nobody was anywhere near brick one.

`[beat]`

*(He lays the rope across the edge of the doorstep, so the stone is under it, and
sets the shell's rough edge on top.)*

Narrator: Crumb laid the rope over the edge of the step, so there was something
hard underneath it.

Narrator: Then he put the rough edge of the shell on top, and pushed.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Nothing.

Narrator: So he pulled it back, and pushed again.

Narrator: Ssh.

Narrator: Ssh.

Narrator: Ssh.

`[beat]`

Narrator: That was five minutes.

Narrator: The rope looked exactly the same as it had five minutes ago.

`[beat]`

Crumb: *(not stopping, flat)* Fine.

Narrator: Ssh. Ssh. Ssh.

`[beat]`

Narrator: That was ten minutes. Down at the jetty the water went from step seven
to step six, and nobody was watching that either.

`[beat]`

Narrator: And then, somewhere in the middle of all that rope, one thread went.

Narrator: *Tick.*

`[beat]`

Crumb: *(much too pleased for one thread)* HA.

`[beat]`

Narrator: One thread. Out of hundreds.

`[beat]`

Narrator: But a rope is like that. Once it starts, it keeps starting.

Narrator: Tick. Tick. Ticktick.

Narrator: Twelve minutes.

`[beat]`

*(The last of it lets go all at once. The two ends spring apart. Crumb sits down
hard on the doorstep with the shell on top of him.)*

Narrator: And then the rest of it went all at once, the way things do at the end.

`[beat]`

`[beat]`

Narrator: And there were two ropes.

`[beat]`

*(He sits where he landed. Wide, quiet, no music. The slip. The sun. Two ropes.)*

Narrator: Crumb sat on the doorstep and looked at them.

`[beat]`

Narrator: The sky did not fall in.

Narrator: The sea came up one more stone.

`[beat]`

*(Close on the new end: not an end at all. A soft fat fuzz of honey-coloured
threads, already opening out.)*

Narrator: The old end of the rope was neat. Somebody finished it a long time ago,
so it would never come undone.

Narrator: Crumb's end was not neat.

`[beat]`

Narrator: It was soft, and fat, and fuzzy, and it had already started coming
open.

Crumb: *(touching it with one claw)* Hm.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Then Crumb did the tidying, and the tidying was the slow bit.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Twelve minutes of sawing. The rest of the twenty was measuring and
tidying.

`[beat]`

Narrator: He lifted the door. He is good at that door now.

Narrator: He shoved the big rope back inside. It went in easier than it came out,
because there was eleven Crumbs less of it, and the step goes downhill that way.

Narrator: And he went up the crab pots with the sawn end in his claw, and hung it
back on its peg, where it lives.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Twenty-nine Crumbs of the only rope in Salt Pocket Cove. On its peg.
Tidy as anything.

`[beat]`

Narrator: With a fat fuzzy sawn-off end on it, hanging down at exactly the height
where somebody looks.

`[beat]`

`[beat]`

*(Crumb in the doorway, the oyster shell in his claws, the door held up on his
back. He looks in at the peg. He looks at the shell.)*

Narrator: Crumb stood in the doorway with the shell in his claws.

`[beat]`

Narrator: He could let the door down. Nobody looks in a shut shed.

`[beat]`

`[beat]`

*(He puts the shell back under the door and lets the door settle onto it. Propped
open. Exactly as it has stood all morning.)*

Narrator: He put the shell back under the door, and left it propped open.

Crumb: *(quietly)* Thank you, shell.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Anybody coming by could look straight in and see it.

Narrator: He did that on purpose.

`[beat]`

Narrator: A quarter to eleven. Step six. Sixteen bricks to go.

`[beat]`

Narrator: And there was something in Crumb's middle that had not been there at
twenty-five past ten.

Narrator: It was not a sore thing. It was not a sorry thing.

`[beat]`

Narrator: It was a small tight knot, and it had come out of a rope.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Nobody had ever cut the mooring line. Now somebody had. That is all it
was.

Narrator: But he had not told anybody yet, and it sat there.

`[beat]`

*(He takes the neat end — the old finished one — and gets it over his shoulder.
The fuzzy end is at the far end of eleven crab-lengths of rope, behind him, in
the dust.)*

Narrator: Crumb took hold of the neat end and put it over his shoulder.

Narrator: The fuzzy end he left behind him, where he could not see it.

`[beat]`

Narrator: And he pulled.

## `c-cut.2` · Sixteen bricks

**When/where:** a quarter to eleven to twenty past eleven · tide at step six, and
step five from eleven o'clock · the top of the sea wall, brick one east to brick
fourteen.

*Staging.* The wall top: a stone road about two crabs wide, running away east,
warm, with the beach on one side and the drop to the pools on the other from
brick ten. Crumb at the front, leaning so far forward he is nearly lying down.
Eleven crab-lengths of honey-coloured rope coming along behind him in a long
slack curve, and at the very back of it, out of shot most of the time, the fuzzy
end, ploughing a little line in the dust. Blue sky. Nobody hurrying anywhere
except one crab.

Narrator: Here is how you drag eleven Crumbs of mooring line along a sea wall,
when you are one Crumb.

`[beat]`

Narrator: You lean forward until you are nearly falling over.

Narrator: Then you fall over. That is the first bit.

Narrator: Then you get up and do it again.

`[beat]`

Narrator: And the rope comes after you. A bit at a time. A bit later than you
would like.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Crumb did that for a quarter of an hour.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Then the kittiwakes sang eleven o'clock.

Narrator: There are four notes in the hour. They got two. They always get the
same two.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Crumb was at brick seven.

Narrator: Which is not very far, if you are counting bricks.

`[beat]`

Narrator: It is quite far, if you are counting rope.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Some things Crumb found out on the way.

Narrator: One. A rope does not come when you pull it. It comes afterwards.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Two. If you stop, the rope does not stop.

*(He stops for a breather. The rope catches up with him and folds itself gently
over his head.)*

`[beat]`

Crumb: *(from underneath)* Sorry.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Three. From brick ten there are tide pools down below the wall.

Narrator: And rope loves an edge.

*(The tail slides off the top and goes over the side, and hangs there, being
heavy.)*

`[beat]`

Narrator: Twice the end went over the side, and hung down, and got heavier.

Narrator: And twice Crumb walked all the way back along his own rope and hauled
it up again, and then walked all the way to the front.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Which is how you turn sixteen bricks into about thirty.

`[beat]`

Narrator: He kept it out of the water, though. Every time. He was very fussy
about that.

Crumb: *(hauling, to the rope)* You're staying dry.

`[beat]`

*(Somewhere around brick eleven: his back end is further out of the shell than it
was, and the shell is riding high and loose on him.)*

Narrator: By brick eleven his back end was sticking further out of his shell than
it had ever stuck in his life.

Crumb: *(over his shoulder, to the shell)* Stay on.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Brick fourteen is where Crumb lives.

Narrator: A little chalk shelf, and a row of empty shells on it, and the big
spiral one at the end of the row.

`[beat]`

Crumb: *(going past, not stopping)* Not yet.

`[beat]`

Narrator: And at brick fourteen, at twenty past eleven, Crumb caught up with Nan
Prattle.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Nan Prattle left brick one at eight o'clock this morning.

Narrator: Crumb left brick one at a quarter to eleven.

`[beat]`

Narrator: That is how fast Nan Prattle goes.

`[beat]`

Nan: Crumb! Crumb, Crumb. That's a rope.

Crumb: *(hauling, no breath at all)* Mm.

Nan: Don't stop, my love.

Crumb: *(hauling)* Not stopping.

`[beat]`

Nan: *(delighted)* Neither am I.

`[beat]`

*(He goes past her. Two creatures on the same wall going the same way, one of
them very slightly faster. It takes about half a minute and it is the first time
in Crumb's whole life.)*

Narrator: And Crumb went past Nan Prattle. Same wall. Same way. Faster.

Narrator: That had never happened before.

`[beat]`

`[beat]`

Narrator: Now. Nan Prattle knows everything about everybody in Salt Pocket Cove.

Narrator: And Crumb had a whole rope's worth of time to tell her one more thing.

`[beat]`

*(He hauls. She talks. The words are right there and they do not come out.)*

Narrator: He did not say it.

`[beat]`

Narrator: The knot got a bit tighter, and he kept pulling.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Twenty past eleven. Brick fourteen. Step five.

## `c-cut.3` · The last three bricks

**When/where:** twenty past eleven to a quarter to twelve · tide at step five,
and step four from half past eleven · the top of the sea wall, brick fourteen
east to the top of the jetty steps, brick seventeen.

*Staging.* Close enough now that the jetty is not a dark line any more: you can
count the posts. The last post, with the Tide Bell on it, and no rope on the
bell. Below the wall the pools have gone under and the water has come up to the
wall's own foot. Crumb head-down, hauling, in short goes with long rests. The
fuzzy end a long way behind him, out in the sun, quietly getting worse.

**Note to the assembler:** the segment stops on the arrival, before anybody
speaks or Crumb looks up. Who is at the top of the steps, and what is said about
the sawn end, is `ending` (E1, E2, E5).

`[beat]`

Narrator: The last three bricks took as long as the first ten.

`[beat]`

Narrator: At twenty-five past eleven he came to brick fifteen.

Narrator: Grumble's Rock is down below there. Flat, and barnacled, and shining
just under the water.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Here is why the end is the slow bit.

Narrator: One. The water was up at the foot of the wall now, and the rope kept
wanting to go and look at it.

Narrator: A dry rope is heavy. A wet rope is twice.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Two. Crumb had been pulling for forty minutes and he was out of puff.

`[beat]`

Narrator: And three.

`[beat]`

*(He rests. He looks east. The jetty is bigger than the last time he looked.)*

Narrator: Every time he stopped for a breath, he looked east.

Narrator: And every time, the jetty was a bit bigger.

`[beat]`

Narrator: You could see the last post now.

Narrator: You could see the bell on it.

`[beat]`

Narrator: You could see there was no rope on the bell.

`[beat]`

Narrator: And the closer Crumb got, the tighter the knot in his middle went.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Because on that post there is somebody who has watched this cove for
ninety years, and does not miss things.

Narrator: And Crumb was bringing him a rope with a fuzzy end on it.

`[beat]`

Crumb: *(quietly, pulling)* He'll see it.

`[beat]`

`[beat]`

Narrator: And Crumb pulled anyway.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Half past eleven. The water went to step four. Brick sixteen.

Narrator: Half an hour to noon, and one brick to go, and one brick was plenty of
work.

`[beat]`

*(Pull. Rest. Pull. Rest. The rope comes along behind him in its own time. Behind
Crumb, one brick back and coming, a periwinkle, still talking.)*

Narrator: Behind him, one brick back, Nan Prattle came on. She had not stopped
either.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Brick seventeen is the last brick.

`[beat]`

*(He comes up over the last of the wall with his head down and the rope over his
shoulder, and gets it onto the flat at the top of the jetty steps, and stops
there. He does not look up. He is looking at the stone about a claw in front of
his face.)*

Narrator: At a quarter to twelve, Crumb dragged eleven Crumbs of mooring line up
onto the top of the jetty steps, and stopped.

`[beat]`

Narrator: A quarter to twelve. Brick seventeen. Step four.

`[beat]`

Narrator: He had not looked at the sawn end since a quarter to eleven, and he was
not going to look at it now.

`[beat]`

*(Wide. The rope lying all the way back along the top of the wall behind him, and
at the far end of it, in the sun, the fuzz.)*

Narrator: Behind him, at the end of all that rope, the sawn end lay in the sun,
coming open, soft as a dandelion.

`[beat]`

Narrator: Fifteen minutes to noon.

`[beat]`

### Writer's notes (not part of the state docs)

- **Insert budget: one used, one allowed.** C-V1, the rock at brick fifteen. It
  is forced: on `pipe` paths Grumble left the rock at half past ten and the rock
  is empty for the whole of Crumb's drag; on `wall` paths he is sitting on it
  until half past eleven and Crumb goes by at twenty-five past. The skeleton
  reads correctly either way — the lead-in and the lead-out are shared, and the
  variant is one frame of one rock.
- **Nan is skeleton, not a variant.** She is at brick fourteen from a quarter
  past eleven to half past on every path, and Crumb's drag puts him there at
  twenty past, so he overtakes her east of brick twelve whatever he chose at
  choice one. She gets three short lines, gives nothing and learns nothing, and
  does not stop (P-4). On `wall` paths the kelp cord stays in his shell-pocket:
  P5 and P6 are paid at brick seventeen at noon (E4), and the moment for that is
  not a moment when neither of them can stop.
- **The haul is bare-clawed on both paths.** Nan's cord made a haul-loop for the
  forty-Crumb coil in `mid` (M3), but eleven Crumbs of rope has an end you can
  get a claw round, so there is no loop here — which keeps the physical comedy
  word-for-word identical on all four paths and spends no budget.
- **Nobody sees him do it, and the geography does the work.** He saws on the
  doorstep at brick one with the door shut behind him. Nan is away at brick ten
  and going away; Barnaby is away at brick seventeen, sixteen brick-lengths off,
  and can see a speck, not a claw; Grumble is at brick fifteen or already east of
  it on either timetable. That is why E5 can land: Barnaby has to find the sawn
  end, not have watched it happen.
- **The twenty minutes is the measuring, the sawing *and* the tidying.** Bible
  P-1 is eighty minutes all-in whatever the load, so the scene budget is twenty
  minutes flat: walking out ten Crumbs and re-marking at eleven, then twelve
  minutes of shell-on-rope counted out loud, then lifting the door, shoving
  twenty-nine Crumbs back inside, going up the crab pots, hanging it and
  re-propping the door. The narration says the split out loud, so a child hears
  the clock and an auditor can add it up.
- **The pacing of the drag is deliberately uneven** — thirteen bricks in
  thirty-five minutes, then three in twenty-five — and the segment says so out
  loud ("the last three bricks took as long as the first ten"). It is what
  dragging feels like, and it is also what makes the timetable work: he passes
  Grumble's Rock at twenty-five past eleven, while Grumble is still on it on the
  default day, and arrives at brick seventeen at a quarter to twelve with Nan one
  brick behind. Total: twenty sawing plus sixty dragging, eighty minutes, P-1
  intact.
- **The door is left propped on purpose, and that is the whole moral load.** He
  could shut it. He doesn't. He hangs the rest of the rope back on its peg, at
  looking height, and leaves the shed open — which opens P7 and is the reason
  Crumb can own up on his own at E5 rather than be caught. Nothing in the segment
  says cutting was wrong (T-2); the narrator says only *nobody had ever done it,
  now somebody had*, and lets the knot sit as weather.
- **`feeling:` and `shell:` are identical on both docs.** Nothing in either
  choice-one ledger touches them, and after eighty minutes of the same rope both
  crabs are in the same state. The shell riding loose is the plant for P1's
  payoff on the long pull at `ending`.
- **The raw end travels behind him, unwatched.** He hauls on the neat old end; the
  fuzz is eleven crab-lengths back in the dust, fraying the whole way, and he
  does not look at it once after a quarter to eleven. That is deliberate staging
  for E5 — Barnaby sees it before Crumb does.
