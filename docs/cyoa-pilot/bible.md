# World bible — *Crumb and the Bell That Lost Its Rope*

Phase-2 CYOA pilot (`docs/CYOA.md`). Kids' series register, first audience age
six. **This world is standalone** — no Drip, no Sunny, no Cloudia, no
water-cycle canon. Nothing here is branch-specific: everything below is true on
all four paths, and nothing in any segment may contradict it.

**Story span: one morning.** Eight o'clock to noon, continuous, no gaps and no
night. Every segment states its own start and end time and both must sit inside
that morning.

---

## Characters

Five voices, house maximum (see the header of `src/videos/wind/narration.mjs`).
Voice picks are provisional — this pilot is text only.

| Who | What | Personality in one line | Voice / tone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Crumb** | hermit crab, about six | Earnest, quick, tries hard, apologises to objects out loud. | small and fast; `af_sky` ~1.08 (audition) |
| **Old Barnaby** | acorn barnacle, ancient | Bolted to the last jetty post since before anyone remembers; keeper of the Tide Bell; tells the same three stories and finds that funny. | slow, warm, gravelly; `am_liam` ~0.92 |
| **Nan Prattle** | periwinkle, grandmother | Creeps the sea wall every morning to meet her grandsons' boat; talks the whole way; knows everything about everybody. | continuous, fond, gossipy; `bf_emma` ~1.0 |
| **Grumble** | lobster, old | Lives in the Pipe. Comes out to his rock to get through his nine complaints about the weather. Deadpan, doleful, secretly loves the cove. | flat, doleful; `am_puck` ~0.90 |
| **Narrator** | — | Warm storyteller, occasional deadpan. May cameo-voice an object if a scene needs one. | `af_heart` 1.0 |

**Off stage, never seen close up, no lines:** the **Prattle boys**, Nan's
grandsons, crew of the fishing boat *Sandhopper*; and the **kittiwake choir** on
the cliff, who sing the hour and do nothing else.

No other named character exists. A writer who needs a person must use one of
these five.

## The place: Salt Pocket Cove

A crescent of beach under a chalk cliff. Everything in the story sits on one
line, west to east, and the **sea wall** is the ruler: seventeen bricks, brick
one at the west end, brick seventeen at the east end.

| Brick | What is there |
| --- | --- |
| **1** (west) | **The Boat Shed** — tarred wood, on the slip. Its door sticks and must be **lifted, not pulled**. Inside: crab pots, an old oyster shell wedged in the door frame, and **the spare mooring line** on a peg. |
| 1–17 | **The sea wall** — a low wall you can walk along the top of. Nan's road. |
| 10–16 | **The tide pools**, below the wall. |
| **14** | **The Shell Shelf** — a chalk ledge up under the cliff. Crumb's home: a row of empty shells, with **one big spiral shell** at the end of the row. |
| **15** | **Grumble's Rock** — flat, barnacled, in the tide pools. |
| **17** (east) | **The jetty.** Twelve steps down to the water. On the last post: the **Tide Bell**, and **Old Barnaby**, at about the height of step five. Beside the top of the steps: **the grating** — the east mouth of the Pipe. |
| — | **The Pipe** — an old stone drain running straight through the headland *behind* the beach, from the grating at brick seventeen to somewhere at the west end. Pitch dark, lined with green slime. Grumble sleeps just inside the grating. |
| above | **The cliff**, with the kittiwake ledges. |
| beyond | **The bar** — a shallow ridge the *Sandhopper* cannot cross until high water. |

Two roads run from the jetty to the Boat Shed: **the wall** (long, bright, along
the top of the bricks) and **the Pipe** (short, dark, through the rock).

## Where everyone is at eight o'clock

- **Crumb** — Shell Shelf, brick fourteen, waking up.
- **Old Barnaby** — his post at the jetty, brick seventeen. Where he always is.
- **Nan Prattle** — sea wall, brick one, setting off east.
- **Grumble** — asleep just inside the grating, brick seventeen.
- **The *Sandhopper*** — out beyond the bar with the Prattle boys, waiting.
- **The tide** — at step eleven, rising.
- **Weather** — flat calm; high thin cloud that burns off by ten; clear and
  sunny after. The same on every path.

## Rules of the world — physical

These are laws, not preferences. A scene that breaks one is wrong.

- **P-1 · The cove is eighty minutes wide.** Jetty to Boat Shed, by any road, in
  either direction, takes eighty minutes. The cove's saying for it is *"Dark and
  short, bright and long, both the same."* **No choice in this story buys or
  loses time.** Every journey between the cove's two ends takes eighty minutes
  all-in, whatever the load and however the time splits between working and
  moving — twenty minutes of sawing and sixty of dragging is the same eighty as
  twenty-five of waiting and fifty-five of riding, and neither is a faster road.
- **P-2 · The tide is the clock.** The water climbs **one jetty step every half
  hour**: step eleven at eight o'clock, step three at noon. Noon is high tide.
  Nothing anyone does changes this. The step number is visible in every scene at
  the jetty and is the writers' and auditors' shared clock.
- **P-3 · Barnaby cannot move.** Not a little, not ever. He is bolted on. He can
  see the whole cove from his post and he can talk; he cannot fetch, carry,
  reach, help or leave. Any plan that has Barnaby doing a physical thing is void.
  **He speaks only when Crumb is on or at the jetty** — he does not shout across
  the cove in this story.
- **P-4 · Nan cannot stop.** She creeps east along the wall at one brick every
  quarter of an hour and she talks while she creeps. She never stops, never
  hurries, never turns round, never leaves the wall. Anyone who wants to talk to
  her walks alongside her, and if they are going the other way they walk
  backwards for a moment and then get on. **Nothing any branch does changes
  where Nan is at a given time.**
- **P-5 · Grumble's day is a list.** Nine complaints about the weather, in a
  fixed order, twelve minutes apart. He will not skip one, reorder one, or do a
  tenth. When the list is finished, so is he.
- **P-6 · There is one rope.** The spare mooring line on the peg in the Boat Shed
  is the only rope in Salt Pocket Cove. It is exactly the length a mooring line
  is cut to and nobody has ever cut it. (The *Sandhopper* carries her own warp,
  so the boat is never in trouble — the bell is what is at stake, not the boat.)
- **P-7 · The Boat Shed door sticks and opens on a lift.** Always. It cannot be
  locked, and it cannot be pulled open however hard you pull.
- **P-8 · The Pipe is dark and green and eighty minutes long.** It is not
  dangerous. It is damp, black, and lined with green slime that will not wash off
  in a day. **Nobody now living knows where its west end comes out.** Grumble
  sleeps just inside the grating and has never in his life gone further in; he
  says he has no interest, and that is true.
- **P-9 · No bell, no boat.** The Tide Bell tells the *Sandhopper* the water is
  over the bar. If the bell is late the boat simply waits outside, and Nan waits
  at brick seventeen. That is the whole of the stakes and it is sad enough.
- **P-10 · The sea is cold, slow and awkward, never lethal.** The beach shelves
  gently; the water is shallow at the wall's foot until eleven. Crabs float.
  Nobody is ever in danger of drowning.
- **P-11 · Nobody flies and there is no magic.** The kittiwakes sing the hour
  from the cliff — eight, nine, ten, eleven, twelve — badly, and do nothing else.
  Everything that happens could happen on a real beach with small talking
  animals on it.

## Rules of the world — tone

- **T-1 · Six years old.** Short sentences, one idea each. Speech bubbles six
  words maximum. No digits anywhere in narration text: *"twenty to nine"*, not a
  numeral (TTS rule, see `src/videos/wind/narration.mjs`). The six-word cap binds
  **on-screen speech bubbles at video time, not spoken dialogue lines**; a longer
  line is split across bubbles or carried by voice alone, and that is decided at
  staging time, not in the script.
- **T-2 · Neither option is the right one.** In either choice. No character ever
  says or hints that the other way would have been better; the story never
  punishes a choice; nothing that follows a choice is *worse* than what follows
  the other, only *different*.
- **T-3 · Nobody is a villain.** Grumble is grumpy and kind. Nan is nosy and
  kind. Barnaby is old and kind.
- **T-4 · No peril and no darkness-as-threat.** The Pipe is uncomfortable, not
  frightening. Nobody shouts at Crumb.
- **T-5 · Crumb solves it.** In every branch. Grown-ups give information; they
  never rescue.
- **T-6 · Give the gags room.** The comedy here is deadpan repetition (Grumble's
  list, Nan's talking, Barnaby's three stories) and physical smallness. A
  punchline gets a beat of silence after it, per `docs/STYLE.md`.
- **T-7 · Nothing is lost forever.** A cut rope can be spliced. A wet rope dries.
  A shell that is too small can be swapped.

## Rules for writers — what closes a judgment call

- **W-1 · No new named characters and no new locations.** If you need a thing, it
  is already in this file. If it truly is not, it is a note back to the
  architect, not an invention.
- **W-2 · Nothing may differ between branches unless a ledger entry in
  `tree.md` says so.** If two branches want different props, weather, NPC
  positions or off-stage facts and no entry licenses it, the branch is wrong.
- **W-3 · Every scene records its time and its tide step**, and both must agree
  with P-2. Times are spoken aloud in words at least once per segment so a child
  can hear the clock ticking.
- **W-4 · Nan's brick and Grumble's place are computed, never chosen.** Use the
  timetable in `tree.md`. A branch that puts an NPC somewhere the timetable does
  not is wrong, even if nothing in the scene depends on it.
