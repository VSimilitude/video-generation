# EP 4 — TREATMENT A: "THE KITCHEN"

Angle: photosynthesis staged as a kitchen. The seed is a tiny chef — the
smallest thing in the world running the world's biggest kitchen from one
spot in the dirt, ordering the sky around. Ingredient arrivals are
deliveries she inspects, bosses, and checks off. Comedy centre of gravity:
the tiny-boss register colliding with Sunny's maximum ego (two characters
who both believe they are in charge of the same kitchen), and the indignity
of the biggest objects in the universe taking orders from something the
size of a full stop. Pedagogy payoff: the recipe actually cooks — sugar
from light + water + air; oxygen is what the kitchen vents out the window.

---

## 1. Logline + title candidates

**Logline.** The smallest thing in the world opens the world's biggest
kitchen from one spot in the dirt. To cook the very first sugar she has to
order the sky itself around — and by the last delivery the mechanism has
cornered her into saying, out loud, the one thing nobody in the series ever
wants to say to Sunny: the kitchen does not work without him.

**Title candidates** (pattern: hero's name + story noun):

1. **"Pip and the World's Biggest Kitchen"** (primary)
2. "Pip and the Recipe for Sunshine"
3. "Pip and the Kitchen in the Dirt"

## 2. Hero spec

- **Name: Pip** (brief's working name kept — one syllable matches
  Drip/Puff/Ray, and it roll-calls beautifully). Alternatives logged per
  brief: *Poppy* (real-world tiniest-seed resonance, two syllables),
  *Pippin*, *Bean* (risks species-specificity), *Basil* (chef pun,
  parent-only). Recommendation stays Pip.
- **Want:** to run the kitchen — every ingredient delivered, inspected,
  and cooked RIGHT. Loud, active, and fully expressible from a fixed
  position: a chef never leaves the kitchen anyway; the kitchen is the one
  job in the world you do by making everything come to you. She is never
  bored and never counts time — between deliveries she is always PREPPING
  (roots down, leaves up, orders shouted). **Not a waiting arc**; the quest
  is a supply chain and she is its dispatcher.
- **Attitude / spine:** the one character in the world who is not
  impressed by Sunny. She classifies him on arrival as "the delivery
  service." The mechanism spends three acts building the case against her
  position — every single ingredient traces to him (his light, his lifted
  rain, his wind) — until the finished sugar, which is literally stored
  sunshine, forces the admission out of her. Her love language is job
  titles, so the admission comes as a promotion: he was never the delivery
  boy — he is **the oven**. Plus the plain fact, out loud: no sun, no
  sugar; no sugar, no her. Then a grudging "...Thank you." — and she is
  instantly the chef again. Nobody falls; both egos stay in charge of the
  same kitchen. (Sunny exits the episode at maximum altitude for the ep-5
  drop; no wrongness fired anywhere.)
- **Comic register (casting target — top of cast table):** *smallest voice
  in the show, biggest authority in the show.* Clipped, brisk, bossy,
  certain, warm underneath, never shrill, never whiny. A drill-sergeant
  soufflé chef aged about seven. Audition on her three hardest asks: an
  enormous order shouted at the sky, the two-word stamp "It will do.", and
  the quiet "...Thank you." MiniMax, new voiceId (the episode's only new
  voice).
- **Motion law:** once planted she NEVER moves — no slides, no hops, no
  camera cheats. Her only locomotion is **growth**, vertical and earned
  (sprout push, leaf by leaf); her gesture rig is her leaves (leaf-point,
  leaf-stamp on the order ticket) plus eye-lines. Prop: the order
  ticket/recipe card (SVG, foreground).
- **How stuck-in-one-spot is PLAYED — the stoop rule:** everything that
  addresses her must come DOWN to her mark — Ray bends his whole ribbon,
  Drip falls to her, Puff flattens, Sunny stoops a sunbeam. The biggest
  objects in the universe queueing at a full stop's service hatch is the
  episode's standing sight gag, and it is honest: that is what a plant
  actually does — it makes the world come to it.

## 3. Act-by-act beat sheet

### COLD OPEN

- **CO-1 — The kid.** The kid silhouette (fourth episode running — keeps
  the streak) kneels in a meadow and blows on a dry seed head. Seeds fly.
  Wind does the work: the one free ride of Pip's life, and a free ep-2
  callback with no cast cost.
- **CO-2 — The ride + ROLL CALL firing #1.** Pip mid-air among identical
  drifting seeds, cheerfully naming them: *Pip, Pipsqueak, other Pip,
  Pippa, Pip the third.* One flat Narrator middle line (family-name
  explanation, 0.92). Unbothered, unseasoned button from Pip in chef
  register — she is already hiring them ("Good staff."). Nobody replies
  inside the naming.
- **CO-3 — Landing. Planted.** Dirt. She looks the premises over exactly
  like a chef inspecting an empty restaurant. **"It will do."** (source
  firing of the sameAs chain). Narrator frames the premise: the smallest
  thing in the world, about to open the biggest kitchen in it — and it
  will never move again. Pip is entirely unbothered: a good chef never
  leaves the kitchen. → TITLE CARD.

### ACT 1 — THE KITCHEN OPENS (setup + LIGHT)

- **1.1 — Sunrise.** Sunny's greeting+brag pair, series constant
  ("GOOD MORNING, EVERYBODY!" → "I invented mornings!"). The whole meadow
  adores him. One spot of dirt does not look up. Narrator plants the
  engine flat: the one character in the world who is not impressed.
- **1.2 — The order.** Pip opens the kitchen: the recipe card revealed.
  Tonight's dish: SUGAR. Ingredients: water, a pinch of air; cooked on
  light. Dirt is NOT on the recipe — first pedagogy beat carried as chef
  indignation when the obvious question is raised (load-bearing line:
  **"Dirt is not food. This is a kitchen, not a mud puddle."**). Plants
  make their own food; nobody eats dirt here.
- **1.3 — Equipment.** Her one big physical act: the sprout push (effort
  comedy from a dot; her only power move all episode, so it gets room).
  Two leaves up = the pans. She brags about the equipment: the green is
  the catching stuff — the pan that catches light (chlorophyll not named,
  no card).
- **1.4 — First order shouted at the sky.** A full stop with a clipboard
  orders the SKY around. Sunny assumes it is fan mail. **Seed↔Sunny engine
  opens**: kitchen-ownership dispute — he announces it is HIS kitchen
  (everything is); she classifies him as **"the delivery service."** The
  indignity detonates. "You're welcome! HA! HA!" (firing 1 of a planned
  6; ceiling 8).
- **1.5 — LIGHT DELIVERY: Ray + Blue cameo.** Ray entrance, stooping his
  whole ribbon down to her hatch. Narrator: **"Different show. Same
  light."** (fresh blank, 0.92 flat). "Look up. That's me." (firing 1).
  **Blue ricochets in claiming first** — "First! Sorry, sprout! I am
  FIRST!" — and is WRONG: the light has been arriving since sunrise;
  Sunny was on the job hours before anyone (Sunny, delighted: first was
  him, HA!). Anti-Sunny seed planted, not paid, Blue exits unbothered and
  still certain. **Violet garnish:** stands in the delivery queue behind
  Blue, vibrating, never inspected, never noticed, exits. No line.
- **1.6 — Light inspection (Pip↔Ray two-hander).** She inspects the light
  like produce. Ray — a pedant — cannot let the recipe card stand: light
  is not an ingredient, it is the COOKING (load-bearing correction:
  **"It is not an ingredient. It is the oven."**) — which makes Sunny
  MORE important, which Ray finds structurally upsetting, which Sunny
  overhears ("HA! HA!", firing 2). Pip stamps the delivery: **"It will
  do."** (chain firing 2). Ray verifies the pan: the green catches; the
  pedant approves the equipment. Physics honesty: the light is used, not
  taken — Ray pours; Ray remains Ray.
- **1.7 — Act button.** Pip is already shouting the next order: WATER.
  Sunny, from altitude: also him — he lifts ALL the water (ep-1 canon,
  annoyingly true). The ensemble runner starts: everyone on stage
  visibly decides not to confirm that out loud.

### ACT 2 — THE WATER ORDER

- **2.1 — Prep, not waiting.** No countdowns, no clock: she DRILLS. Roots
  down — the straw. Busy-work comedy of a dot bossing her own roots. Soil
  stays an unexplained given: she drinks through it; nobody says what it
  is. (Establishing wide here: **the volcano on the measured horizon**,
  scenery, continuously visible in all subsequent wides. No stir yet.)
- **2.2 — The delivery van (OPTIONAL Cloudia one-scene cameo).** A grand
  cloud arrives. "Special delivery, darling." The grandest hotel manager
  in the sky, moonlighting as a courier and insisting it is a promotion.
  One scene, one-two lines, no arc. (Cuttable without a scar — flagged in
  §8.)
- **2.3 — WATER DELIVERY: Drip.** Rain. Standing entrance, identical
  text, third firing: "Hi! It's me! I'm the weather!" Narrator:
  **"Different show. Same weather."** (fresh blank — and a joke, since it
  buttons her own line). **Seed↔Drip two-hander:** Drip wants to be taken
  as BIG; Pip logs the delivery as "One water." Size dispute: THE WEATHER
  versus a line on a clipboard.
- **2.4 — The straw.** The kitchen's front door is at the BOTTOM: water
  cannot land on the pans, it has to come up through the roots. Drip goes
  down into the dirt and rides the straw UP — the traveller travels, the
  chef stands still, pedagogy rides the slide (plants drink through
  roots). Drip inside the plant, thrilled ("I'm the plumbing!" energy).
  Sunny claims the rain from altitude — he lifted it, he is right —
  "You're welcome! HA! HA!" (firing 3). Runner beat: Drip almost credits
  the sun for the lifting, catches herself mid-word.
- **2.5 — Water inspection.** **"It will do."** (chain firing 3). Drip is
  outraged at the grade. Narrator, flat, holds the button: it is the
  highest grade she gives. (Deadpan gets the room; button unseasoned.)
- **2.6 — The kitchen grows.** More leaves, more pans — growth beat over
  drawn geometry, so it carries character business inside it per the
  measured standard: Ray still delivering, Drip waving from a leaf vein,
  Pip promoting equipment. Wide: volcano present, asleep, unremarked.
- **2.7 — Act button.** Next order: AIR. Sunny: ALSO him — he makes all
  the wind (ep-2 canon, true again). Every ingredient now traces to
  Sunny. The ensemble's faces do the math and refuse to say the total.
  "You're welcome! HA! HA!" (firing 4). Pip does not look up.

### ACT 3 — THE TINY PART OF THE AIR (air + THE COOK + vent + admission)

- **3.1 — AIR DELIVERY: Puff.** Entrance; Narrator: **"Different show.
  Same stuff."** (fresh blank — his ep-2 arc word). Puff catch-phrase,
  exactly once: "You can't see me. But you can FEEL me." **Seed↔Puff
  two-hander — the parts dispute:** she ordered "air"; Puff delivers ALL
  of himself; she wants only **the tiny invisible part** (carbon dioxide,
  named once in speech, no card). Puff is not offended — he is THRILLED:
  someone finally wants a specific PART of him. Being made of parts is
  the most stuff-like thing that has ever happened to him. (Ep-2 arc
  respected: no apology reflex, no invisibility crisis.)
- **3.2 — The tiny part goes in.** The leaf takes the tiny part; Puff
  watches a piece of himself join a recipe. **"It will do."** (chain
  firing 4 — the last clean firing).
- **3.3 — THE COOK + BIG WORD.** Water up the straw, tiny part of air in
  the pan, light on full. The pan cooks. **BigWordBeat: PHOTOSYNTHESIS**
  — the five-syllable chant monster, and the fun is that it defeats the
  cast in character-true ways: Pip clips it like an order, Drip
  performs it, Puff runs out of breath in the middle, Ray says it
  perfectly and nobody thanks him. SUGAR appears — the first food ever
  cooked on screen from nothing anyone could see. Ray, pedant, delivers
  the fact that costs him the most: the sugar is light, stored —
  **"Look down. That is also me."** (firing 2, variant per his sunset
  precedent) — a correction that lands, again, entirely in Sunny's
  favor. Mind-blower #3 carried here in passing: sugar is stored
  sunshine — eating an apple is eating sunlight.
- **3.4 — THE VENT + THE VOLCANO STIR.** The kitchen breathes out the
  window: oxygen, the leftover. Puff gets a part BACK ("A part came
  back!") — the air gave a piece and got a piece; nothing stolen,
  physics honest. **Wide shot, and on the measured horizon, wordless:
  the volcano's silhouette swells once — a sleeper turning over — and
  releases one small curl of smoke that thins and is gone (~2.5s).**
  Nobody looks at it, nothing names it, no music sting; the visual rhyme
  with the venting kitchen is for rewatchers only. Ladder rep landed:
  ep-3 one-eye → ep-4 small stir → ep-5 wakes.
- **3.5 — What the leftover is.** Narrator, quiet, over the venting leaf:
  that leftover is not rubbish. It is breath. (Staged only; the hard
  mind-blower version is saved for the recap.)
- **3.6 — THE ADMISSION (the spine's held beat).** Pip inspects the
  sugar. Raises the stamp. **Cannot say "It will do."** — the pattern
  break IS the punchline, held in silence (the chain's fifth slot stays
  empty). Then, in the only register she has — job titles — the
  promotion: he was never the delivery boy; **he is the oven.** And the
  plain fact, out loud, that everyone else spent three acts not saying:
  no sun, no sugar; no sugar, no her. **"...Thank you."** Long held
  silence → Sunny's biggest **"You're welcome! HA! HA!"** (firing 5),
  also held. Then both are instantly themselves again — Sunny unbearable,
  Pip announcing the oven is getting a schedule. Two bosses, one
  kitchen, both right. Nobody is humiliated; the admission is a
  promotion, not a surrender.
- **3.7 — The tree (mind-blower #1).** The big old tree at the meadow's
  edge (established in the wides all episode). Puff carries the fact at
  his proudest: all of that — trunk, branches, the lot — is cooked air
  and water; the dirt underneath barely lost any weight. **Rule stamp:
  "A TREE IS MADE OF AIR."** Puff, reverent: he is a building material.
- **3.8 — Sunset + ROLL CALL firing #2.** The same seeds from the cold
  open pop up in a row across the field — the wind was not a careful
  delivery service. Pip's goodnight naming: *Goodnight Pipsqueak,
  goodnight other Pip, goodnight Pippa, goodnight Pip the third.* One
  flat Narrator middle line. Unbothered button in chef register (the
  kitchen is a chain now). Goodbyes ride the same beat: Drip up,
  Puff off, Ray out with the last light.

### RECAP + END CARD

- **R-1 — Chant:** PHO-TO-SYN-THE-SIS, syllable-clapped, the whole cast
  failing and landing it together.
- **R-2 — Mind-blower #2 (the closer):** the oxygen in your next breath
  was breathed out by a plant. The kid silhouette, back at the meadow,
  takes one visible breath. Held.
- **R-3 — Tease / next-time question (end card):** **"What is dirt
  actually made of?"** (see §6).

## 7. Estimated runtime and where the risk is

**Estimate: 14–17 minutes** (between ep 2 and ep 3; ~34 scenes/beats,
three delivery set pieces, one cook set piece; runtime rule says hit the
beats, never target a number).

**Risks, ranked:**

1. **The admission scene's tone (3.6).** The whole episode aims at one
   held beat that must read as a promotion, never a defeat — "wrongness
   is never a comeuppance" binds even though nobody is technically wrong.
   Guardrails in the design: the admission arrives inside her own tic
   (job titles), she reclassifies him upward rather than conceding
   downward, and she is back to bossing him within two lines. If the
   ear-check reads it as her losing, the fix is line-level, not
   structural.
2. **Three deliveries = structural sameness.** The inspection shape
   repeats by design (that is the chain), so each delivery runs a
   DIFFERENT dispute engine: classification (Ray), size (Drip), parts
   (Puff). If any two feel alike at script stage, the disputes are the
   dial, not the deliveries.
3. **Sunny monotony.** A character who is right five times in a row can
   flatline. The escalation is not his (his firings are flat-identical by
   design); it is the ensemble's mounting effort NOT to confirm him, and
   Pip's holdout shrinking one delivery at a time. Track his "HA! HA!"
   spacing against the laugh map; headroom of 2 under the ceiling exists
   for punch-up, not for padding.
4. **The new voice.** The register ("smallest voice, biggest authority")
   is castable but narrow — bossy-child reads shrill fast. Audition on
   the three hardest lines listed in §2; if no candidate lands the quiet
   "...Thank you.", the register bends toward dry rather than loud.
5. **Laugh-gap watch points:** 2.1 (root drilling) and 2.6 (growth) are
   the two stretches nearest to drawn geometry; both carry character
   business inside them by design (per the measured standard). Recompute
   the gap map from the manifest at cut time; do not trust this
   document's self-score.

## 8. Deviations from the brief, flagged loudly

1. **THREE "Different show. Same ___." firings** (light / weather /
   stuff). The brief's wording ("pick a fresh blank") implies one; the
   bible's rate is "one per returning-character entrance," and this
   episode has three returning entrances — ep 2 and ep 3 each fired it
   twice under the same rate. All three blanks are fresh (used: sun, sky,
   rain, air). If the showrunner wants only one, keep **"Same weather."**
   (it buttons Drip's own entrance line and is the funniest of the
   three); the other two cut clean.
2. **Ray catch-phrase variant:** "Look down. That is also me." (3.3) —
   a mutation of "Look up. That's me.", precedented by ep-3's sunset form
   ("That's still me"). Counted inside his 2–3 budget. Flagging because
   it varies a catch-phrase text rather than repeating it.
3. **Cloudia cameo included as OPTIONAL (2.2).** The brief allows one
   scene if rain needs a delivery joke; the treatment stages her as the
   grand courier, one scene, no arc — and marks her fully cuttable (2.3
