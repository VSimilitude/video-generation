# Drip's Big Adventure

**Series:** Little Big World (kids' educational), episode one
**Topic:** the water cycle
**Audience:** six-year-olds — and the grown-up in the room
**Target:** ~10–11 minutes of finished video
**Shape:** cold open, three acts, recap. Thirty-six scenes, one hundred and
twenty-four spoken lines.

The companion file `narration.mjs` holds the exact text sent to the
text-to-speech model. Every line below quotes it verbatim; if you change a line,
change it in both files.

## Cast and voices

| Character | Voice | Speed | Who they are |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Narrator** | `af_heart` | 1.0 | Warm storyteller. Loves Drip. Occasionally deadpans at the cast. Also cameo-voices the flower and the moose in Act Three, so the show never grows past four actors. |
| **Drip** | `af_bella` | 1.1 | Our hero. One drop of ocean water. Curious, brave, slightly dramatic. Running gag: *"I'm not small, I'm travel-sized!"* |
| **Sunny** | `am_puck` | 1.0 | The Sun. Colossal ego, catchphrase *"You're welcome!"*, laugh *"HA! HA!"*. The joke is that he is insufferable **and** completely right — he really does power the whole cycle. |
| **Cloudia** | `bf_emma` | 1.0 | Grand, theatrical manager of the Cloud Hotel. Adores her guests, despises gravity, loses her composure by inches as the hotel fills. *"We are at! Full!! Capacity!!!"* |

## The four Big Words

Each Big Word is said plainly at least twice **and** broken into syllables once
by Drip, who is extremely proud of himself every time.

| Word | Act | Line keys |
|---|---|---|
| **EVAPORATION** | One | `a1_24`, `a1_25`, `a1_26`, `a1_27`, `rc_02`, `rc_06` |
| **CONDENSATION** | Two | `a2_19`, `a2_20`, `a2_21`, `rc_03`, `rc_06` |
| **PRECIPITATION** | Three | `a3_07`, `a3_08`, `a3_09`, `rc_04`, `rc_06` |
| **COLLECTION** | Three | `a3_32`, `a3_32b`, `rc_05`, `rc_06` |

## Line-key convention

`<act>_<number>_<speaker>`, in strict playback order, matching `narration.mjs`
exactly. Act prefixes: `co` cold open, `a1` ocean, `a2` sky, `a3` the fall and
the ride home, `rc` recap.

# COLD OPEN

*Roughly thirty seconds. Hook, hero, title.*

---

### Scene 1 — Open ocean, just before dawn
**On stage:** Narrator (voice only), Drip
**Visual:** Black. One enormous slow-rolling wave fills the frame, and we push in past absurd magnification until a single glittering drop sits alone on screen — who immediately strikes a superhero pose with a cape made of foam, and holds it a beat too long.
**Lines:** `co_01_narrator`, `co_02_drip`, `co_03_narrator`, `co_04_drip`, `co_05_narrator`, `co_06_drip`

> **NARRATOR:** This is a story about a hero. A hero smaller than your pinky finger. Braver than a volcano. Wetter than a sneeze.
> **DRIP:** That's me! Hello! I'm Drip!
> **NARRATOR:** Drip is a drop of water. He is currently doing a dramatic pose.
> **DRIP:** It's my adventure pose. I have been practicing.
> **NARRATOR:** He is very small.
> **DRIP:** I am NOT small! I am travel-sized!

**Pedagogy:** Establishes the unit we will follow unbroken through the entire cycle — *one drop*. Everything after this is spent from the attention we earn here. Running gag fires for the first of four times.

---

### Scene 2 — Title card
**On stage:** Narrator
**Visual:** Camera whips up out of the water; the title splashes on in dripping letters over a sunrise horizon. Drip waves from the bottom corner, far too small to read as anything but a speck.
**Lines:** `co_07_narrator`

> **NARRATOR:** Today, Drip is going to travel all the way around the world, and come right back home. Let's go.

**Pedagogy:** States the promise the episode has to keep — a *round trip*. The word "cycle" is deliberately withheld until Drip works it out himself in Scene 30.

---

# ACT ONE — THE OCEAN

*Evaporation. Ordinary world, want, and the inciting sunbeam.*

---

### Scene 3 — The ocean, wide
**On stage:** Narrator, Drip
**Visual:** Pull back to a huge ocean, then a comic zoom onto an ocean-sized crowd of drops, every one drawn identically with the same face. A little name-arrow is the only way to find Drip.
**Lines:** `a1_01_narrator`, `a1_02_drip`, `a1_03_narrator`, `a1_04_drip`, `a1_05_narrator`

> **NARRATOR:** Our story starts in the ocean. Big. Blue. Extremely wet.
> **DRIP:** This is my home! And these are my brothers and sisters.
> **NARRATOR:** Drip has a lot of brothers and sisters.
> **DRIP:** Hi Drop. Hi Droplet. Hi Dripley. Hi other Drop. Hi Droppy. Hi Drop the third.
> **NARRATOR:** There are more of them than there are stars in the sky. Every single one looks exactly the same.

**Pedagogy:** The ocean is the reservoir — most of the world's water lives here, made of unimaginably many identical drops. Scale delivered as a sight gag.

---

### Scene 4 — Slosh
**On stage:** Drip
**Visual:** Three identical shots of the same wave sloshing, cut back to back with a "Monday / Tuesday / also Tuesday" caption gag. Drip stares into the lens.
**Lines:** `a1_06_drip`, `a1_07_drip`

> **DRIP:** We do the same thing every day. Slosh. Then slosh. Then, for a change, slosh.
> **DRIP:** I want MORE than sloshing! I want to see the sky! I want an ADVENTURE!

**Pedagogy:** The want. Ocean water sits still until something gives it energy — the story is now *waiting* for that something, which makes the Sun's arrival feel like an answer rather than a fact.

---

### Scene 5 — Sunrise
**On stage:** Narrator, Sunny
**Visual:** The horizon goes gold. Something enormous rises. It has a face. The face is extremely pleased with itself.
**Lines:** `a1_08_narrator`, `a1_09_sunny`, `a1_10_narrator`

> **NARRATOR:** And right then, something enormous came up over the water.
> **SUNNY:** GOOD MORNING, EVERYBODY!
> **NARRATOR:** This is Sunny. Sunny believes he invented mornings. He did not.

**Pedagogy:** Introduces the cycle's energy source as a *character*, so "the sun drives the water cycle" becomes something a personality does rather than a sentence to memorize.

---

### Scene 6 — Sunny, introducing himself at length
**On stage:** Sunny, Drip
**Visual:** Sunny performs a slow rotating hero shot with lens flares he has clearly added himself. Drip is a dot in the corner, squinting.
**Lines:** `a1_11_sunny`, `a1_12_drip`, `a1_13_sunny`

> **SUNNY:** I invented mornings! You're welcome! HA! HA!
> **DRIP:** Whoa. Who are you?
> **SUNNY:** I am the SUN! I am ninety three million miles away and you can STILL feel me! HA!

**Pedagogy:** Distance-and-still-warm is the whole idea of radiant energy, smuggled in as a brag.

---

### Scene 7 — The warming
**On stage:** Narrator, Sunny
**Visual:** Sunbeams pour down as thick golden ropes. A cartoon thermometer at the frame edge climbs. The blue of the water shifts warmer, degree by degree.
**Lines:** `a1_14_narrator`, `a1_15_sunny`, `a1_16_narrator`

> **NARRATOR:** Sunny has a very big ego. Sunny also has a very big job.
> **SUNNY:** Watch this. I am going to warm you up.
> **NARRATOR:** The sunshine poured down. And the water got warmer. And warmer.

**Pedagogy:** Energy in. "A very big job" is the episode quietly telling the grown-up that the joke character is also the mechanism.

---

### Scene 8 — All of it, everywhere
**On stage:** Drip, Sunny
**Visual:** Close on Drip going pink and blissful, then a whip-pan globe tour — a lake, a rice paddy, a puddle on a driveway, a dog's water bowl — each steaming gently.
**Lines:** `a1_17_drip`, `a1_18_drip`, `a1_19_sunny`

> **DRIP:** Ooh. Ooh, that is toasty. I feel tingly. I feel BOUNCY!
> **DRIP:** Sunny, are you doing this to ALL the water?
> **SUNNY:** Every ocean! Every lake! Every puddle! Every single day! HA! HA!

**Pedagogy:** Evaporation is universal and constant, not an event that happens to one special drop. The whip-pan is the argument; Sunny's brag is the caption.

---

### Scene 9 — Liftoff
**On stage:** Narrator, Drip, Sunny
**Visual:** Close on Drip vibrating faster and faster, motion arcs coming off him like a shaken jar of glitter. Then his feet peel off the water with a stretchy *thwip* and he rockets upward, cheeks flapping, hundreds of other drops rising around him like slow bubbles.
**Lines:** `a1_20_narrator`, `a1_21_drip`, `a1_22_drip`, `a1_23_sunny`

> **NARRATOR:** Here is the secret. Warm water drops wiggle. And wiggly drops get light. So light that they float right up into the air.
> **DRIP:** Wait. Wait wait wait. My feet are leaving the ocean!
> **DRIP:** Aaaah! WHOOSH! I am FLYING! This is TERRIBLE! This is WONDERFUL! It is terri-wonderful!
> **SUNNY:** You're welcome! HA! HA!

**Pedagogy:** The actual mechanism at six-year-old resolution — heat makes water move faster, fast enough to leave the surface. "Wiggle" is doing real work here, not just being cute. The scared-then-thrilled turn is the tone thesis of the whole episode: new stages are exciting, never frightening.

---

### Scene 10 — Big Word One
**On stage:** Narrator, Drip
**Visual:** Everything freezes mid-rise. **EVAPORATION** slams on in dripping capitals, then splits into syllable blocks that bounce one at a time.
**Lines:** `a1_24_narrator`, `a1_25_drip`, `a1_26_narrator`

> **NARRATOR:** That has a name, and it is a big one. When the sun warms water until it floats up into the air, that is evaporation.
> **DRIP:** Ee-vap-oh-RAY-shun!
> **NARRATOR:** Evaporation. Say it with him.

**Pedagogy:** Big Word One — twice plain, once syllable-ified. The freeze-frame is the show's Big Word signature; the same treatment lands all four times so kids learn the format and know to join in.

---

### Scene 11 — Drip is the best at it
**On stage:** Narrator, Drip
**Visual:** Drip poses again, mid-air. Pull out: he is one of roughly nine million drops rising at exactly the same speed. Push back in on his slightly deflated face.
**Lines:** `a1_27_drip`, `a1_28_narrator`, `a1_29_drip`

> **DRIP:** EE-VAP-OH-RAY-SHUN! I am doing evaporation! I am the best at it!
> **NARRATOR:** He is doing exactly as well as every other drop.
> **DRIP:** I am the best at it QUIETLY.

**Pedagogy:** Reinforcement pass on the Big Word, plus the reminder that this is happening to all the water at once. Act One ends on a laugh rather than a lesson.

---

# ACT TWO — THE SKY

*Condensation, the Cloud Hotel, and the myth of the fluffy pillow.*

---

### Scene 12 — Up through the layers
**On stage:** Narrator, Drip
**Visual:** One continuous rising shot — boats, gulls, a passenger plane, thinning blue. A side gauge shows the air getting colder as we climb. Drip hugs himself and shivers.
**Lines:** `a2_01_narrator`, `a2_02_drip`, `a2_03_narrator`, `a2_04_drip`

> **NARRATOR:** Up went Drip. Past the birds. Past the airplanes. Up where the air is thin and cold.
> **DRIP:** Ooh! Ooh! Why is it so COLD up here? I am a warm drop! I have feelings!
> **NARRATOR:** The higher you go, the colder it gets. And cold makes floating water want to be a drop again.
> **DRIP:** Ooh. I can feel myself getting droppy again.

**Pedagogy:** Altitude and temperature — the reason clouds form up *there* and not at sea level. Sets up condensation as a consequence of cold rather than a random event.

---

### Scene 13 — The Cloud Hotel
**On stage:** Narrator, Cloudia
**Visual:** Mist parts on a grand hotel doorway built into the side of a cloud — awning, brass bell, a swirl of a doorman. Cloudia sweeps into frame with a clipboard.
**Lines:** `a2_05_narrator`, `a2_06_cloudia`

> **NARRATOR:** Then, out of the mist, a door. And a sign. And a lady with a very grand voice.
> **CLOUDIA:** Welcome, darling, to the CLOUD HOTEL! I am Cloudia. I run this establishment.

**Pedagogy:** Gives the cloud a doorway, so "water drops go into clouds" becomes a place a character can be checked into.

---

### Scene 14 — Myth-bust: clouds are not pillows
**On stage:** Narrator, Cloudia, Drip
**Visual:** Drip flops backward expecting a pillow and falls straight through with a *foomp*, popping out the underside. A big red MYTH stamp thuds onto the frame and cracks.
**Lines:** `a2_07_drip`, `a2_08_cloudia`, `a2_09_narrator`

> **DRIP:** Whoa. Is this a giant fluffy pillow?
> **CLOUDIA:** A PILLOW? Darling. No. Absolutely not.
> **NARRATOR:** Big myth, busted. Clouds are not fluffy. Clouds are not soft. If you sat on one, you would go straight through.

**Pedagogy:** The single most common misconception about clouds, corrected by physical comedy rather than by contradiction.

---

### Scene 15 — What a cloud actually is
**On stage:** Cloudia, Drip
**Visual:** Cloudia gestures grandly; the camera dives into the cloud wall, which resolves into a churning swarm of individual drops, each with a tiny face.
**Lines:** `a2_10_cloudia`, `a2_11_drip`, `a2_12_cloudia`

> **CLOUDIA:** A cloud is a zillion tiny water drops, floating together. That is all! No fluff! No feathers!
> **DRIP:** So the hotel is made of the guests?
> **CLOUDIA:** Exactly, darling. You are the wallpaper. Check in, please.

**Pedagogy:** The replacement model. A cloud is not a thing that contains water; a cloud *is* water. "You are the wallpaper" is the whole idea in four words.

---

### Scene 16 — House rules: hug a speck of dust
**On stage:** Narrator, Cloudia, Drip
**Visual:** A velvet rope, and a bowl of dust specks by the door like complimentary mints. Drip picks one up and it visibly balloons him into a proper round cloud droplet.
**Lines:** `a2_13_cloudia`, `a2_14_drip`, `a2_15_narrator`, `a2_16_narrator`

> **CLOUDIA:** Now. Every guest must hug a speck of dust. House rules!
> **DRIP:** Dust? Why dust?
> **NARRATOR:** This part is true, and it is wonderful. Every drop in every cloud is hugging a tiny speck of dust or salt.
> **NARRATOR:** Without a speck to hug, the drop cannot form at all. No dust, no clouds. No clouds, no rain.

**Pedagogy:** Cloud condensation nuclei, at age level. Genuinely surprising to most adults too, and the second narrator line turns the fact into a chain of consequences rather than trivia.

---

### Scene 17 — Kevin
**On stage:** Cloudia, Drip
**Visual:** Extreme close-up on one grey speck. Drip holds it at arm's length like a newborn. Cloudia, mid-clipboard, does not look up.
**Lines:** `a2_17_drip`, `a2_18_cloudia`

> **DRIP:** Hello, dust speck. I shall call you Kevin.
> **CLOUDIA:** Kevin is a lovely name for a speck of dust, darling.

**Pedagogy:** None — this is a pure laugh, placed on purpose right after the densest fact in Act Two so the information has somewhere to land.

---

### Scene 18 — Big Word Two
**On stage:** Narrator, Drip, Cloudia
**Visual:** Freeze-frame. **CONDENSATION** lands in frosty letters that fog at the edges, then breaks into bouncing syllables.
**Lines:** `a2_19_narrator`, `a2_20_drip`, `a2_21_cloudia`

> **NARRATOR:** When cold air turns floating water back into drops, that is condensation.
> **DRIP:** Con-den-SAY-shun!
> **CLOUDIA:** Condensation, darling. It is how I get all of my guests.

**Pedagogy:** Big Word Two — twice plain, once syllable-ified. Cloudia's line ties the word back to the story engine so it is not a free-floating label.

---

### Scene 19 — The hotel fills
**On stage:** Narrator, Cloudia
**Visual:** Time-lapse. Drops stream in from below in a rising column. The hotel swells, sprouts extra floors, then extra extra floors. Cloudia's clipboard fills with check-marks.
**Lines:** `a2_22_narrator`, `a2_23_cloudia`, `a2_24_narrator`

> **NARRATOR:** And the guests kept coming.
> **CLOUDIA:** Room for one more! And one more. And, oh my.
> **NARRATOR:** More and more drops arrived. The hotel grew. The hotel got crowded.

**Pedagogy:** Cloud growth as accumulation. The visual *is* the mechanism — clouds get bigger because more water keeps arriving, one drop at a time.

---

### Scene 20 — Full capacity
**On stage:** Cloudia, Drip
**Visual:** Cloudia jammed against a window, cheek squashed, hat askew, drops packed shoulder to shoulder behind her. The light coming through the cloud dims.
**Lines:** `a2_25_cloudia`, `a2_26_drip`

> **CLOUDIA:** Please stop arriving! We are at! Full!! Capacity!!!
> **DRIP:** It is getting dark in here.

**Pedagogy:** Sets up the grey-cloud explanation in the very next beat by making the darkness something Drip notices from *inside* the cloud.

---

### Scene 21 — Grey, and heavy
**On stage:** Narrator, Sunny, Cloudia
**Visual:** Exterior. The cloud turns from white to charcoal as we watch. Sunny's beams hit the top and stop dead. The whole hotel sags, corners drooping.
**Lines:** `a2_27_narrator`, `a2_28_sunny`, `a2_29_cloudia`, `a2_30_narrator`

> **NARRATOR:** A crowded cloud turns grey. That is not magic. That is just too many drops for the sunlight to get through.
> **SUNNY:** Rude. I was shining SO nicely.
> **CLOUDIA:** The hotel is heavy, darling. Very heavy. I love my guests. I hate gravity.
> **NARRATOR:** And a heavy hotel cannot stay in the sky.

**Pedagogy:** Two ideas in one beat — why storm clouds look dark (light blocked, not colour changed), and why rain has to happen (mass beats buoyancy). The act ends on a physical inevitability, which is what makes Act Three feel earned rather than scheduled.

---

# ACT THREE — THE FALL AND THE RIDE HOME

*Precipitation, collection, and the twist.*

---

### Scene 22 — Check-out
**On stage:** Cloudia, Drip
**Visual:** Cloudia hauls a lever marked CHECK OUT. The floor of the hotel simply ceases to exist. Drip stands on nothing for one long, wide-eyed beat.
**Lines:** `a3_01_cloudia`, `a3_02_drip`, `a3_03_cloudia`, `a3_04_drip`

> **CLOUDIA:** Everybody OUT! It is check-out time!
> **DRIP:** Wait! How do I get down the stairs?
> **CLOUDIA:** Darling. There are no stairs.
> **DRIP:** Ohhhh nooooo. WHEEE!

**Pedagogy:** The transition into precipitation, played entirely as comedy so the fall never reads as danger.

---

### Scene 23 — The best waterslide ever, and Big Word Three
**On stage:** Narrator, Drip
**Visual:** A joyful plummet — Drip tumbling through streaked rain, arms out, doing loop-the-loops no real raindrop would attempt. Freeze mid-loop for **PRECIPITATION**, with rain, snow, sleet and hail icons orbiting the word.
**Lines:** `a3_05_narrator`, `a3_06_drip`, `a3_07_narrator`, `a3_08_drip`, `a3_09_narrator`

> **NARRATOR:** Down he went. The best waterslide ever built, and it goes all the way from the sky to the ground.
> **DRIP:** This is the greatest thing I have EVER done!
> **NARRATOR:** When drops fall out of a cloud, that is precipitation. Rain, snow, sleet, hail. All of it is precipitation.
> **DRIP:** Pre-sip-ih-TAY-shun! WHEEE!
> **NARRATOR:** Precipitation. The falling part.

**Pedagogy:** Big Word Three — twice plain, once syllable-ified — plus the useful extension that snow and hail are the same step of the cycle wearing different coats.

---

### Scene 24 — Landing
**On stage:** Narrator, Drip
**Visual:** *Sploosh.* Drip lands on a mossy mountain rock and plants a tiny flag. On the narrator's winter line the shot cross-fades to the same peak under snow, then back. Drip is still saluting.
**Lines:** `a3_10_narrator`, `a3_11_drip`, `a3_12_narrator`, `a3_13_drip`

> **NARRATOR:** Drip landed high on a mountain. Sploosh.
> **DRIP:** I have landed! I am a mountain drop now! I live here!
> **NARRATOR:** In winter, drops like Drip land as snow instead, and wait on the mountain until spring.
> **DRIP:** I want to try that next time. I would look GREAT as a snowflake.

**Pedagogy:** Precipitation lands on land, not only on sea — the step kids most often skip, and the one that makes rivers possible. The snow aside quietly introduces storage: water can pause in the cycle without leaving it.

---

### Scene 25 — Downhill
**On stage:** Narrator, Drip
**Visual:** Drip slides sideways off his rock mid-salute. A trickle joins a trickle joins a stream; the camera pulls back in stages until we are flying over a wide river. Every merge adds a swarm of waving drop-faces.
**Lines:** `a3_14_drip`, `a3_15_narrator`, `a3_16_drip`, `a3_17_narrator`, `a3_18_drip`

> **DRIP:** Hey. Why am I moving?
> **NARRATOR:** Because water always rolls downhill. Always. Every time.
> **DRIP:** Downhill it is!
> **NARRATOR:** A trickle became a stream. The stream met another stream. And the streams became a river.
> **DRIP:** There are so many of us! Hi! Hi! Hi! Where are we all going?

**Pedagogy:** Gravity plus merging equals rivers. This is the collection idea being built in front of the viewer several beats before it is given its name.

---

### Scene 26 — The flower
**On stage:** Narrator (cameo as flower), Drip
**Visual:** A daisy leans over the riverbank, roots glowing as they drink. Drip, mid-current, loses a small piece of himself and looks personally wronged.
**Lines:** `a3_19_narrator`, `a3_20_narrator`, `a3_21_drip`

> **NARRATOR:** Along the way, the river had visitors.
> **NARRATOR:** A flower leaned over and drank a bit of him. Slurp.
> **DRIP:** Excuse me! That was my elbow!

**Pedagogy:** Living things take water out of the cycle and put it back — first stop on the "who uses this water" montage.

---

### Scene 27 — The moose
**On stage:** Narrator (cameo as moose), Drip
**Visual:** An enormous moose plants itself mid-river, blinks slowly, and does not move for the entire scene. The river parts around its legs. Drip bounces off a knee, then leads a long detour around it.
**Lines:** `a3_22_narrator`, `a3_23_narrator`, `a3_24_drip`, `a3_25_narrator`, `a3_26_drip`, `a3_27_narrator`

> **NARRATOR:** Then a moose walked in and stood right in the middle of the river.
> **NARRATOR:** The moose said, and I am quoting exactly, mmm. Nice water. I live here now.
> **DRIP:** Sir! SIR! You are standing on my family!
> **NARRATOR:** The moose did not move. Moose rarely do.
> **DRIP:** Fine. FINE! We will go AROUND the moose.
> **NARRATOR:** They went around the moose.

**Pedagogy:** Second example of a living thing using the river, and a real property of water smuggled into a gag — water does not stop at an obstacle, it goes around it. Narrator cameo keeps the cast at four.

---

### Scene 28 — Everybody wants a bit
**On stage:** Narrator, Drip
**Visual:** Fast montage off the river — an irrigation channel, a water tower, a tap, a kettle, a bathtub with a rubber duck. Each shot lifts a few glowing drops out of the flow.
**Lines:** `a3_28_narrator`, `a3_29_drip`

> **NARRATOR:** Farmers took some for their fields. A town took some for its taps. Somebody's bath. Somebody's teeth.
> **DRIP:** Everybody wants a bit of me! I am very popular!

**Pedagogy:** Connects the cycle to the viewer's own tap and bathtub — the bridge that makes the dinosaur-puddle fact land in the recap.

---

### Scene 29 — Home, and Big Word Four
**On stage:** Narrator, Drip
**Visual:** The river rounds a headland and the ocean opens up, enormous, in one unbroken shot. Freeze for **COLLECTION**. Then Drip is mobbed by identical siblings from Scene 3.
**Lines:** `a3_30_narrator`, `a3_31_drip`, `a3_32_narrator`, `a3_32b_drip`, `a3_33_drip`

> **NARRATOR:** Then the river turned a corner. And the water got wide, and blue, and salty, and enormous.
> **DRIP:** I know this place.
> **NARRATOR:** When all the water gathers back into the streams, the rivers and the ocean, that is collection. The gathering-up part.
> **DRIP:** Coll-ECK-shun! I have been collected!
> **DRIP:** Hi Drop! Hi Droplet! Hi Dripley! I am BACK!

**Pedagogy:** Big Word Four, plain plus syllables, spoken over its own visual proof. The sibling callback pays off Scene 3 and confirms the round trip physically, before anybody says the word "cycle".

---

### Scene 30 — The twist
**On stage:** Narrator, Drip
**Visual:** Drip's journey redraws itself as a glowing line over a world map — up, across, down, along — and the two ends snap together into a ring. Drip's jaw drops.
**Lines:** `a3_34_drip`, `a3_35_drip`, `a3_36_narrator`, `a3_37_drip`

> **DRIP:** Wait. Wait a minute. I went UP. I went across the sky. I went down a mountain.
> **DRIP:** And I came all the way home. I ended up exactly where I started!
> **NARRATOR:** You did. That is the twist, Drip. It is not a line. It is a circle.
> **DRIP:** A circle. A CYCLE! THE WATER CYCLE!

**Pedagogy:** The episode's thesis, discovered by the character instead of announced by the narrator. Home was both the start and the end of the adventure — which is the definition of a cycle, arrived at as a story beat.

---

### Scene 31 — Credit where it is due
**On stage:** Narrator, Sunny
**Visual:** Sunny slides into frame at maximum brightness, arms wide, over the completed ring. The ring begins to rotate, visibly driven by his beams.
**Lines:** `a3_38_sunny`, `a3_39_narrator`, `a3_40_narrator`

> **SUNNY:** AND I POWER THE WHOLE THING! You're welcome! HA! HA!
> **NARRATOR:** This time, annoyingly, he is completely right.
> **NARRATOR:** And it never stops. Not once. Not ever.

**Pedagogy:** Closes the loop on the Sun as the cycle's energy source, and states the key property — the cycle is continuous. "Annoyingly" is the grown-up's payoff for two acts of Sunny.

---

### Scene 32 — Round again
**On stage:** Drip, Sunny
**Visual:** Drip hops up and down on the ocean surface. Sunny leans in. Drip starts rising mid-sentence, cheeks already flapping.
**Lines:** `a3_41_drip`, `a3_42_sunny`, `a3_43_drip`, `a3_44_sunny`

> **DRIP:** So I get to do it all AGAIN? Sunny! Warm me up! I am ready!
> **SUNNY:** Say the magic words.
> **DRIP:** You're welcome?
> **SUNNY:** HA! HA! Good drop.

**Pedagogy:** The cycle restarting, shown rather than stated — and the catchphrase gag resolving into an actual friendship.

---

# RECAP

*Roughly forty-five seconds. Chant, mind-blower, tease.*

### Scene 33 — The chant

## Production notes

**Tone guardrails.** No sarcasm a six-year-old cannot parse — the narrator's deadpans are always *about* a character's obvious behaviour (Sunny bragging, Drip posing), never an ironic reversal. No scary peril: the fall in Act Three is framed as a waterslide from its first frame. No potty humour.

**Line length.** Almost every line sits between five and fifteen words, and nothing in the script exceeds twenty-five. The energy comes from ping-pong, not from paragraphs. Longest lines in the script are `a1_24_narrator` and `a3_32_narrator`, both Big Word deliveries, both allowed to breathe.

**Running gags, and where they fire.**
- *"I'm not small, I'm travel-sized!"* — `co_06`, `rc_14`, plus the visual gag of Drip as an unreadable speck in Scenes 2, 11 and 36.
- *"You're welcome!"* — `a1_11`, `a1_23`, `a3_38`, resolved at `a3_43` when Drip finally says it back.
- *"HA! HA!"* — `a1_09`, `a1_11`, `a1_13`, `a1_19`, `a3_38`, `a3_44`, `rc_12`.
- *"Darling"* — every Cloudia line except the panicked ones, which is exactly what makes dropping it in `a2_25` read as a loss of composure.
- **Sunny is always right.** `a1_14` ("a very big job"), `a1_19`, `a3_39` ("annoyingly, he is completely right"). The running joke and the pedagogy are the same joke.

**Big Word signature.** Big Words always arrive on a hard freeze, slam in as capitals, then split into bouncing syllable blocks as Drip pronounces them. Identical treatment all four times, so a child learns to expect the prompt and shout along.

**Sound-word spellings** are locked to the exact strings in `narration.mjs` — WHOOSH, WHEEE, Sploosh, Slurp, Aaaah, and the hyphenated syllable-words. Do not "correct" them; they are shaped for the text-to-speech model, not for a copy editor.

**Audition list.** Before building any visuals, listen to `a1_22_drip` ("Aaaah! WHOOSH!"), `a3_04_drip` and `a3_08_drip` (both "WHEEE!"), and `a2_25_cloudia` (the triple-exclamation capacity line). Long vowel runs and stacked punctuation are the likeliest things here to need a respell.
