# Ep 3 comedy rewrite — design brief (showrunner, 2026-08-02)

## The mandate (Mike, verbatim, after the family screening)

> "I don't think we accomplished what we wanted with the rewrite. We didn't
> change much to be honest. It's still quite dry most of the episode. The
> colors need to drive the humor much more. Give them more personality, let
> them shine. Make room for them to have fun interactions (even if it
> stretches the episode longer). Rewrite as much as necessary to get some real
> humor and fun in the episode from the moment the colors are introduced to
> the end. Even the race felt very dry, they barely interacted, and we zoomed
> through the whole thing."

This supersedes the runtime flag (R2) and the reply-free roll-call ruling
(R1 — which was always "Mike may override at screening"; he just did, in
substance). It does NOT supersede: the cast (voices/emotions per color are
FINAL), the pedagogy, the physics-honesty rules, Violet's silence, Sunny's
no-verdict arc, or the series canon (volcano rule, banked ep-4 material).

## Diagnosis — why the delivered episode is dry

1. **The ensemble doesn't speak.** ~15 color lines in 13.5 minutes (Red 5,
   Blue 4, Indigo 2, Yellow 2, Orange 1, Green 1, Violet 0). The colors were
   cast as an ensemble and written as a diagram with faces.
2. **Colors never talk to each other.** One color-to-color address in the
   whole episode (Yellow → Violet, `a3_13d`). Every other color line is a
   physics confirmation aimed at the Narrator. No conversations = no
   interactions = no fun, definitionally.
3. **Nobody wants anything.** Six of seven temperaments are kinds of
   NON-reaction. Comedy is characters wanting things and colliding; this cast
   was engineered for stillness. The personalities exist as motion laws
   (frequency, gaps, speeds) — legible on a paused frame, invisible to a
   six-year-old's funny bone.
4. **The stillness religion starved the middle.** 42 held beats marked
   "nothing enters this beat." Individually correct; collectively the episode
   is a sequence of reverent silences. Deadpan only lands against liveliness,
   and there is not enough liveliness to push against.
5. **The race is not a race.** No start, no stakes, no rivalry, no banter, no
   one who wants to win. Exits are single polite lines. ~90 seconds for what
   was sold as the ensemble comedy centerpiece.

Supporting evidence with timings: `scratchpad/ep3_comedy_audit.md` (measured
laugh-gap map, per-color line inventory, interaction map, race timing).

## Design pillars for the rewrite

### P1 — Every color gets a WANT (the comedy engine)

The signature moves stay (they're built and they're good); each now expresses
a want that can collide with the others':

- **Red** wants *peace and quiet*. Not stoic — actively, quietly invested in
  nothing happening. Other colors' chaos is a thing he endures. His deadpan
  now has a target.
- **Orange** wants *to be Red*. Not just agreement — devotion. Pre-agrees,
  quotes him, defends his silences ("He meant that."). "What Red said." is a
  personality, not a line; it escalates.
- **Yellow** wants *everyone to have a wonderful time*, including people
  actively leaving, losing, or ignoring her. Cheers for both sides. The
  only one who sees Violet.
- **Green** wants *to sit down*. Everywhere is potentially a nice spot. Has
  opinions ONLY about spots. Can be lured or stalled by anything sittable.
- **Blue** wants *to be everywhere first and to be liked by everything he
  crashes into*. Apologizes mid-collision, introduces himself to things.
- **Indigo** wants *to be Blue*. Arrives after the joke, claims Blue's lines
  as his own, is never acknowledged as a separate person by anyone but Blue.
- **Violet** wants *to be noticed* and NEVER speaks and NEVER is (until the
  series decides otherwise). His want is staged, never voiced. Unchanged.

Emotion fields stay one-per-color (that rule held and it's the voice
identity). Wants must be expressible inside each color's single emotion.

### P2 — The colors talk TO EACH OTHER

Recurring two-hander engines, usable in any scene the colors are on stage:

- **Blue ↔ Red**: maximum-speed meets immovable. Blue orbits Red trying to
  get him excited; Red answers in the fewest possible words, always after
  his 16f pause, never what Blue hoped.
- **Orange ↔ Red**: devotion bits. Orange translating Red's silences,
  agreeing before Red finishes, being one body-length behind even in
  conversation.
- **Indigo ↔ Blue**: the echo who thinks he's a duet. Blue is the only one
  who ever answers Indigo, and only to say some form of "I just said that."
- **Yellow ↔ Violet**: the one-way friendship. She narrates his achievements
  to people who don't look. He never responds. She is not discouraged.
- **Green ↔ the plot**: sits at load-bearing moments; has to be collected.
- **The seven ↔ Ray**: Ray is their earnest literal-minded... self. They
  treat him variously as boss/sibling/stranger, which nobody resolves.

The **roll call (Scene 10) gets replies** — in character, escalating, and the
button still lands flat ("I have never met me before."). The series shape
(name → flat narrator line → unbothered button) survives; the middle gets
funnier. This is the R1 override Mike's feedback authorizes.

### P3 — The race becomes an actual RACE

The physics is untouched (exit order = spectrum order, exits bounce UP,
nothing is taken away, one-eye beat sacred, Green's mid-spectrum line kept in
substance). Everything around the physics changes:

- **A start line and stakes.** The colors KNOW it's a race and care about
  winning (except Red, who is just walking home). Blue declares he will
  obviously win. That declaration is the setup for the entire act.
- **Trash talk / banter at the start and during legs.** Interaction density
  goes UP as the field shrinks — the whole middle of the race is two-handers
  (Blue taunting Red as he ricochets past him backwards; Yellow cheering
  every single exit as an achievement; Orange giving Red a play-by-play Red
  didn't ask for; Indigo echoing Blue's taunts to a Red who has already been
  taunted).
- **Exits get SCENES, not lines.** Each departure is a beat with at least one
  other color reacting to it. Blue's exit is the biggest: the boy who said
  he'd win, bounced out FIRST, in maximum denial ("I am WINNING! I am winning
  UPWARDS!").
- **Red doesn't know it's a race / doesn't care — and wins.** The finish is
  Orange conceding the only thing he'll ever claim for himself (arriving
  second and thrilled about it), and Red's "Peace and quiet." lands as the
  punchline of a 3-minute structure instead of a mood.
- **Slow it down.** The race should breathe — more legs or longer legs,
  Mike explicitly authorized the runtime. Target feel: the centerpiece of
  act 3, not a montage.
- Consider a **commentary layer** (Narrator drops into deadpan sports
  commentary; Sunny provides self-crediting color commentary from the
  horizon). Test in treatment; kill if it crowds the colors.

### P4 — Room, and where the stillness survives

Runtime ceiling is lifted (Mike, explicitly). Add lines, beats, and scenes
where the comedy needs them. The held-beat architecture is no longer
sacrosanct BUT these holds stay empty because they're the episode's spine:
crayon choice (s1), the seven-reveal count beat (s9, 60f), "He has a point."
(s23), the black lunar sky (s34, 60f), the world turning (s31, 75f), the
volcano's one eye (s28b, 45f). Everything else is negotiable — ensemble
business is now ALLOWED in ordinary beats, and many "nothing enters" notes
should be deleted where they serve restraint for its own sake.

### P5 — What must not change

- Pedagogy beats and the physics-honesty rules (no "smaller", no "never
  bounces", nothing taken away, honest violet answer, Moon as control).
- Sunny's arc: no verdict this episode; "He has a point." stays; ep-4 bank
  (wrongness ceremony, "That is not me.") untouched.
- Violet NEVER speaks. His gag chain can grow but never with a line.
- The volcano rule (scenery, no line about it, one eye in 28b, stir in 35).
- Cast/voices/pitch/speed/emotion-per-color. New lines use existing casting.
- Series signatures: roll-call SHAPE (button still flat), "You're welcome!"
  ceiling at seven, sameAs discipline for repetition gags.
- The cold open / title / Scenes 1–8 (pre-colors) are OUT OF SCOPE — Mike's
  mandate starts "from the moment the colors are introduced."

### P6 — Density target

From Scene 9 to the end: a kid-graded laugh candidate every 25–30s, max gap
≤50s, measured against the audit's map. Any stretch carried by diagram or
narrator exposition gets a character beat inside it — colors are now allowed
to be present (and funny) in mechanism scenes they were absent from (15, 17,
20, 22, 27 are the big empty ones; the audit has the full list).

## Process

1. TWO story-writers draft competing TREATMENTS in parallel (beat-level,
   scene by scene from Scene 9 → end; every new/changed line sketched, every
   new scene outlined, laugh-density self-scored). Different angles:
   - Writer A: "sitcom ensemble" — maximize two-hander engines throughout
     acts 1–2, race as act-3 payoff of established rivalries.
   - Writer B: "the race is the episode" — build act 3 into a 3.5–4.5 min
     centerpiece and seed acts 1–2 with exactly the setups it pays off.
2. Showrunner synthesizes (pick a spine, graft the other's best beats).
3. One story-writer produces the full dialogue draft (script.md-format
   deltas: full text of every new/changed line with keys, engine fields,
   speeds, gaps; scene-level staging notes at script altitude, not build
   altitude).
4. Showrunner edit pass → revision doc to Mike for sign-off. NO
   implementation until he approves.
