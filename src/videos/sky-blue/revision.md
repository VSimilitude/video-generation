# Ray and the Sky Nobody Painted — major revision

**Status: PROPOSAL, 2026-07-31. Text only.** Nothing here is wired into
`narration.mjs`, `Video.tsx` or any scene file, and no clip has been
synthesized. `script.md` remains the source of truth for the delivered cut
(12:17.9) until this document is approved and applied; where the two disagree,
`script.md` wins until then and this file wins after.

**Trigger:** Mike's solo watch of the finished cut — *"needs a full review and
some significant revision, some rewrites"* — plus six binding notes, reproduced
in the orchestrator's brief and answered scene by scene below.

**What this is.** The biggest rewrite the series has run. It changes the
episode's comedy engine (the seven colours become an ensemble with real
personalities, two of them cast), rebuilds five scenes, cuts one, adds one, and
— the largest thing in it — **moves the series' wrongness ceremony out of this
episode and into episode four**. It also specifies a one-line change to a
shipped episode (`wind`'s `rc_15_narrator`).

**What it does not change.** Every pedagogy beat, every stated fact, the crayon
frame story at both ends, the grey-drain, the split reveal's counting beat, the
homework beat, the world-turn ending and its `sameAs` greeting, all three Big
Word cards, the Moon control experiment, and the tone guardrails. Nine of the
thirty-five scenes are untouched and are marked KEEP.

**The title survives, and gets sharper.** *Ray and the Sky Nobody Painted* was
already naming the promise rather than the mechanism. Under the new Act Two
ending it becomes the setup for the episode's best line: Sunny keeps the paint
and loses the painting, and nobody painted the sky, which is what the card said
in the first forty seconds.

---

# 1. Treatment

## The problem the revision solves

The delivered cut is a well-paced episode whose funniest character is a Sun who
appears in six scenes, and whose largest asset — seven colour blobs with faces,
present for eight of the twelve minutes — is used for three sight gags and
otherwise treated as diagram elements. The mechanism (blue bounces, red does
not) is *stated by a narrator over drawn geometry* and *illustrated* by the
colours. The revision inverts that: the mechanism is **performed by the
colours**, because their temperaments *are* the physics.

That is the same move episode two's punch-up made with Cloudia (her hotel being
full *is* condensation) and the same rule STYLE.md now carries: *put the funny
characters inside the mechanism scenes and let them deliver the facts.* Here it
is not a punch-up item. It is the episode's spine.

## The new shape, act by act

**Cold open (unchanged).** Crayon, question, Sunny's theory. `co_09_narrator`
still says *"Keep hold of it. We need it later."* and the promise is still kept
— just not the way two episodes have trained the audience to expect.

**Act One — the ensemble is born.** Ray still splits into seven at the raindrop
and the 60f counting beat is still sacred and still empty. What changes is what
happens *after* it: the seven do not stand in an arc waiting to be named, they
come alive in character, left to right, one per beat, and they never stop being
those characters for the rest of the episode. The roll call (the series
signature, and the biggest laugh in the cut) now plays against seven reactions
instead of seven bobs, and its best beat is a colour who does not react at all.
The RAINBOW card is already staged as a place characters sit; now they sit on it
in character.

**Act Two — the double act.** Two of the seven turn out to have voices, and they
are the two the mechanism is about. **Red** is the calm end of the spectrum and
he walks the corridor of air without deviating by a pixel, because he has never
hurried in his life. **Blue** is the jittery end and he ricochets off everything
in the frame while apologising to it. Their temperaments are not decoration on
the physics; they *are* the physics, and a six-year-old who has met them cannot
un-know why the sky is blue. Everything else in Act Two survives: both
myth-busts (one of them rebuilt), Puff, the SCATTER card, the interlock line,
and the violet honesty tax with "Sorry, Violet." on the end of it.

**Act Two's ending changes.** Sunny is no longer declared wrong. The mechanism
plays out, he claims total victory, and the Narrator's concession is precise
about what he actually owns: the light is his, the painting is the air's. He
ends the episode undefeated, insufferable, and — the point — with the audience
knowing exactly which half he is going to remember.

**Act Three — Red's act.** The volcano's one line and its 60f hold are gone; it
returns to being scenery on the horizon that nobody mentions, which is what made
it work in episode two. The two back-to-back cross-section diagrams now have
Red and Blue standing in them. The goodbye roll call survives intact. And the
sunset gets what it has never had: **a scene of its own, belonging to the
character whose calm is the reason it exists.** Red walks out of the end of two
hundred miles of air into an empty orange sky, having outlasted everybody, and
says two short things about it.

**Recap — the ensemble takes a bow.** The three-panel chant is unchanged in
shape and each Big Word is still attached to the character who embodied it — and
now two of the panels get argued with. Blue claims SCATTER off Puff (it takes
both of them, which is the lesson); Red flatly and correctly informs Sunny that
the sunset is mostly him. The Moon is untouched. The tease is rebuilt: the
volcano stirs, Sunny reflexively claims it, and the Narrator declines to have an
opinion.

## What the revision is betting on

That **temperament is the cheapest pedagogy in the show.** "Blue is jumpy" is a
sentence a child forgets. A small blue character who cannot stand still, who
apologises to the air he keeps hitting, and who ends up in every direction at
once, is a thing a child *has met*. The bet is the same one the roll call won
twice: the joke and the lesson are the same object.

# 2. The seven — the ensemble sheet

Physics-true, in spectrum order. Scattering rises steeply toward the violet end,
so the temperament spectrum runs the same way: **jittery at the violet end, calm
at the red end.** Nobody is ever described as *smaller* (Physics honesty,
unchanged) — the whole spectrum is a spectrum of *behaviour*, which is what the
staging can show and what a six-year-old can keep.

| Colour | Voice | Temperament, in one line | Physical signature (must be legible in a paused frame) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Red** | **CAST — MiniMax** | Big, slow, deep and entirely unbothered; has never hurried in his life and does not intend to start. | Walks. Always the same speed, always a dead-straight line, never reacts to anything crossing his path. |
| **Orange** | silent | Red's second. Agrees with Red about everything, including the pace. | Matches Red's stride exactly, one body-length behind, and never overtakes him. |
| **Yellow** | silent | Cheerful about literally everything, including bad news. | Waves. At everyone, continuously, including at things that are leaving. |
| **Green** | silent | The mellow one. Content. Has no notes. | Sits down the instant anything on screen stops moving. |
| **Blue** | **CAST — MiniMax** | Fast, eager, permanently over-caffeinated, interrupts himself, apologises to everything he hits. | Ricochets. Never travels more than half a frame without changing direction. |
| **Indigo** | silent | Blue's shadow. Does everything Blue does, slightly worse, half a beat late. | Copies Blue's last move on a delay, and arrives after the joke. |
| **Violet** | silent, **and never speaks** | Works harder than anybody on screen and is never once looked at. | Vibrates so hard his own outline blurs — the fastest object in any frame he is in. |

**The two poles are the mechanism.** Blue's jitteriness *is* why the sky is
blue: he cannot cross a room without hitting something, so he ends up going in
every direction, so he arrives at your eye from all of it. Red's calm *is* why
the sunset is red: he plows straight through two hundred miles of air without
deviating, so at the end of the longest trip of the day he is the one still
walking. Neither fact is ever stated as a property. Both are staged as a
temperament and then named by the Narrator in the lines that already exist
(`a2_23`, `a2_25`).

**Violet's gag deepens rather than repeats.** Under the delivered cut he is the
one nobody notices. Under the revision, *two of his six siblings turn out to
have speaking parts* and he still does not get a word. He out-bounces the entire
dome shot in Scene 20 and Blue — who bounces less than he does — has three lines.
That is a better version of the same joke and it costs nothing, because the rule
that protects it is unchanged: **he never gets a line, ever.** The moment he
speaks he is an eighth voice and the joke stops being that nobody notices him.

**The hard staging condition, now binding on all seven.** `script.md` currently
requires Violet to be the *same blob every time* (same seventh colour, same
`SHARD_PHASE[6]`, same silhouette) or he is four different accidents. **That rule
now applies to each of the seven.** Each colour keeps one phase index for the
whole episode and one signature move. Seven bodies that bob identically are a
diagram; seven bodies that each move wrongly in their own way are a cast, and the
difference is a lookup table, not new art.

**Five silent, two cast — and that is the whole cast growth.** Voice count goes
from five to seven. STYLE.md's rule is *a body with a face and a line gets its
own voice*, and *voice count is not a budget worth defending* — but the corollary
is that the other five must stay wordless, and they do. Every joke Orange,
Yellow, Green, Indigo and Violet make in this document is a movement.

## Red — candidates

Red needs *slow, low, and genuinely unbothered*, which is a narrower ask than it
sounds: the failure mode is not "too deep", it is **sleepy**. A Red who sounds
tired makes the sunset scene read as the light dying, which is the one thing the
tone guardrail forbids. He must sound like somebody who is not hurrying because
he has decided not to.

Two names are excluded up front. **`Deep_Voice_Man` is the rock** (`wind`, ep 2)
and reusing it here would make Red sound like a returning bit-part. **`Elegant_Man`
at pitch −6 is reserved for the volcano** and must not be spent on a supporting
colour in the episode that teases it.

| Candidate | Why | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **`Casual_Guy`** | The description is the character — unbothered, unhurried, no performance in it. Top pick on paper. | Casual can arrive as *flippant*; Red is serene, not sarcastic. |
| **`Determined_Man`** | Already auditioned in this repo (the Sunny round, `public/narration/auditions-sunny/`) and rejected **for Sunny** on the grounds of sounding too settled and authoritative — which is exactly Red. A rejection for one part is data for another. | May carry weight Red does not need; listen for effort. |
| **`Patient_Man`** | Proven on this pipeline, and *patient* is the note. | It is ep 2's **beetle**. Different episode, tiny part, but a returning ear may hear it. Rank third for that reason alone. |
| **`Friendly_Person`** | The warm-and-slow option, and the safest against "sleepy". | May be too light to read as the *big* one. |

Audition on the two lines that ask opposite things (see §9 for the full ear-check
list):

```
npm run narration -- --audition sky-blue:a3_18d_red <dir> \
  --engine minimax --voices Casual_Guy,Determined_Man,Patient_Man,Friendly_Person \
  --emotion calm --speed 0.85
```

`a3_18d_red` ("Peace and quiet.") is the hardest thing he does and the one the
sunset scene stands on. Then `a2_23b_red` ("I go straight through. Always have.")
for the flat brag. **Also render each candidate at `--speed 0.9`**: 0.85 on a
MiniMax character line is untested in this repo and 0.9 is the fallback.

## Blue — candidates

Blue needs *fast, high-energy, eager and slightly breathless*. The hard
constraint is **timbre separation from Ray**, and it is sharper here than
anywhere else in the series, because Blue is a *piece of Ray* and the two of them
share scenes. A voice that sounds like Ray makes the split reveal's whole point
("every single one of them was also Ray") arrive as a technical confusion instead
of a joke.

| Candidate | Why | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **`Sweet_Girl_2`** | Maximum separation from Ray, Sunny and Red in one move; a light fast read carries the jitter without shouting. On Ray's original audition list, so its behaviour on this pipeline is partly known. | Third girl in the cast, after Drip and Puff. Check her against `Lively_Girl` back to back. |
| **`Lovely_Girl`** | Same argument, warmer. Also from Ray's audition list. | Warmth is not the note; Blue is not comforting anybody. |
| **`Friendly_Person`** | Neutral-bright, and the best chance of a Blue who does not read as either a boy or a girl — which suits a colour. | May not go fast enough to be funny. |
| **`Decent_Boy`** | Ray's own runner-up, and a family resemblance to Ray is arguably *true* — Blue is literally part of him. | **The declared risk.** The ep-3 casting note is explicit that timbre separation from adjacent speakers is what got Ray his part. Audition it, but audition it *against Ray's clips*, not on its own. |

```
npm run narration -- --audition sky-blue:a2_25b_blue <dir> \
  --engine minimax --voices Sweet_Girl_2,Lovely_Girl,Friendly_Person,Decent_Boy \
  --emotion happy --speed 1.05
```

**`speed: 1.05` is a first for the suite.** No line in three episodes has ever
run above 1.0, and this one does because *the character is faster than everybody
else* — that is the physics and the joke in one field. Ear-check it at 1.0, 1.05
and 1.1 in the audition round; if the model chipmunks or slurs at 1.05, take 1.0
and put the speed in the ricochet path instead, where it is free.

## Casting hygiene, both parts

- **One constant each**, exactly as `RAY_MINIMAX_VOICE` and ep 2's `PUFF_ENGINE`:
  `RED_ENGINE` / `RED_MINIMAX_VOICE` and `BLUE_ENGINE` / `BLUE_MINIMAX_VOICE`,
  with a kokoro stand-in behind each so a recast is a free re-time.
- **Sweep the sound words if either is recast.** Blue's lines carry repeated
  exclamations ("Sorry! Sorry!") and MiniMax reads repetition differently from
  kokoro. Fallbacks are pre-written in §8.
- **Red carries exactly one `emotion` in the whole episode (`calm`) and never
  changes it.** That is not laziness, it is the character; a Red who plays a
  reaction has stopped being Red.

# 4. The series arc change — Sunny is not declared wrong

This is the largest item in the document and it reaches outside this episode.

## What changes and why

**Removed from ep 3:** the verdict (`a2_49_narrator`, *"He is wrong."*), the
ceremony that carried it (the diagram stopping dead, the beams drooping, the grin
coming apart across a 36f beat), Sunny's doubt (`a2_50_sunny`, *"Wrong. Me. I
have never been wrong."*), the flat denial (`a2_51_narrator`, *"There is no
paint. There never was."*), the two-verdict summary (`a2_54_narrator`), the
Scene 16 foreshadow (*"We will come back to Sunny."*), and the inversion at the
very end (`rc_18_sunny`, *"That is not me."*).

**Two reasons, and the second is the real one.** First, the delivered cut gives
the verdict away in the cold open and then spends eleven minutes walking toward
a beat the audience has been told is coming — it lands as a scheduled event.
Second, and this is the one that decides it: **it is not true enough.** In every
sense the show is willing to defend, Sunny *did* provide the paint. The blue is
made of his light and nothing else. Telling a six-year-old "he is wrong" and then
immediately conceding "he is right about the light" is a script arguing with
itself for a punchline, and the punchline was already available without the
argument.

**What replaces it:** a precise allocation of credit, which is funnier, truer,
and leaves the character undefeated.

> **NARRATOR:** I checked. Then I checked again.
> *(45f — the beat two episodes have trained the audience to read as a verdict.)*
> **NARRATOR:** He has a point.

Two episodes have taught the audience to expect *"He is right. Again."* This
episode's cold open has taught them to expect *"He is wrong."* They get a third
thing, and the third thing is a joke rather than a ruling.

**And the biggest grown-up laugh in the episode survives verbatim.**
`a2_55_narrator` — *"He will only remember one of those."* — needed a two-item
list in front of it, and it still has one:

> **NARRATOR:** It is his light. It is not his painting.
> *(20f)*
> **NARRATOR:** He will only remember one of those.

Same beat, same length, same laugh, no verdict anywhere in it. And it is now the
title paying itself off out loud: *nobody painted the sky*, and the last word on
it is who did not.

## What is banked for episode four

- **"That is not me."** The inversion of the show's oldest running gag is not
  spent here. It moves to ep 4, where it can land *after* the wrongness ceremony
  rather than instead of it: the volcano claim, the correction, and then Sunny
  declining a claim for the first time in four episodes.
- **The ceremony itself** — *"I did that." / "No. He really didn't."* — is ep 4's,
  as instructed, and it will be about a thing Sunny genuinely had nothing to do
  with, which is what makes a verdict payable.
- **Ep 2's mid-episode fuse stays lit.** *"One day Sunny will be wrong about
  something. It is not today."* (`wind`'s `a2_45`/`a2_45b`, now two clips with 36
  frames between them) is **not touched**. It was a long fuse and it is now a
  longer one. A returning viewer will hear the shape of the beat in Scene 23, get
  a different word, and still be waiting — which is a better use of a plant than
  collecting it on schedule.

## The ep-2 ending tweak — one line

`src/videos/wind/narration.mjs`, `rc_15_narrator`. Currently:

```js
rc_15_narrator: {
  text: "Sunny has a theory. It is wrong.",
  ...NARRATOR,
  speed: 0.9,
},
```

**Change to:**

```js
// Ep 3 no longer declares the theory wrong — the wrongness ceremony moved to
// ep 4 — so this tease must promise the theory without ruling on it. The
// tautology is the joke and it is a pure non-verdict: a returning viewer can
// read it either way, which is exactly the state ep 3 wants them in. Kokoro,
// so this is free and re-times the episode by about a second and a half.
// `rc_16_sunny` ("Wait. What?") is UNCHANGED and gets funnier: he cannot tell
// whether that was a compliment.
rc_15_narrator: {
  text: "Sunny has a theory. It is a very Sunny theory.",
  ...NARRATOR,
  speed: 0.9,
},
```

**Nothing else in ep 2 moves.** `rc_16_sunny`'s 45f held beat is unchanged and
now carries *"…was that an insult?"* instead of *"…oh no"*, which is the same
length of silence doing a better job. `script.md` (wind) Scene 36's stage
direction — *"Sunny holds his pose while the sentence catches up with him"* —
survives word for word. Cost: one free kokoro clip, ep 2 runs about **+1.4s**
(12:25 → ~12:26).

**Alternates, if the showrunner prefers:** *"Sunny has a theory. He is very
pleased with it."* (more visual, slightly softer) or *"Sunny has a theory. Wait
until you hear it."* (funnier read-aloud, but it leans "the theory is bad", which
is a verdict in a costume). The recommended line is the only one of the three
that genuinely declares nothing.

## 6.1 Scene 5 — Are we there yet **(REWRITE)**

### Scene 5 — Are we there yet
**On stage:** Narrator, Ray
**Visual:** Deep space. Ray streaking left to right across an unchanging star
field, tiny, with Earth a blue dot that does not appear to get any closer. **The
shot never cuts, and nothing in it ever changes** — not the star field, not the
speed, not the size of the dot. That is the whole set and it must stay that
boring for twenty-five seconds.
**Lines:** `a1_13_ray`, `a1_14_narrator`, `a1_15_ray`, **`a1_15b_narrator`**,
**`a1_15c_ray`**, **`a1_15d_narrator`**, **`a1_15e_ray`**, `a1_16_narrator`,
**`a1_16b_ray`**

**HELD BEAT — 45f (1.5s) before `a1_13_ray`** (i.e. on `a1_12_ray`'s
`gapFrames`, in Scene 4). Unchanged.

> **RAY:** Are we there yet?
> **NARRATOR:** One minute down. Seven to go.

**HELD BEAT — 30f (1.0s) after `a1_14_narrator`.**

> **RAY:** Are we there yet?
> **NARRATOR:** Two minutes down. Six to go.

**HELD BEAT — 45f (1.5s) after `a1_15b_narrator`.**

> **RAY:** Are we there yet?
> **NARRATOR:** Four minutes down. Four to go.

**HELD BEAT — 60f (2.0s) after `a1_15d_narrator`.**

> **RAY:** Are we there yet?
> **NARRATOR:** Seven minutes down. One to go.

**HELD BEAT — 75f (2.5s) after `a1_16_narrator`.** **The longest silence in Act
One, and the peak of the gag.** Same star field, same speed, same distance to go.
Nothing enters it — no bubble, no gesture, no emotion change, and above all no
sign that anything is about to happen.

> **RAY:** Are we there yet?

**Scene tail: 6f.** *(Deliberately almost nothing.)* The fifth firing is
**not answered**. Scene 6 cuts hard to a garden at full brightness on the frame
after it, and `a1_17_narrator` — *"And then Ray arrived, all at once, the way
light always does"* — is the answer. The joke's button is a cut.

**The escalation, and why it is built this way.** Three tools were available and
all three are used, because they do different jobs:

- **The five firings are one recording.** `a1_13_ray` is synthesized; the other
  four are `sameAs` aliases of it. Not four takes — MiniMax returned episode
  two's one repeated sentence at 2.20s and then 2.84s, and this gag is *only*
  the sameness. Five identical clips also means five identical mouth shapes and
  five identical bubbles, which is what makes the picture as flat as the sound.
- **The gaps escalate: 30 / 45 / 60 / 75.** The escalation is the *rhythm*, not
  an attempt to bore anybody. This episode's own audit warned about that
  explicitly — *"the 60f hold is two seconds, which is nowhere near long enough
  to actually bore anybody… nobody should lengthen the beat trying to make it

## 6.11 Scene 26 — The volcano **(CUT)**

**Deleted entirely.** `a3_06_narrator` is removed from `narration.mjs` and from
`Video.tsx`; the `s26_volcano` scene spec is deleted; the 60f hold goes with it.
**−9.6s.**

**Why.** The volcano gag's entire value is that *the show appears not to think it
is important*, and in the delivered cut the show stops the episode, points a
camera at it for twelve seconds, and says a sentence about it. That is the one
thing the gag cannot survive. Episode two planted it correctly — snoring smoke
rings on a horizon, one four-frame glance, no dialogue, nobody mentioning it —
and it worked because nothing acknowledged it.

**The volcano rule, rewritten, for whoever stages Act Three:**

> The volcano is **scenery** and nothing in this episode acknowledges it. It sits
> on the *measured* horizon (sample the plate or read the drawn `HORIZON`, never
> guess), it snores smoke rings on its three-second loop, and it must be
> **continuously visible for the whole of every shot it appears in** — a
> background gag that vanishes mid-scene reads as a bug. It appears in Scenes 25,
> 28b, 29, 31 and 35 and in no other frame of the episode. Nobody looks at it,
> nobody points at it, no arrow marks it, no music sting acknowledges it and the
> Narrator has **no line about it anywhere in this episode**. It wakes up in
> Scene 35 and that is the first and only time it does anything.

**Cost of the cut:** the density map loses one adult-leaning LAUGH beat at 8:45,
and *"The volcano is still asleep… It is extremely good at it"* leaves the
grown-up smirk list, taking it from nine to eight against a brief of four. That
is the right trade — an eight-second deadpan the show has to stop for is
expensive, and the beat it created is replaced four times over by Red and Blue in
the same act. It also buys back most of Scene 5's spend.

alone (Orange is right there), nothing is ending, and the Narrator's last line is
about anticipation rather than loss. The `calm` emotion on both Red lines is
doing this work and it is why Red must not be cast on a sleepy read.

**Pedagogy:** none new, and one thing restated as a picture: the sunset is *what
is left* after the long trip, not something that arrived. "Everybody bounced off"
is `a3_14c_narrator`'s *"They did not go anywhere. They went everywhere else"* said

# 11. The three biggest risks

## 1. The two new voices are the mechanism, and they cannot be fixed for free

Violet's three-firing gag cost one two-word clip because he never speaks. Blue
and Red are the opposite: the episode's new engine is two paid, unheard,
un-auditioned performances at speeds nothing in this repo has ever used (0.85 and
1.05). **Red at 0.85 is the single most fragile clip in the file** — `a3_18d_red`
("Peace and quiet.") has to be *contented*, and the failure mode is *tired*, and
a tired Red turns Scene 28b from the sunset's payoff into the light dying, which
is the one thing the tone guardrail forbids. A wrong Blue is cheaper but worse in
a different way: if he sounds like Ray, the split reveal's whole point collapses.

**Mitigations, all of them cheap and all of them before any staging:** audition
both on their hardest lines and at two speeds each (§3, §9); keep each behind one
constant with a kokoro stand-in so a reversal is a free re-time (the `PUFF_ENGINE`
shape, which has now worked twice); and **audition Blue against Ray's existing
clips**, not on his own — the ep-3 casting note says timbre separation for
adjacent lines is what got Ray his part, and this is the same call one character
further along.

## 2. Removing the verdict may read as a fizzle rather than as a precision

The cold open tells the audience to hold onto Sunny's theory. Episode two told
them, four minutes from its end, that one day he will be wrong. The Narrator
checks, and checks again, across a 45-frame silence built specifically to make a
ruling land. **And then the ruling does not come.** If "He has a point." plays as
a dodge instead of as a surprise, the episode has spent twelve minutes and three
scenes of set-up on an anticlimax, and it has done it in the one place the show
has never disappointed anybody.

The whole load sits on two clips and one sentence: `a2_49_narrator` landing flat
rather than apologetic, `a2_50_sunny`'s triumph landing as a joke rather than as
a shrug, and *"It is his light. It is not his painting."* being parseable at six.
**The safety net is that `a2_55_narrator` survives verbatim** — the biggest
grown-up laugh in the episode still fires, on schedule, off a two-item list, and
it converts a non-verdict into a punchline. Ear-check `a2_49` first, before
anything else in this document is built; if it cannot be made to work, the
fallback is not to restore the verdict but to make the concession *more* precise,
not less.

## 3. A cast defined by jitter fights every legibility rule the show has

Blue's character is that he cannot hold still. Violet's is that he vibrates hard
enough to blur. Indigo's is that he is always four frames behind. Meanwhile:
bubbles are ≤6 words and pop out of their tail; *deadpan is stillness*; *a
background gag has to be findable in a paused frame*; *a suggestive silhouette is
invisible to whoever drew it*; and a motion trail on a round body has to be two
lines or it is a tadpole. **This document asks for seven blobs moving wrongly in
seven different ways, in frames that also contain Ray, Puff, a crowd of air and
sometimes a diagram** — and it asks for three of Blue's bubbles to be legible
while he is ricocheting between corners.

The failure modes are known and specific: Blue and Violet's blurs becoming
indistinguishable from each other; the three-corner bubble in Scene 19 arriving
as a smear (the `hedge` lesson — *geometry checks test end states, the picture is
wrong in between*); Blue's ricochet reading as a rendering error rather than as a
character; and seven personalities firing at once in Scene 9 producing noise
instead of a cast.

**Mitigations, written into the scene specs above and repeated here because they
are the ones most likely to be skipped:** Blue's signature is a change of
*direction*, Violet's is amplitude in place, so the two blurs are different kinds
of blur; **Blue's bubbles are still even when Blue is not**, and each one is two
words with `tailAt` pointing at where he actually is; the seven come alive
**sequentially**, one every eight frames, never together; and every one of these
gets **stills rendered inside the animated beat**, not at its endpoints — the one
QA lesson this repo has now learned three separate times.
