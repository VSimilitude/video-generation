# EP 6 — Treatment B: "THE COUNT IS THE EPISODE"

Story-writer draft B from `ep6-brief.md`. Binding angle for this draft: the
**count is the spine, the hero and the lesson at once.** A thunderstorm is
the one event in the series a six-year-old can MEASURE with the hands in
their lap, so this treatment builds every act around one repeated action —
flash, the kid's fingers go up, the Narrator counts out loud, and on the
number the thunder arrives and announces himself. The same four-beat figure
(flash → count → arrival → "Present!") fires about a dozen times, and it
means something different in each act without changing a single word of
his: in act 1 it is the accusation (he is late, the card said so, everyone
says so); in act 2 it is a ruler (three counts, one kilometre — and the
count shrinks as the storm walks up to the house, down to the one firing
where there is nothing to count); in act 3 it is the goodbye (the count
grows, the kid runs out of fingers, and the last flash arrives with no boom
at all). The mechanism act is the funniest act because the mechanism IS the
gag: Blue's four-episode payoff, Cloudia's composure break, the MYTH bust
and Boom's break firing are all arrivals inside the shrinking count. Boom
is never late and never learns a moral. He changes what he is proud of:
he came to be on time; he leaves as the only character in the series you
can measure with your fingers.

---

## 1. Logline + title candidates

**Logline.** On the heaviest afternoon of the summer, Sunny's heat sends
the Cloud Hotel UP — a tower with a freezer on top, thousands of guests
rubbing shoulders, a crackle in the walls Cloudia calls "character" —
until the building cannot hold it and a guest checks out at the speed of
light. Born in the same instant, from the same crackle, is Boom: enormous,
warm, the loudest thing in the sky and the gentlest character in the show,
who has never been late in his life and keeps arriving to find everyone
already looking the other way. A kid at a window counts him in on their
fingers — nine, then six, then three, then nothing at all — while Blue,
who has been wrong about being first for three straight shows, turns up
with the lightning and is finally, physically right. Thunder is not late.
Thunder is far. And far, it turns out, is a number you can count.

**Title candidates** (series pattern: hero's name + story noun):

1. **"Boom and the One, Two, Three"** — preferred. The title is the count,
   which is the episode; a six-year-old can chant it, and the chant ends on
   his name ("one, two, three — Boom"). It also tells a parent exactly what
   the kid will do at the next storm.
2. "Boom and the Flash Who Never Waited" — names the asymmetric engine
   (he talks, she has gone); second choice.
3. "Boom and the Hotel That Went Up" — names Cloudia's set piece; flatter.
4. "Boom and the Late Thunder" — the card's word; too on the nose (he IS
   the thunder).

**Hero name — BOOM, confirmed, and argued.** (a) One syllable: he is the
fourth beat of the count ("One. Two. Three. BOOM.") and the kid can say
his name on the finger they have left. (b) It is the word every child
already uses for him — no teaching cost, and the title card pays for
itself. (c) The obvious alternative, *Rumble*, is not his name but his
distance: up close he is a crack, far away he is a rumble, and the
Narrator uses exactly that as pedagogy in S17 ("From here, he is a
rumble. He is still all Boom."). Naming him Rumble would spend the lesson
on the cast list. (d) No rhyme collision with Drip/Pip/Puff (the ep-5
Zip worry), and the vowel is nowhere else in the cast. Alternatives
logged: *Clap* (one syllable, "thunderclap" is real, but it is also
applause and a room of six-year-olds will applaud), *Thud* (dull, reads
as something falling), *Bang* (peril-adjacent — rule 11), *Rolo* (a
sweet). One standing rule the name needs: the Narrator says "Boom" only
as his NAME; the thunder's sound is staged sound design, never a
narrated sound word, on any engine.

## 2. Hero spec — Boom

- **Who:** the sound of the lightning, he/him. Born on screen at the end
  of act 1 inside Cloudia's hotel, in the same instant, from the same
  crackle, as Flash. Big, round, soft-edged — his body is a warm
  grey-violet ball with two offset concentric rings (motion-trail law:
  always two lines) that SPREAD as he travels: honest, because sound
  spreads out and gets quieter with distance. Near the cloud he is small,
  tight and sharp; at the window after a nine-count he arrives huge and
  soft. The rig is the physics.
- **Want (fresh):** to be ON TIME. He has never been late in his life. He
  leaves the instant she leaves. Everyone — the card, the kid, the
  Narrator, the crew — says thunder is late, and he keeps arriving to find
  the whole room already looking at where Flash was.
- **Attitude (heroes are comedians):** a proud professional with a
  punctuality complex. He treats the Narrator's count as ROLL CALL — a
  teacher is counting, his name is being called, and he answers it:
  **"Present!"** — and he has THEORIES about why everyone else is early
  (the planet is early; the ground is moving; she has a head start,
  which he has checked, and she does not). Never whiny, never sorry
  (Puff's reflex is spent), never a verdict-giver (Ember's), never a
  pedant (Ray's). He LIKES being loud: "The loud part is the best part."
  (an echo of the volcano's ep-5 "This is the loud part.", not a quote —
  he has his own sentence). His register is warm run-on sentences at
  1.0 — the opposite of Rock's full stops and the volcano's 0.9 — and
  the run-on is where the comedy of a big soft thing talking too long
  lives.
- **The turn (the lesson IS the arc, argued from the stance):** the
  episode never tells him lateness is fine. The COUNT tells him it was
  never lateness. In act 2 he hears the rule (light is fast, sound is
  slow, three counts to a kilometre) but takes it as a scoreboard — the
  count shrinking as the storm approaches reads to him as CATCHING UP,
  and the break firing (count zero, right overhead, Flash and Boom in one
  frame) is him getting exactly what he wanted. Then the storm leaves and
  the count grows again — three, six — and he has not changed one thing.
  That is the turn: "late" was never something he was; it is the
  distance between him and whoever is listening, and that distance is a
  NUMBER. He wanted to be on time; he gets to be a ruler — the only
  character in the series a kid can measure with. His quietest line is
  an instruction, not a concession: **"Count me."** No moral is stated
  (ep-5 precedent); he just changes what he is proud of, and his pride
  is louder than before.
- **Flash — silent, she/her, the fourth silent constant.** Blue-white
  zig, same body every time (Violet's same-blob law), gone before anyone
  finishes a sentence. Boom addresses her seven times in the episode and
  is answered **zero** times — she has always already left. The zero is
  the physics (she is light; he is sound) and the asymmetry is the whole
  engine: Ember↔volcano was 2 in 9; this is 0 in 7 and must stay 0. She
  strikes the sea far out, or the sky itself; nothing on screen is ever
  hit.
- **Casting (audition spec — new MiniMax voice, PROVISIONAL, measured
  first, Mike's ear second):** big, warm, resonant, a little goofy — a
  friendly giant, not a boom-box. Must clear Rock (`Deep_Voice_Man`,
  reserved) and the volcano (`Elegant_Man` −6) in timbre AND pace: Boom
  talks at 1.0 in run-ons, which neither of them does. Must not be a
  bright girl (band full). Candidates for the audition agent per brief
  (`Imposing_Manner` is free, `Casual_Guy`, `English_Jovialman`,
  `English_FriendlyPerson`, `English_CaptivatingStoryteller`, the
  `English_*Deep*` ids — probe for dead ids). Cast emotion `happy`;
  seasoning budget 2, both cited in §5. **Audition trio (mark in
  script):** (1) the chain source "Present!" (S7 — the one recording that
  fires eight times); (2) his longest proud run-on (S12, after the break:
  "Did you see that? She went and I went and there was no counting at
  all…"); (3) the turn's quietest line, "Count me." (S16 — must land
  warm, not defeated).

## 3. The count — the engine's mechanics

The Narrator's count is the episode's metronome and it has rules:

- **Ten kokoro clips, "One." through "Ten.", one recording each**, fired
  ~52 times across the episode as aliases (kokoro is deterministic; the
  aliases state intent and lock wording). The timeline places each
  number on a **30-frame grid** — one number per second — so the count
  IS seconds and the physics is literally true on screen (three counts ≈
  three seconds ≈ one kilometre). Never hand-tuned faster: a faster
  count is a pedagogy bug.
- **The kid's fingers are the picture.** Silhouette at the window, one
  finger per number — the first time in six episodes the kid DOES the
  thing the audience can copy. Ten fingers is the kid's ruler, and
  running out of fingers is act 3's joke.
- **During every count, Boom is visible travelling** — a soft ring
  rolling across the bay toward the window while the numbers climb. Sound
  made visible: the count is a chase ("will he make it by three?"), the
  diagram is a character, and no stretch of the episode is ever carried
  by drawn geometry alone.
- **Boom's arrival chain — "Present!"** — one MiniMax recording, `happy`,
  1.0, SOURCE = S7 (his birth, inside the hotel, the earliest firing).
  `sameAs` aliases at every counted arrival: S9 (six), S10 (three), S11
  (two), S11 (one), S15 (three), S16 (six), S17 (nine). **THE BREAK = S12,
  count zero** — a fresh recording, **"Present."** — full stop, no shout,
  `auto`, 0.95: right under the cloud, with Flash still in the frame, the
  loudest character in the sky does not need to announce himself because
  for the first time in his life the room is already looking at him. The
  crack of the thunder itself (sound design) is the loudest noise in the
  episode; his voice after it is the softest he has — the contrast is the
  joke and the physics is untouched (his voice is character; the thunder
  is SFX). **The far firings (S16, S17) are the SAME clip mixed with
  distance** — low-pass and room on the instance, never a re-record: the
  recording is identical; the distance does the acting (tech ask, §10).
- **Approach gap is NOT identity.** Blue's 4f is who he is; Boom's gap is
  the count, and it changes with distance. The script says so in one
  plain Narrator sentence (S11).
- **The count's three meanings** (the repetition gag that changes
  meaning): act 1 — the accusation (nine counts: "Late."); act 2 — the
  ruler (six, three, two, one, zero: "Three counts is one kilometre.");
  act 3 — the goodbye (three, six, nine, ten-and-nothing: "He said it.
  He always says it. From here, you cannot hear him.").

## 4. Act-by-act beat sheet

Horizon law in every coastal wide: the volcano awake, summit lit, Ember's
island beside him, continuously visible — a kid will check. The kid is at
the WINDOW from S2 onward and never leaves it; every outdoor scene is
framed through or beside that window. Laugh engine named per stretch as
**Engine:**.

### COLD OPEN

- **S1 — The heavy morning.** Garden, the kid (silhouette, silent, sixth
  canon episode) with bare feet on hot flagstones; volcano + island on the
  horizon, sea calm. Sunny up, enormous: **"GOOD MORNING, EVERYBODY!"** /
  **"I invented mornings!"** (series constant, six-for-six). He heats the
  ground — hard. Sunny, precision mode, every word true: "Feel that
  ground? Every bit of that warm is me. Every bit." Narrator: "It is.
  Hold on to that thought. By tonight that warm will have built the
  biggest thing in the sky, and he is going to want it on the record."
  Sunny: **"You're welcome! HA! HA!"** (firing 1 — the chain source for
  the episode's aliases). *Engine: Sunny's ego on a true claim + the
  Narrator's flat forecast — the kids are told he will be right before
  he is.*
- **S2 — The window rule, and the first count.** Afternoon; the light goes
  heavy and yellow; out past the volcano a cloud stands UP — a tower,
  flat on top. The kid goes indoors and takes position at the window
  (mug, blanket, cosy — this is a show, not a danger). Narrator, once,
  plainly: "Storms are for watching from a window. That is the rule."
  Far out at sea: FLASH. The kid's fingers come up, out of habit, and the
  Narrator counts with them — **"One." "Two." … "Nine."** (nine clips on
  the second grid; a soft ring is visible crossing the bay under the
  count) — then a low far rumble (SFX only; he is not introduced yet).
  Narrator: "There he is." [beat] "Last time, somebody asked why thunder
  is always late." [beat, 0.92] "Everyone says he is. Even the card said
  so. Let's go and ask him. He is up there." Camera lifts toward the
  tower. **TITLE CARD** over the thunderhead. *Engine: the deadpan room
  of a nine-second count — the series' repetition gag in its purest
  form, before the hero exists; the kids are counting before they know
  why.*

### ACT 1 — THE HOTEL GOES UP (Cloudia's set piece; Boom is born in it)

- **S3 — Lobby, drizzle-grey.** Inside the cloud: the Cloud Hotel exactly
  as it has always been — flat, grey, full of drops hugging specks.
  Cloudia, composed, "darling" on every line: "Welcome back to the Cloud
  Hotel, darling. We are grey. We have always been grey. It is a look."
  Narrator: **"Different show. Same darling."** (0.92 flat; fresh blank).
  Then the floor starts to RISE: Puff arrives from below carrying guests
  up at a speed Cloudia has never been delivered anything — his entrance
  line is a lift operator's: "Going up!" Cloudia, canon callback (fresh
  Abbess recording — a quote, not a chain): "Darling. There are no
  stairs." Puff: "There is an up, though." Puff: "Everybody hold on to a
  speck." (house rule: every guest must hug a speck of dust). From
  outside, Sunny allocates — the precision device, true in both halves:
  "The hot is me. The hurry is his." Narrator: "Correct. Both halves."
  (Pedagogy 1: hot ground, warm air in a hurry up — the ep-2 law, said by
  the character it belongs to.) *Engine: Cloudia's grandeur vs a hotel
  that has suddenly acquired a direction; Sunny's precise half-claim.*
- **S4 — Vertical.** The tower cross-section (DIAGRAM — character beats
  inside it, by rule): the lobby stays at the bottom; floors stack
  upward past where a hotel has any business being; a flat roof (the
  thunderhead — the anvil's reason is NOT named). Drip rides up on
  Puff's updraft, standing entrance, **fifth identical firing,
  cache-migrated from sky-blue `a1_31_drip`: "Hi! It's me! I'm the
  weather!"** ("Different show" skipped — the entrance carries itself,
  ep-5 ruling). Drip, going up: "I have never gone up this fast. I have
  never gone up this FAR." (bubble caps only; clip lowercase). Cloudia,
  weeping with joy, darling intact: "We have never had an upstairs,
  darling. We have never had an UP." / "Penthouse. We have a penthouse.
  Darling, I could weep." The top floors are above the freezing line.
  Cloudia: "The penthouse is a freezer, darling. The guests adore it.
  They have gone quite stiff." Drip, at the top, freezes into a hailstone
  — and is, for once, BIG: **"I have never been this big."** (her want,
  granted by physics; the hail is honest: drops carried up the tower
  freeze; optional one Narrator sentence: carried up and down, they
  freeze layer on layer, like an onion — CUTTABLE). *Engine: Drip's
  want fulfilled by a freezer + Cloudia's rapture at owning an up.*
- **S5 — ROLL CALL, firing 1 (greeting): the hail.** The penthouse is a
  crowd of near-identical frozen guests, bumping gently in the updraft —
  the picture the scene already has. Drip, cheerfully, slow list speed:
  **"Hi, Hail! Hi, Hailey! Hi, other Hail! Hi, Hail the third!"** [beat]
  **"…Hi, Stony."** (the big one at the back). Nobody replies inside the
  naming. Narrator, one flat explanatory line, 0.92, with the mechanism
  INSIDE it: "Every guest on the top floor is called Hail. They keep
  bumping into each other. It builds up." Button, unseasoned — Drip: "I
  am one of them now." *Engine: the series signature, picked because the
  joke IS the lesson — the bumping that builds the crackle is said as a
  roll call two scenes before it is said as physics.*
- **S6 — The crackle.** The guests rub past each other; every bump leaves
  a tiny spark in the walls. Cloudia, composure holding: "A little
  crackle in the walls, darling. Character." The crackle piles up — one
  kind on the top floor, the other in the basement. Cloudia: "Top floor
  one kind. Basement the other. They are not speaking, darling. It is
  building up." **Big Word card: STATIC.** Narrator, the kid-checkable
  aside: "You have made this. Rub a balloon on your hair in the dark.
  That crackle is this crackle. That is lightning, tiny." (no signs, no
  electrons — two kinds, separated, building). Cloudia, darling still
  attached but thinning: "I cannot hold it, darling. The hotel cannot
  hold it." *Engine: a hotel manager describing charge separation as a
  staffing dispute; the balloon aside is Math's.*
- **S7 — FLASH. And Boom.** The hotel cannot hold it: a channel jumps
  from the tower to the sea, far out — over in a blink; nothing is hit
  but water. **Big Word card: LIGHTNING.** Cloudia's BREAK — no
  "darling", new wording, a flat sentence said too fast: "That was a
  guest. That was a guest LEAVING. Nobody checks out of my hotel at that
  speed. Nobody." Then — same instant, same place, from the same crackle
  — the hotel's walls make a noise the hotel has never made, and there he
  is, in the lobby, brand new, enormous, delighted: **"Present!"**
  (CHAIN SOURCE, firing 1 — inside the cloud he is exactly on time, and
  nobody has told him yet that anywhere else he won't be). Cloudia,
  composure RETURNING because a noise is a thing a manager can handle:
  **"…Darling. Who is making that noise?"** Boom: "That is me. I am the
  noise. I came with her." Cloudia: "She did not sign the register,
  darling. She did not even take a towel." Boom, looking for Flash,
  addressed-not-answered #1: "Where did she go? She was right here. We
  left at the same time." [45f] Boom: "…She is quick." THE VOLCANO'S ONE
  LINE (argued §10; CUTTABLE): cut to the coastal wide — volcano, island,
  the tower out at sea — 48f, the politest reaction in the world to a
  louder-than-him noise: **"…Louder than me. Well done."** Boom beams.
  No line, no bubble — the volcano is never answered into banter. *Engine: the
  butler-grade compliment + Cloudia↔Boom (whose noise is this) + the
  first unanswered address to Flash.*
- **S8 — The same nine seconds, from the window.** Hard cut to the kid at
  the window, fingers already up: **"…Seven." "Eight." "Nine."** — the
  far rumble arrives. Narrator: "There he is." [beat] "Up there, he was
  on time. Down here, he was nine counts late. Those are the same
  seconds." [beat, 0.9] "Keep counting." **ACT BREAK.** *Engine: the
  cut IS the lesson — the audience has just watched both ends of one
  nine-second gap; nobody explains it yet.*

### ACT 2 — THE COUNT COMES DOWN (mechanism; funniest act at writing stage)

- **S9 — The storm arrives ahead of itself.** At the window the curtain
  stirs; the kid feels cold air on their face before anything has
  happened. Puff, at the glass, catch-phrase exactly once, cache-migrated
  from wind `a1_40_puff`: **"You can't see me. But you can FEEL me."**
  Puff: "I ran ahead. I always run ahead." Narrator: "That is the storm,
  arriving before itself. Cold air falls out of the bottom of the hotel
  and runs along the ground in front." (honest gust front). Then: FLASH,
  over the bay. Fingers. **"One." … "Six."** — the ring rolls in — and
  Boom arrives at the window for the first time, huge and soft, to a room
  already looking out to sea: **"Present!"** (firing 2). Boom: "Why is
  everybody looking over there? I am over here. I just got here." Then, to
  the empty sky where Flash was (addressed #2, unanswered): "You could
  wait. Just once." The kid turns. Narrator: "Six." Boom: "Six whats?" Narrator: "Counts." Boom:
  "Counts of what?" Narrator, 0.92: "You." Boom, THEORY 1, run-on: "I
  have a theory. Everybody down here is early. The whole planet. It is a
  very early planet and I am the only one on it who is on time." *Engine:
  the arrival to the wrong-facing room — the episode's repeatable
  humiliation that never humiliates him; Boom↔Narrator deadpan.*
- **S10 — Blue. The payoff fires.** FLASH over the sea — and with her,

  she goes." Narrator's button — the FORMAT FLIPPED, after
  three shows of the other sentence, 0.92, kind: **"He was first. He was
  also very fast. Today, those are the same thing."** BLUE'S STUNNED
  BEAT — the series' second — **90f**, nothing enters it; he has no line

  episode's first-vs-on-time engine, both of them getting what they
  wanted from the same flash: Boom: "Who is that?" Narrator: "That is
  Blue. He was first." Boom: "It is all right. I was on time." Narrator,
  gently: "You were one kilometre away." [beat] Then THE LESSON, once,
  plainly (rule 16): "Light is fast. It is here before you can blink.
  Sound is slow. It walks. About three counts for every kilometre."
  **RULE STAMP: "LIGHT IS FAST. SOUND IS SLOW."** Sunny, from behind the
  tower — interrupts a scene not about him, true in every clause: "I AM
  STILL UP HERE! THE CLOUD IS IN THE WAY! THE HEAT THAT BUILT THE CLOUD
  IS MINE!" Narrator: "All true. He is behind a cloud he built." *Engine:
  the four-episode payoff landing as a physics fact + Blue's first
  silence + Sunny shouting through a cloud.*
- **S11 — Three counts is one kilometre (the count shrinks).** The
  flash-to-boom DIAGRAM: the bay as a ruler, the hotel walking along it

  "Two."** — **"Present!"** (firing 4). Boom, THEORY 2: "Two! I am

  **"Present!"** (firing 5). Boom: "One is nearly none. Nearly none. I am

- **S12 — ZERO. The break.** The hotel is directly overhead; the window

  BUMP."** / "Big myth. Busted." **Big Word card: THUNDER** (a full
  minute after LIGHTNING; fired now that he is understood, not at his
  first arrival). Boom, proud: "That is me. I am the jump." / "The loud
  part is the best part." **ACT BREAK.** *Engine: Cloudia's wounded
  hospitality IS the correction; Boom gets his Big Word.*

### ACT 3 — THE COUNT GOES UP (the goodbye)

- **S14 — The grandest check-out in hotel history.** Rain and hail pour
  off the hotel onto the lawn and the sea; Cloudia, in her glory and her
  grief, canon line as a fresh Abbess recording (a quote, not a chain):

  once. All downward. The grandest check-out in the history of the
  trade." The kid watches it from the window, mug up. Sunny, behind the

**Physics-honesty guards (all held):**

- Flash and Boom leave the cloud in the same instant, from the same
  crackle — he is born in her building, in her frame (S7). "Late" is never
  asserted as true by the Narrator after S8; from S10 on, every gap is
  named as distance.
- **Even at count zero, light is first.** At the overhead strike the gap
  is a fraction of a second — not countable, but real — so Blue's payoff
  holds at every distance and Boom never claims "first", only "on time";
  the Narrator never says they arrived "together", she says "I did not get
  to one."
- The count shrinks because the STORM moves, not because Boom speeds up;
  his "catching up" theory is corrected as half-right (the hotel is
  moving) and he is never allowed to be the thing that changed.
- Sound ≈ 343 m/s → 2.9 s/km: "about three counts for every kilometre" is
  honest to the word "about"; the parent aside "about five seconds a
  mile" is also honest (4.7 s).
- The no-boom flash at S19 is honest ONLY if the storm is far: thunder is
  rarely heard beyond ~15 km, so the Narrator says "more than fifteen
  kilometres — forty-five counts", which is why the kid's ten fingers
  cannot reach it. The script must not let a ten-count flash go silent
  (3.3 km is audible).
- Overhead thunder is a sharp CRACK; distant thunder is a low RUMBLE
  (long channel + echoes + the far end arriving late). The sound design
  follows this exactly; Boom's distant "Present!" firings are the same
  recording mixed with distance, never re-performed.
- Sound spreads and quietens with distance — Boom's rig grows and softens
  as he travels; the tight small sharp Boom is the near one.
- Charge separation: collisions of ice in the updraft; two kinds; top and
  bottom; builds until it jumps. No positive/negative, no electrons, no
  plasma, no anvil mechanism. The balloon-on-hair comparison is the same
  phenomenon at kid level and the Narrator says "tiny".
- Lightning strikes only the sea far out or the sky; most lightning never
  touches the ground is stated or implied; no tree, house, creature or
  character is struck; the kid is indoors from S2 to the end.
- The gust front (S9) is real: cold air falls out of the storm's base and
  runs ahead along the ground — the kid feels the storm before it arrives.
- Hail falls at the storm's peak (S12), on grass, nobody under it; it melts
  to puddles by morning (S18). Drip is big as hail and drop-sized again as
  it melts — rain IS her.
- **Every Sunny claim is individually true:** the ground's warmth is his
  (S1); "the hot is me, the hurry is his" (S3); he is still shining above
  the cloud and his heat built it (S10); the rain is water he evaporated
  that morning (S14); he was behind the storm, never gone (S18); his
  surface ~5,500 °C, his core ~15 million °C (S21); he can explain the
  Moon's phases (S22). He never claims the bolt's heat — the bolt's heat
  is the one thing in the sky hotter than his surface and he says so.
- The Moon at S22 is half-lit — a phase a kid can check that week.
