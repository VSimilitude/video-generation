# EP 6 — Treatment A: "THE HOTEL IS THE EPISODE"

Story-writer draft A from `ep6-brief.md`. Binding angle for this draft: the
**Cloud Hotel is the set piece and the hero is born out of it.** Five
episodes have shown Cloudia's hotel drizzle-grey, overbooked and heavy; this
treatment spends the whole storm as HER gala — the hotel goes vertical in
act 1, freezes its penthouse, fills its walls with crackle, and throws the
grandest check-out in hotel history at the top of act 2. Boom does not
arrive NEAR the storm; he is born in her lobby, in the same instant as the
flash, from the same crackle, and his first word in series history is
spoken to a hotel manager who has just watched a guest leave through the
wall. Everything the lesson needs falls out of the building: the hotel is
the one place in the episode where thunder is never late (zero distance),
the kid's window is the one place where he always is (the count), and the
whole episode is the hotel walking from one frame of reference to the
other and out the far side. Blue's payoff, the MYTH, the count and the Big
Words all sit inside the mechanism act (S12–S18), which is written to be
the funniest act in the file.

---

## 1. Logline + title candidates

**Logline.** On the hottest afternoon of the series, Cloudia's drizzle-grey
Cloud Hotel is picked up by an invisible lift and delivered a penthouse in
the freezer, a lobby full of crackle, and a flat roof nobody ordered. When
the hotel can hold no more, a guest leaves through the wall — and in the
same instant, in the same lobby, something enormous, warm and extremely
loud is born, says "Present!", and sets off after her. He is Boom, the
thunder, and he has heard a rumour that he is always late. He has never
been late in his life. He leaves when she leaves. Everybody else, in his
professional opinion, is early. Meanwhile a kid at a window counts on
their fingers, Blue is finally first, and the loudest thing in the sky
discovers that "late" was never a thing he was — it was how far away you
were standing, and that is a job.

**Title candidates** (series pattern: hero's name + story noun):

1. **"Boom and the Hotel With No Stairs"** — preferred. Names the set
   piece with Cloudia's own canon joke ("Darling. There are no stairs."),
   which this episode pays off twice: the lift that gets everyone to the
   penthouse, and the check-out "through the floor". A six-year-old can
   say it and it is funny before the episode starts.
2. "Boom and the Hotel That Went Up" — the literal one; safe.
3. "Boom and the Grandest Check-Out" — names the act-2 set piece; a
   little abstract for the age.
4. "Boom and the Very Early Sky" — names his theory; too oblique as a
   title, excellent as a bubble.

**Hero name — BOOM, confirmed.** Argued: (a) it is the word every child
already uses for the thing, so his name is the kid's own vocabulary
turning up with a face; (b) one syllable, shoutable, and legal as a
whole-shout one-word MiniMax line; (c) his NAME can carry the loud part
while the actual thunder stays staged sound design (brief §6 — no
narrated "BOOOOM" on a MiniMax line, ever; the character says "Present!",
the mix says boom). Logged alternatives: **Rumble** (two syllables, warm,
but it names what he sounds like when he is FAR — i.e. the thing he
spends the episode being mistaken for; the Narrator uses "rumble" as a
word in S25 instead, where it is the pedagogy); *Clap* (reads as
applause); *Bang* (violent register — tone rule); *Thud* (too small for
the loudest thing in the sky); *Rolly* (a dog).

## 2. Hero spec — Boom

- **Who:** the sound of the lightning, he/him. Born on screen at the top
  of act 2, in the lobby of the Cloud Hotel, in the same instant as Flash,
  from the same crackle. The biggest body the series has drawn with a
  voice (the volcano is bigger and mostly scenery); the gentlest character
  in the episode. Proposed body: a huge, soft, rounded dark-grey shape
  with two offset concentric sound-rings for motion (the series' two-line
  trail rule), a warm round face, no arms needed — his gesture rig is
  SCALE and approach. He walks. He always walks at exactly the same pace
  (the physics; see Speed below). He is drawn in every window shot
  arriving from the direction of the hotel.
- **Want (fresh): to be ON TIME.** The ep-5 card said he is late; the
  Narrator says it; the window says it; he has heard. He has never been
  late in his life. He leaves the hotel the instant she leaves it. He is
  baffled, proud, and keeps arriving to find everyone already reacting to
  the flash — which he explains, happily, as everybody else being early.
- **Attitude (heroes are comedians): a proud professional with a
  punctuality complex and THEORIES.** He is never sorry (Puff's reflex,
  spent), never files verdicts (Ember's, spent), never corrects anyone
  (Ray's, spent). He explains. His theory of the sky is that it is a very
  early sky: "Everybody down here is early." / "That one is early for a
  living." (Blue) / "Even the kid is early. The kid finished counting
  before I got here. That is how early the kid is." He LOVES being loud
  ("The loud part is my favourite part." — echo of the volcano's V3, not
  a quote) and he tells Flash everything even though she has never once
  stayed to hear it.
- **Speech:** warm run-on sentences at 1.0, commas not full stops, the
  register of a very large friendly man who has just arrived somewhere
  and would like you to know he set off promptly. Big, resonant, a little
  goofy — a friendly giant, not a boom-box.
- **Speed is the physics, so it never changes:** every Boom line is 1.0,
  all episode, including the turn (the Ember precedent of an audible
  speed drop is deliberately NOT used — sound travels at one speed, and
  so does he). His emotional arc is carried by what he is proud of, not
  by tempo.
- **The turn (the lesson IS the arc, un-moralised):** nobody tells him.
  The count does. Right under the cloud (S22) he arrives WITH Flash and
  nobody even starts counting; as the storm leaves (S24) the window
  counts more and more of him — and he notices, out loud, that they are
  not counting how late he is. They are counting how FAR he is. Nobody
  has ever counted how far he is before. "I was never late. I was far."
  And far is a job: he is the only character in the series a kid can
  measure with. He changes what he is proud of — "I like that better
  than on time." — and no moral is stated by anyone.
- **The Flash engine (asymmetric by design — zero replies, ever):** he
  addresses her four times in the episode (S13 lobby, S16, S22 the break,
  S24 the turn). She is gone before his second word every time. The zero
  is the physics (a flash has no time to) and the sweetness is that he
  tells her anyway: "She is always gone. I tell her everything anyway."
- **Casting (audition spec — new MiniMax voice, PROVISIONAL, measured
  first, Mike's ear second):** big, warm, resonant, goofy-friendly; must
  clear Rock (`Deep_Voice_Man`, not in this episode but reserved) and
  the volcano (`Elegant_Man` −6, ONE line in this draft, S22) in timbre
  AND pace — Boom talks at 1.0 in run-ons, the opposite of both. Not a
  bright girl. Probe list per brief: `Imposing_Manner` (free),
  `Casual_Guy`, `English_ManWithDeepVoice`, `English_Jovialman`,
  `English_Deep-VoicedGentleman`, `English_FriendlyPerson`,
  `English_Comedian`, `English_CaptivatingStoryteller`,
  `English_MatureBoss`, `English_Trustworthy_Man`. Cast emotion `happy`;
  seasoning budget 2, one spent (`surprised` at the S22 break — stage
  direction: the only time in his life he sees her arrive), one in
  reserve. **No caps in any Boom line text**; his loud is the mix and the
  bubble.
- **Audition trio (marked in the beat sheet):** (1) the chain source
  "Present!" (S13); (2) his longest proud run-on (S16); (3) the turn's
  quietest line (S24 — must land warm, not defeated).

## 3. Cloudia's set piece — the hotel, and her barometer

Casting per canon: minimax `Abbess`, 1.0, "darling" on every composed
line. This treatment makes her the episode's second lead: ~30 lines, every
one of them hotel management, every one of them mechanism. The hotel does
four things it has never done, in order, and each is a pedagogy beat with
her stress or her pride on it:

| Hotel event | Scene | The physics it is | Her line (shape) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A lift arrives at a hotel with no stairs | S4 | the updraft — warm air in a hurry, up | "Darling. There are no stairs." / Puff: "There is now a lift." |
| The hotel goes VERTICAL; a flat roof she did not order | S6 | the thunderhead | "I did not order a flat roof, darling. I shall keep it." |
| The penthouse is in the freezer; guests are ice; they go up and down | S7–S8 | hail — layer on layer | "You have been up and down four times, darling. You are enormous." |
| The crackle in the walls; her hair stands up | S9 | STATIC — charge building by collisions | "Darling, why is my hair doing that." |
| A guest leaves THROUGH THE WALL | S12 | LIGHTNING — the channel to the sea | **BREAK — no darling:** "Somebody has just left through the wall!" |
| A noise is born in her lobby | S13 | THUNDER, at zero distance — never late here | "Darling. Who is making that noise?" |
| She busts the MYTH personally | S15 | clouds do not bump; the boom is the heat | "Darling. We do not bump. We mingle." |
| Every penthouse guest checks out at once, through the floor | S23 | the hail falls | roll-call goodbye (Drip) / Narrator: "There are no stairs." |

**The composure barometer.** "darling" holds on EVERY composed line through
the tower, the freezer, the up-and-down and the crackle (the stress is
visible, the word never drops — that is what makes the drop land). It
breaks exactly ONCE, at the first lightning (S12): fresh wording, no
darling, bubble in caps, clip text lowercase. It is back one line later,
strained — "Darling. Who is making that noise?" — and from there it holds
to the end. Ep 1's "We are at! Full!! Capacity!!!" is spent; the word
"capacity" does not appear in this episode.

**Her want, fresh:** a gala. Six shows of drizzle; she was "born for a
gala, darling." Her joy peaks not at the lightning (that startles her) but
at S6 — the day gravity loses: "I love my guests, darling. And today,
gravity has lost." (A variant of her ep-1 canon "I love my guests. I hate
gravity." — flagged §11; the updraft is the one day of her life the
building goes UP.) Gravity then wins the hail back (S8, S23), which she
takes as the hotel's natural rhythm: "Up, darling. Down. Up. That is a
hotel."

**Her frame of reference is the lesson's other half.** Boom is born beside
her; she hears him the instant he exists; he has never once been late to
her hotel. When the Narrator (at the window) says he is three counts
behind, Cloudia's answer is pure hotel management and accidentally the
whole physics: "Then the window is far, darling." (S18). She never
understands it as a lesson. Boom does, two scenes later, with the count.

## 4. Flash — the silent constant; Blue — the payoff

**Flash (the lightning), she/her, the series' fourth silent constant.**
Blue-white zig, same body every firing (Violet's same-blob law), on screen
for ≤6 frames and never more; the sky whites with her; she is gone before
anyone finishes a sentence. Zero lines, zero clips, zero replies, forever.
Eight flashes in the episode (one per Boom firing + the break); her
targets are the sea far out (S12, S20), across the sky (S16, S21, S22 —
"most lightning never touches the ground", and the overhead one goes
sideways on purpose), and the sea again as she leaves (S24, S26). Nothing
on screen is ever struck. She gets one visual slam in the chant
(LIGHTNING) and is gone before the card finishes landing.

**Blue — THE BANKED ANTI-SUNNY PAYOFF FIRES (showrunner call, brief §4;
TOP-logged).** One scene (the continuous window scene S12–S14), kind,
physics-honest, placed where Boom's lateness is the subject. Blue is
light; the flash is light; when Flash goes, Blue ricochets off the window
glass with her: **"First! Sorry, window! I am first!"** — and three
counts later Boom arrives and proves it. Exact flipped button, Narrator,
0.92 flat: **"He was first. He was also very fast. Today, those are the
same thing."** His reaction is the series' second stunned beat: 45f, no
line — he has never been right before and does not have a line for it
(the Narrator says so). His recovery is the only line he could possibly
find: "I was first!" — and Boom, delighted, hands him the other half:
"And I was on time. Good team." Both got what they wanted from the same
flash. Narrator: "Both true." Blue exits on a fresh first ("First to go
home! Sorry, curtain!"). Three lines total; "I just said that!" (capped)
untouched; Indigo OUT (default).

## 5. Act-by-act beat sheet

Horizon law: the volcano is AWAKE on the measured horizon of every
coastal wide, summit lit, Ember's island beside him, continuously visible
— including through the dark act 3, where his lit summit is the ONE
steady light on the horizon while the storm walks over the house (a kid
will check; the staging makes checking rewarding). Laugh engine named per
stretch as **Engine:**. All MiniMax line text is lowercase inside
sentences; caps shown here live in bubbles.

### COLD OPEN

- **S1 — The hottest morning.** Coastal garden, the kid (silhouette,
  silent, sixth canon episode) flat on the grass with a glass of water,
  too hot to move. Volcano + Ember's island on the horizon, summit
  glowing in daylight. Sunny, at full wattage: **"GOOD MORNING,
  EVERYBODY!"** / **"I invented mornings!"** (the pair, six-for-six) —
  and, about the heat: "This one is going to be a big one. I can tell.
  I am doing it." — TRUE (his heat builds today's storm) — **"You're
  welcome! HA! HA!"** (firing 1, SOURCE). Narrator, flat: "He is doing
  it. He does not know what 'it' is yet. Neither do you." *Engine:
  Sunny's precision ego (every claim true, and he claims it anyway) +
  Narrator deadpan.*
- **S2 — The drizzle hotel.** A small grey cloud drifts in over the sea,
  the way it has in every show it has been in. Narrator: **"Different
  show. Same hotel."** (firing 1 of 2, 0.92 flat). Cloudia, composed,
  bored to the bone: "Six shows, darling. Six. Not one gala. Drizzle.
  I am known for drizzle." / "I was born for a gala, darling." Narrator:
  "Today she gets one. She does not know that yet either." *Engine: the
  grand manager with nothing to manage; the want planted.*
- **S3 — The card, re-posed.** Narrator: "Last time, somebody asked why
  thunder is always late." [30f] "Today the thunder is going to be born
  — in a hotel — and answer that himself." [beat] "He has not been born
  yet. He is already not late." **TITLE CARD** over the grey hotel and
  the glowing horizon: *Boom and the Hotel With No Stairs.* *Engine: the
  promise carried visibly; the joke in the title.*

### ACT 1 — THE HOTEL GOES UP (Cloudia's gala; STATIC)

- **S4 — Air in a hurry.** Heat shimmer off the ground. Narrator: the
  ground is hot, the air above it gets hot, and hot air does the thing
  it learned in show two — it goes UP, fast. Puff enters already working,
  an invisible lift arriving at a hotel that never had one — his entrance
  is a NEW line, not the catch-phrase (deviation §11): Cloudia, canon
  verbatim, to the first guest looking for the penthouse: "Darling. There
  are no stairs." Puff: "There is now a lift." Cloudia: "...Darling." (a
  composed one-word line — the word holds). Puff: **"Going up."** (CHAIN
  SOURCE, `sameAs`, firing 1 of 3). Guests rise. *Engine: hotel logistics
  colliding with physics — the lift IS the updraft; Puff as the chirpiest
  lift operator in the sky.*
- **S5 — A guest checks in.** Drip rides in on the lift — standing
  entrance, FIFTH identical firing, cache-migrated from sky-blue
  `a1_31_drip`: **"Hi! It's me! I'm the weather!"** Narrator: **"Different
  show. Same guest."** (firing 2 of 2, fresh blank, 0.92). Cloudia, who
  has heard this entrance before: "You are a guest, darling. The weather
  is the management." Drip: "Then I would like the top floor." Puff:
  "Going up." (chain firing 2). *Engine: the standing entrance meeting a
  manager who files it; the want-to-be-big planted on "top floor".*
- **S6 — The hotel goes vertical.** Cross-section WITH characters in it
  (diagram-sag rule): the lift runs up the middle, the hotel stacks
  floors as fast as guests arrive — ten, twenty, a tower — until it
  wears a flat roof. Cloudia: "I did not order a flat roof, darling."
  [beat] "I shall keep it." Narrator: "Very tall clouds get a flat top.
  That is the shape of a thunderstorm. You can see one coming from a
  long way off." (shape named, reason not — brief). The tower passes
  Sunny on the way up and BLOCKS him. Sunny, from behind it: "Hey. HEY.
  Who put a hotel in front of me?" Narrator: "Cloudia. With your help."
  Sunny, instantly delighted, precise: "Then the hot is mine! And the up
  is mine! The up runs on the hot!" Narrator: "The hot is his. The up is
  his as well. The hotel is hers." Sunny: **"You're welcome! HA! HA!"**
  (firing 2, alias). And Cloudia's peak, the gala arriving: "I love my
  guests, darling. And today, gravity has lost." *Engine: Sunny claiming
  exactly his part through a wall of cloud + Cloudia's happiest sentence
  in six shows.*
- **S7 — The penthouse is in the freezer; ROLL CALL, firing 1.** Drip
  arrives at the top and freezes mid-sentence — she is ICE, and for the
  first time in her life she is BIG: "I am ice. Look at me. I have never
  been this big." Cloudia, by the house phone: "The penthouse is above
  the freezing line, darling. I did not put it there. The lift did." The
  top floor is full of near-identical frozen guests. Drip, cheerfully,
  the biggest stranger last: **"Hi, Hail! Hi, Hailey! Hi, other Hail!
  Hi, Hail the third! …Hi, Stony."** (per-line speed slowed for the
  list; Stony is the one who has done the most trips). Narrator, one
  flat explanatory line, 0.92: "Every guest on the top floor is called
  Hail. They keep rubbing past each other in the lift. It builds up."
  Button, unseasoned — Drip: "Big crowd." Nobody replies inside the
  naming. *Engine: the series-signature roll call on the picture the
  scene already has; the joke is the lesson — the STATIC plant is inside
  the Narrator's middle line.*
- **S8 — Up, down, up.** Gravity wins a round: Drip drops a floor, Puff
  catches her — "Going up." (chain firing 3, final) — she freezes
  another layer: "I am bigger again!" Cloudia: "You have been up and
  down four times, darling. You are enormous." Narrator, plainly: that
  is how hail grows — up to the freezer, a layer of ice, down, up again,
  another layer. Stony has been up and down more than anybody. Cloudia,
  surveying her building with pride: "Up, darling. Down. Up. That is a
  hotel." *Engine: the lift chain paying off on hail physics; Drip
  finally big and getting bigger.*
- **S9 — The crackle; Big Word: STATIC.** Cloudia's cloud-wisps rise off
  her head and stand up. "Darling, why is my hair doing that." Narrator:
  every time two icy guests rub past each other, a little crackle rubs
  off — the SAME crackle as a balloon rubbed on your hair. (Kid-check,
  stated plainly: rub a balloon on your hair, in the dark, pull it away
  — tiny lightning. Yours.) **Big Word card: STATIC.** Cloudia walks the
  cross-section like a manager reading a fault report: "The top floor has
  gone one way, darling. The lobby has gone the other way. And the lift
  is full of crackle." (honest charge separation, no signs, no electrons
  — top one kind, basement the other). Sunny, from behind the tower,
  interrupting a scene not about him — truthfully, and for the record:
  "The crackle is not mine. I would like that written down. I did the
  hot. The guests did the crackle." Narrator: "Written down." Then the
  act's last line — Cloudia, composed, the word still holding under
  maximum stress: "The hotel cannot hold it, darling." *Engine: a diagram
  with a hotel manager standing in it + Sunny disclaiming with precision
  (the new steady state demonstrated in reverse).* **ACT BREAK.**

### ACT 2 — THE GRANDEST CHECK-OUT (the mechanism act; written to be the funniest)

**Physics-honesty guards (all held):**

- **Simultaneity is staged, not asserted.** Boom is born beside Cloudia
  and she hears him INSTANTLY (zero distance); the same recording arrives
  at the window three counts later (S13→S14). The lesson is played as
  sound a full scene before the Narrator says it in words (S17).
- **The count measures distance to the lightning, in seconds.** Each
  Narrator number + gap must be ~30f so three counts are three seconds
  (production gate). "How far the hotel is" is the kid-level phrasing;
  the bolt leaves the hotel, so the distance is the hotel's to within
  the storm's own size — honest at this scale.
- **Flash's targets:** the sea far out, or across the sky. Nothing on
  screen is struck; the overhead bolt (S22) goes sideways on purpose —
  and the Narrator has already said most lightning does (S16). No tree,
  house, creature, rod, drill. The kid is behind glass the whole act.
- **Hail:** grows by repeated trips up to the freezer (the lift chain IS
  the layering); falls when gravity wins (the check-out); bounces on
  grass and path, pea-sized, nothing harmed; small hail melts on the way
  down — Drip lands as a drop again (honest, and it is her button).
- **Charge separation at kid level:** "rubbing past each other builds a
  crackle; the top floor has gone one way, the lobby the other." No
  positive/negative, no electrons, no plasma. The balloon analogy is the
  sanctioned kid-check and is stated as the SAME crackle, which it is.
- **The anvil:** shape named ("a flat top — that is the shape of a
  thunderstorm"), reason never given. No tropopause.
- **The gust front:** cold air descends out of the storm and runs ahead
  of it across the ground; the kid feels it before the storm arrives
  (S19). Puff's "Going down." is the downdraft; the catch-phrase is its
  arrival at the window. Honest and in storm order (after the first
  lightning, before overhead).
- **The rumble:** a channel is kilometres long, so its sound arrives over
  a span; hills reflect it. "The same him. He just takes longer to
  finish." No new mechanism invented.
- **Too far to hear:** a flash with no audible boom at ~12 counts and
  beyond (thunder is rarely heard past ~15–20 km) — honest, and the
  goodbye.
- **Blue is light** — he arrives with Flash because light arrives with
  light; the flipped button is physically true on every flash in history.
  The series finally lets him be right about the one thing he literally
  is.
- **Every Sunny claim is individually true:** his heat built the storm
  ("I am doing it." / "the hot is mine, the up runs on the hot"); the
  crackle is NOT his (he says so — the guests' collisions did it); the
  world is still running on him when he is hidden; he never left; the
  surface vs the inside (~5,500 °C vs ~15 million °C). The Narrator
  checks each one on screen.
- **Cloudia's two frames are both true** and the Narrator says so twice
  (S18, S26): at the source, flash and boom are together; at a distance,
  they are not; the difference is the distance. No frame is wrong.
- **The volcano:** awake, lit, island present, continuously visible in
  every coastal wide — the dark act makes his lit summit the horizon's
  one steady light (continuity obligation turned into staging value).
  One line, 48f, `auto`, −6, 0.9, bubble from the mouth.
- **Out of scope stays out:** no charge signs, plasma, sprites, ball
  lightning, rods, strikes on anything, drills, tornadoes, hurricanes.
  "Lightning never strikes twice" (optional second stamp) is NOT fired —
  the episode's one stamp is cleaner; it is garnish if synthesis wants
  it (a tall-tower line at S28 would carry it in one sentence).

## 10. Estimated runtime and where the risk is

**Estimate: 15–17 minutes.** 30 scenes; two set pieces (the tower, the
check-out); the count ledger alone is ~55 seconds of metronome spread over
eight firings, which reads as pace rather than air because the kid's hand
is doing something in every one of them.

**Risks, ranked:**

1. **The hero's first line is at ~minute 8.** This is the stance — he is
   born out of the building, and the building takes an act to go up. The
   cold open names him (S3: "He has not been born yet. He is already not
   late.") so the audience is waiting for him, not wondering where he is;
   and act 1 is carried by Cloudia, Puff's lift chain, Drip's freeze and
   the roll call, which is the series' proven act-1 density. But a hero
   who arrives this late is a real risk at the six-year-old's end and the
   synthesis should weigh it against B. Fallback inside the stance: a
   one-line cold-open tease of his VOICE from the future ("Present!")
   over black — rejected here because it spends the chain source before
   the lobby, but it is one clip if the wait proves too long.
2. **Laugh cadence through S17–S18 (the diagram + the ruler).** Two
   drawn-geometry beats back to back; both have Boom standing inside
   them talking ("Distance. I have a lot of that." / "Everybody keeps
   counting at me.") and S18 ends on Cloudia's two-frames joke. Audit
   first at script stage; the max gap on spoken lines should stay under
   ~40s but that is a self-score.
3. **The count's honesty is a production constraint, not a creative
   one.** If the number clips + gaps drift from ~1 s each, "three counts
   = one kilometre" is a lie on screen. Gate it in the manifest.
4. **The new voice.** Boom is ~28 recordings on an overnight-measured
   PROVISIONAL casting that must clear two reserved deep male voices and
   not be a boom-box. Mitigation per precedent: one constant in
   `narration.mjs`, chain source identified before generation, recast =
   re-buy, 1.0 on every line so a recast is a voice-id swap only.
5. **Flash's legibility at ≤6 frames.** A kid must register a CHARACTER
   went past, not a glitch: same body every time, the sky whites with
   her, and the Narrator introduces her by name at S12 while the card
   holds. Her chant slam being six frames is a joke only if the body has
   been seen eight times first.
6. **Three Big Words + three stamps in ~7 minutes (S9–S18).** Spacing
   checked: STATIC ~6:00, LIGHTNING ~8:00, MYTH stamp ~9:30, THUNDER
   ~10:30, LIGHT IS FAST ~11:30, THREE COUNTS ~12:15. Within "one card a
   minute" with margin; if the mechanism act runs short, the two rule
   stamps merge onto one stamp (brief allows either).
7. **One-word clips** ("Present!", "One."–"Twelve.", the chant) are the
   known highest-TTS-risk class — fallback respellings pre-written at
   script stage; "Present!" auditioned as the first of the trio.
