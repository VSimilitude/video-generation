## 0. The commission

The ep-5 end card asked **"WHY IS THUNDER ALWAYS LATE?"** The kids have
seen it; Mike ratified it. This is the storm episode — the weather crew's
reunion (Cloudia is OWED a storm: her hotel has only ever been
drizzle-grey) — and the most kid-checkable question the series has asked:
at the next thunderstorm, the kids can count flash-to-boom themselves and
be right. Claire (6) wants deadpan repetition, roll calls, and room; Math
(8) wants true checkable facts and myth-busts; Mike catches physics
intuition breaks and dry stretches. Runtime 10–30 min — hit the beats,
never target a duration (expect ~14–18 min at ep-5 density).

## 1. The single takeaway

**Thunder is not late. Thunder is far.** Lightning and thunder are ONE
event — the flash is what you see, the boom is what you hear — and they
leave the cloud at the same instant. Light is so fast it is here before
you can blink; sound is slow (about three seconds for every kilometre).
The gap between the flash and the boom is not lateness, it is DISTANCE —
and you can count it.

Pedagogy beats, all rideable by characters (world rule 2), in the order
the storm builds:

1. **Hot ground, air in a hurry UP.** Sunny heats the ground on a heavy
   afternoon; warm air rises fast (ep-2 law redeployed — "Different show.
   Same ___." is available). Puff is the UPDRAFT: he carries the guests up
   faster than Cloudia has ever been delivered anything.
2. **The Cloud Hotel goes VERTICAL.** Drops ride the updraft so high the
   hotel becomes a tower with a flat roof (a thunderhead; do not name the
   anvil's reason). The penthouse is above the freezing line: guests up
   there are ICE. (Hail is drops carried up and down the tower, freezing
   layer on layer — honest, optional.)
3. **The crackle.** Up in the tower the icy guests bump and rub past each
   other; every bump builds a little crackle — the SAME crackle as a balloon
   rubbed on your hair (kid-checkable at home, in the dark: tiny
   lightning). The crackle piles up (top floor one kind, basement the
   other) until the hotel cannot hold it. **STATIC** is the Big Word
   candidate here. (Honest kid-level account of charge separation by
   collisions in the updraft; do not say positive/negative, do not say
   electrons.)
4. **LIGHTNING.** The crackle jumps — a channel from the hotel to the
   ground, the sea, or across the sky (most lightning never touches the
   ground). It is over in a blink. Nothing on screen is harmed by it:
   it strikes the sea far out, or the sky itself. Big Word: LIGHTNING.
5. **THUNDER is the lightning's SOUND.** The bolt makes the air around it
   so hot, so fast, that the air jumps out of the way all at once — that
   jump is the boom. Same instant, same place, one event. Big Word:
   THUNDER — fire it when Boom is finally understood, not on his first
   arrival (cards need a minute between them).
6. **Light is fast. Sound is slow.** The flash is here before you can
   blink. The boom walks — about three seconds for every kilometre.
   So: **count.** Flash — one, two, three — boom: one kilometre away. Six
   counts, two kilometres. The count is a ruler. Rule-stamp candidate (not
   a Big Word): "LIGHT IS FAST. SOUND IS SLOW." and/or "THREE COUNTS =
   ONE KILOMETRE." (Units: kilometres and seconds; the writer may add a
   parent-aside "about five seconds a mile" ONCE if it fits — logged for
   Mike.)
7. **Why the rumble.** A bolt is kilometres long; the sound from its near
   end arrives before the sound from its far end, and hills send it back
   — so one bang arrives as a long grumble. Honest, cheap, and a natural
   home for a crowd of identical rumbles/echoes if a treatment wants one.
8. **The count changes.** As the storm comes closer the count shrinks
   (three… two… one…); right under the cloud, flash and boom are
   together; as the storm leaves, the count grows (four… five… eight…)
   until the flash arrives with NO boom at all — too far to hear. The
   storm says goodbye by getting "later". This is act 3's spine and the
   goodbye roll call's natural engine.

Safety, handled by STAGING not lecture: the kid watches the whole storm
from a WINDOW, indoors, from the moment the sky darkens — counting on
fingers. One plain Narrator sentence at most ("Storms are for watching
from a window. That is the rule.") and never fear. World rule 11: no
scary peril. The storm is a SHOW, Cloudia's gala — not a danger.

OUT OF SCOPE: electrons/charge signs, "positive/negative", plasma, the
tropopause/anvil mechanism, sprites, ball lightning, lightning rods, any
strike on a tree/house/creature, storm-safety drills beyond the window
rule, tornadoes, hurricanes.

## 2. Mandatory bible items — NAMED HERE before treatments start

**World rule 17 — the checkable world-scale fun fact (recap mind-blower
slot, "Now here is the amazing part."):**
- PRIMARY: **Lightning is about five times hotter than the SURFACE of the
  Sun** (a bolt heats the air to around 30,000 °C; the Sun's surface is
  about 5,500 °C). True, checkable, world-scale — and it lands ON SUNNY,
  whose new steady state is precision: he concedes the surface and claims
  the inside ("The surface. Only the surface. Inside, I am MUCH hotter."
  — true: the Sun's core is about fifteen million degrees). The Narrator
  checks it and it holds. Awe held in silence first, Sunny's button after.
- SECONDARY (Math bait, the "look it up" slot): **Lake Maracaibo in
  Venezuela gets thunderstorms more than a hundred nights a year — the
  lightning capital of the world** (Catatumbo lightning). Pronounceable;
  the Narrator may say it. Alternative if a treatment prefers: lightning
  strikes the Earth more than forty times every second (about three
  million times a day).
- Fine second-beat facts about the viewer (do not discharge the rule
  alone): the balloon-on-hair crackle IS lightning, tiny; you have never
  heard thunder that was not already over.

**World rule 18 — the MYTH stamp (a genuine kid misconception, corrected
as a gift):**
- **"Thunder is the sound of clouds bumping into each other."** The red
  MYTH stamp (ep 1 Sc 14 / ep 3 Sc 15–16 house device: stamp thuds on,
  cracks, silence, "Big myth. Busted.") busts it, and **Cloudia carries
  the bust** — she is personally offended ("Darling. We do not BUMP. We
  mingle.") and the honest mechanism rides inside her defence: the icy
  guests DO rub shoulders upstairs, and that builds the crackle — but the
  BOOM is the lightning's heat making the air jump. Clouds are a zillion
  tiny drops floating together (her ep-1 line, canon); two of them meeting
  make no sound at all. Stamp text candidates: "CLOUDS DO NOT BUMP." /
  "THUNDER IS NOT CLOUDS BUMPING."
- OPTIONAL second stamp if a treatment earns it cheaply: "Lightning never
  strikes the same place twice" — false (tall towers are struck dozens of
  times a year). Keep the first one mandatory; this one is garnish.

## 3. The hero — NEW character, a fresh arc (binding guardrails)

Arcs SPENT, do not reuse (bible §5 / audience.md): the circle (Drip), I
am real stuff (Puff), wrong-about-self (Ray), ambition from one spot
(Pip), impatience vs slow (Ember). Attitudes SPENT: Puff's apology reflex
(counted to zero in ep 2 — the hero must NOT be apologetic), Ember's
verdict runner ("___ is broken."), Ray's pedantry.

**Working name: BOOM — the thunder.** He/him (the series' last three
heroes were girls; the bright-girl voice band is FULL — bible "Casting
constraints"). Treatments may counter-propose the name (Pip/Ember
convention: short, sayable by a six-year-old; "Rumble" is the obvious
alternative) — argue it.

- **What he is:** the sound of the lightning. Born in the same instant as
  the flash, in the same place, from the same crackle. He is big, LOUD,
  and soft-hearted — the loudest thing in the sky and the gentlest
  character in the episode.
- **Want (fresh): to be ON TIME.** Everyone — the card, the kid, the
  crew — says thunder is late. He has never been late in his life. He
  leaves the instant she leaves. He is baffled, proud, and keeps arriving
  to find everyone already reacting to the flash.
- **Attitude (heroes are comedians):** a proud professional with a
  punctuality complex. He announces himself like attendance — the
  repetition engine candidate is **"Present!"** (one MiniMax recording,
  `sameAs` chain, fired every arrival, exactly on the kid's count) — and he
  has theories about why everyone else is early. Never whiny, never sorry.
  He LIKES being loud ("the loud part is the best part" — note the
  volcano said "This is the loud part." in ep 5; an echo is allowed, a
  quote is not).
- **The turn (the lesson IS the arc):** "late" is not a thing he is — it
  is the distance between him and whoever is listening. Right under the
  cloud he is on time; a kilometre away he is three counts "late"; the
  farther you are, the later he seems. He was never late. He is FAR. And
  far is useful: he is the only character in the series a kid can measure
  with — the count tells you where the storm is. He wanted to be on time;
  he gets to be a RULER. Resolution stays un-moralised (ep-5 precedent: no
  moral stated; he just changes what he is proud of).
- **Flash — the lightning — is a SILENT CHARACTER, she/her, the series'
  fourth silent constant** (with the kid, Violet, and formerly the
  volcano). She is gone before anyone finishes a sentence; she never
  waits; she never speaks (a flash has no time to). Same body every time
  (blue-white zig, Violet's same-blob law). The **Boom ↔ Flash engine is
  asymmetric by design: he talks to her, she has already left — zero
  replies, ever** (ep 5's Ember↔volcano ratio was 2 answers in many; this
  one is 0, and the zero is the physics).
- **Casting:** NEW MiniMax voice, auditioned by measurement (f0, IQR,
  chars/s, centroid against every neighbour in the episode), PROVISIONAL
  for Mike's ear. Register: big, warm, resonant, a little goofy — a
  friendly giant, NOT a boom-box. Must clear Rock (`Deep_Voice_Man`,
  reserved, not in this episode) and the volcano (`Elegant_Man` −6) in
  timbre and pace: Boom talks at 1.0 in warm run-on sentences, which is
  the opposite of both. Must not be a bright girl. Candidates for the
  audition agent to probe: `Imposing_Manner` (free), `Casual_Guy`,
  `English_ManWithDeepVoice`, `English_Jovialman`,
  `English_Deep-VoicedGentleman`, `English_FriendlyPerson`,
  `English_Comedian`, `English_CaptivatingStoryteller`, `English_MatureBoss`,
  `English_Trustworthy_Man` (several will be dead ids — probe). Cast
  emotion `happy` likely; seasoning budget 2, cited.
- **Audition trio (writers: mark these lines in the script):** his
  arrival chain source ("Present!" or whatever the chain becomes — the one
  recording that fires many times), his longest proud run-on, and the
  turn's quietest line (must land warm, not defeated).

  fixed three-part shape: cheerful naming of near-identical strangers →

  **the echoes/hills** Boom bounces off (why thunder rumbles — the

  identical rumbles. Goodbye firing belongs in act 3 as the storm

- **Catch-phrase ceilings** are hard (bible table). Greeting+brag pair:
  fire. "You're welcome! HA! HA!" ≤8, source = earliest firing. Puff ×1

- **`sameAs` discipline:** every repetition gag = one recording, earliest

## 6. Tone + engine rules (unchanged, binding)

No sarcasm a six-year-old cannot parse; **no scary peril — the storm is a
gala, the lightning is fireworks, nothing on screen is hit, nobody is
frightened (the kid is cosy at a window; a character may be STARTLED by
Boom, which is funny, never scared)**; no potty humour; corrections are
gifts — Blue being right is not a comeuppance for anyone, Boom being
"far" is not a flaw. Narrator + Sunny on kokoro (letter-stretch allowed;
no emotion/pauses). Characters on MiniMax (emotion as seasoning with a
stage-direction cite; NO all-caps words inside MiniMax line text — caps
live in bubbles; a whole-shout one-word line survives; pause markers only
for scripted intra-line timing; sound-word spellings per engine — the
thunder's sound is STAGED sound design, never a narrated "BOOOOM" on a
MiniMax line). Spell out initialisms. Repetition gags on MiniMax =
`sameAs`. Bubbles ≤6 words.
